## Section 6: Colour selects the leader — THE LEADER THEOREM and ties
Working note of 24 Oct (his v1 test, script COLOUR-LEADER-01, kill conditions written first):
- Setting: light bouncing between surfaces or passing through layers "acts on the three channels as a positive map x -> Mx". Colour converges to the leading (Perron) eigenvector of M, shrink per pass |λ2/λ1|.
- Ka: dies if chromatic distance to the Perron colour does not fall like |λ2/λ1|^n (fitted rate within 2% of ratio). Kb: dies if the per-pass change in ln h does not approach ln λ1 (to 1e-8 after 200 passes). Kc: dies if, at exact tie, different starting colours converge to one common colour. Opposite tried before any verdict: does every single pass bring two colours closer in Schrödinger's metric (as Birkhoff proved for Hilbert's projective metric)?
- Results: (a) median fitted shrink/|λ2/λ1| = 1.0000, 5th–95th percentile 0.9793..1.0154 over 111 random positive M — survives; (b) brightness rate vs ln λ1 worst difference 2.4e-15 — survives ("The eigenvalue is the brightness rate; the eigenvector is the colour"); (c) exact tie M=diag(0.9,0.9,0.5): three starts end at three colours [0.8687,0.1313,0], [0.2092,0.7908,0], [0.5695,0.4305,0], Schrödinger spread 1.450 and 0.690 — survives. Near-tie passes to settle (distance<1e-3): relative gap 0.5: 24; 0.1: 154; 0.01: 1,605; 0.001: 16,119. Opposite: in Schrödinger's metric one pass pushed two colours farther apart in 955 of 20,000 cases (worst stretch ×4.48); in Hilbert's projective metric 0 of 20,000. "So the selection itself is measured by Hilbert's metric, and Schrödinger's metric describes where it ends up."
- His readings (verbatim): "Colour selects the leader. The leader is the colour that survives; its brightness rate is the leading eigenvalue. Rate is weight; colour is direction." "At a tie, colour cannot choose. The starting colour, the memory, decides. This is the colour version of the tip/tail law: a leader is only selected when its gap is resolved." "The physical case is published. Interreflections shift colour toward the reflectance peak (Yu, Wijntjes, Eisemann and Pont, Journal of Vision 22(3), 2022)." "What may be new is the framing ... Novelty not yet checked against the literature."
- Ledger line: "leader median 1.0000 (0.979-1.015), rate 2.4e-15, tie 1.450/0.690, settle 24/154/1605/16119, Schrödinger stretches 955 of 20000 x4.48, Hilbert 0 (s6)"; "boundary shrink sqrt(r), Jordan factors, tie with transfer not frozen (s6)"; "Davis-Kahan 900 trials, 74 deg vs 0.27 deg CITED (s6)"; "Yu 2022 interreflections (s6)". AI-assisted") states: (1) source-free decay: c_i(t)=c_i(0)e^{-k_i t}, ratio identity exact; recovering elapsed time needs known nonzero rate gap and known initial ratio. (2) amount vs composition: m=1ᵀc, q=c/m; if all occupied components share rate k, q constant while m(t)=m(0)e^{-kt}. (3) independent losses and unique survivor: unique smallest occupied loss rate k* ⇒ q → basis vector; tied slowest components keep their initial relative weights; "even an uncoupled tie among only the slowest components need not freeze the entire trajectory". (4) repeated positive maps: Perron–Frobenius, ρ simple, v,w positive eigenvectors, wᵀv=1; hₙ₊₁/hₙ → ρ. (5) repeated leading eigenvalue semisimple: limit Px₀/(1ᵀPx₀) ("A multidimensional leading eigenspace can retain initial-state dependence. Strict positivity rules out that Perron degeneracy; a diagonal tie example is nonnegative, not strictly positive."); Jordan blocks need separate analysis. (6) general normalised equation ċ=Ac+s: transfers and selective production can change composition even when eigenvalues coincide; a source proportional to q changes amount not composition. (7) tied counterexample ċ₁=-kc₁+gc₂, ċ₂=-kc₂, 0<g≤k: both eigenvalues -k, Jordan block, c₂/c₁ ~ 1/(g t), q₁→1: "The tie has not frozen direction" (k=0.05/day, g=0.02/day, initial (1,0.15)). (10) convergence is not one-step contraction: strictly positive passive filter with column sums ≤ 0.99 expands one selected pair about 8.72×.
