Title: "A subgraph density obstruction to biplanarity, and the thickness of inflated cycles". Jeromie Beasley, September 2026.
## Definitions
Biplanar = edges covered by two planar subgraphs. Catlin graphs C_n[K_r] = r-inflation of cycle C_n (lexicographic product): |V|=rn, |E_fibre|=n·C(r,2), |E_join|=r²n. χ(C_n[K_r]) ≥ n for odd n (independence number r); C_7[K_4]: 28 vertices, 154 edges vs biplanar ceiling 6·28-12=156 (margin two edges), χ=10 exactly.
## Results
- Lemma 1 (Subgraph density): G biplanar on N vertices, H⊆G triangle-free ⇒ |E(H)| ≤ 4N-8. Proof: planar girth-4 bound 2N-4 on each layer.
- Theorem 1: for all n≥4, r≥4, thickness Θ(C_n[K_r]) ≥ 3. Proof: join-edge subgraph J triangle-free (C_n triangle-free for n≥4); r²n ≤ 4rn-8 fails; for r=4 sides are 16n vs 16n-8, fails by exactly 8 independent of n. Remark: r≥5 follows from plain edge count (cross at r=5); r=4 "the case no edge count reaches — 22n edges against a ceiling of 24n-12". Table 1 join edges vs capacity.
- Corollary 1: Θ(C_n[K_4])=3 for all n≥4; in particular C_7[K_4] is not biplanar (upper bound via arboricity of closed 2-blowup, Albertson-Boutin-Gethner; answers their Open Problem 3 for r=4).
- Verification (computer, scripts "available from the author"): C_7[K_4] 3276 vertex triples none a join-only triangle; counts 28/42/112/154, per-layer join capacity 52, two-layer 104; reproduces non-biplanarity of C_5[K_4] (80 join edges vs 72); four SAT biplanar decompositions for path sub-cases re-verified (join counts 41/39, 49/50 ≤ capacity); "A witness exceeding the capacity would refute Lemma 1; none does".
- Two consequences: screening test; On search: "Such searches find witnesses; they refute nothing unless run to completion... The obstruction is global, and no witness-finding search could have exposed it." 100 join edges vs capacity 104 when four of sixteen closing edges restored.
