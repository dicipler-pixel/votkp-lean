## Abstract (his words, key)
- "A transported spectral subspace supports two distinct readings: an intrinsic tangent pairing and a contour-defined spectral record. The pairing can become degenerate along a parameter family while the spectral record remains well defined, provided its contour and analytic branch remain admissible."
- Operator-first framework for light-matter response; separates orthogonal-projector geometry from biorthogonal extensions, spectral scalars from measurements that retain orientation of a subspace relative to a probe.
- Three connections in explicitly stated finite-dimensional models: (1) ground-state projector split across a spatial cut satisfies C_A - C_A^2 = BB^dag: transition-window census exactly a singular-value count of cross-boundary coupling -> rigorous bound on boundary-filtered directional channels, reproduces original lattice census. (2) Schur elimination then Smith/Cayley transform pulls boundary's non-returned response back to Hermitian part of interior operator, no normality needed. (3) Electric-field-driven projector geometry determines transition-resolved optical polarizability via excitation-energy weights. Below gap metric and symmetric response have identical real nullspaces; between resonances an exact numerator zero can cancel response while metric stays nonzero.
- Rigidity fixed throughout: R_eta = [sigma_min(A_intr)^2 + eta]^{-1} in declared inner products; resolvent representation, stability bound, refraction law of a specified scalar transport action proved. "The independently calculated optical coefficient is not identified with this rigidity without a constitutive map."
- Fixed symmetric-plus-skew family has even scalar contour ledger to all orders, while directed boundary response can change at first order. Permutation holonomies, graph-subspace angles, compression identities supply further tests.
- Edition 7.2: extends to gapped spectral measures, derives oscillator-strength bounds on metric and response; conserved-strength upward redistribution theorem (excitation energies/transition amplitudes can change while a common kernel for metric loss and optical-response loss retained); four-reading bound needs no upper spectral cutoff; exactly solved quantum harmonic oscillator realizes mechanism, connects field-state fidelity, polarizability, dilute-medium optical coefficient. These have written proofs and fresh exact/numerical checks; outside archived Lean certification.
- "The assignment of the two registers to light and matter is an organizing physical interpretation, not a consequence of the finite algebra alone. Experiments motivate the questions; the computations reported here are model calculations." Three research extensions reserved: physical identification of transport rigidity with optical coefficient; continuum optical directional-capacity bound; dynamical test of long-lived light-source architecture. ## 1 Introduction

These are the paper's two rails. [H] They are not two additional fields, and the assignment is not exclusive: light has quantum-state geometry, matter supports phase coherence, and measurements can depend on both."

## 9 Discriminating measurements and model tests
- "A physical test must measure the operator or response used in the theorem, with its units and thresholds fixed in advance." ## 11 Conclusion (his words)
- "The finite response results now give a direct way to ask whether silence is coupling lost or contributions cancelled. Two distinct squared photon energies in one pole-free interval recover the metric kernel through a positive response difference. A single tune-out can hide active transitions; two exact scalar zeros in that interval cannot, under the stated lossless assumptions."
- "The central distinction survives the stricter formulation: local geometry, spectral continuation, and measured response are different readings of related operator data. A pullback metric can lose rank without a spectral contour becoming inadmissible. An individual mode can become singular while the projector of its entire isolated cluster stays regular. A scalar ledger can be even or isospectrally constant while a directed, probe-dependent response changes."
- Five concrete components: orthogonal-projector geometry; exact cross-boundary census + filtered-channel bound; Schur-Cayley interior-to-boundary response identity; transition-resolved connection between electric-field geometry and optical polarizability; exact tune-out and nullspace results. "Equal total metrics can nevertheless have unequal static response." Rigidity: controlled regularizer, resolvent representation, finite stability bound, conditional scalar-action refraction law.
- "The relational information cannot all be compressed to eigenvalues, a determinant, a defect norm, or a list of directions. Probe couplings, holonomy order, boundary maps, and coherent composition retain different parts of it."
- "Light and matter provide the organizing physical interpretation. [H] The mathematical contribution is the finite operator framework and its explicit response relations, supported by reproducible model calculations and 91 verified light-specific Lean declarations." Spectral-measure bounds + conservative redistribution theorem; oscillator realizes intervention with full sum rule and static response saturates metric-moment bound; "not proof of an unrestricted identification of light, the spectral ledger, and transport rigidity.
