01

The wall was already inside the knot

Non-Normality Is Connection Energy §10  ↔  VOTKP Table 6  ↔  vacuum disk, open problem 6

VOTKP counted 494 double Alexander roots where the signature does not move and left them unexplained. Connection Energy had already named what most of them are: a wall is a nonzero nilpotent. At 376 of the 494 the knot's own turning operator has one Jordan block at the root, so a single level comes down to zero, touches, and goes back. The other 118 are two levels crossing in opposite directions: the mirror fusion of the branch-cut work.

  • 575 of 575 double roots: touch exactly when the factor is absent from the second Alexander polynomial
  • 198 of 198 checked directly on the Seifert matrix: one Jordan block of size 2 at every touch
  • all 81 jumps of ±4 are split roots; a touch can never jump
  • the 61 walls 'not covered by theory': 45 touches, 16 opposed crossings
Reproduces standard module theory once stated; the recovery is that two of the papers already held both pictures.
02

Dirac Time's staircase predicted the missing roots

Dirac Time Thm 13.1  ↔  vacuum disk, open problem 1

The disk paper found one direction each for the torus knots 819 and 10124 and asked for the rest. The arc ends of Dirac Time give the answer before any search: direction = −k²/(pq), with k the staircase step.

  • predicted first: 819 {11/12, 2/3}; 10124 {14/15, 11/15}
  • found: 2/3 (106 times) and 11/15 (45 times), with the paper's own solver on the braid drawing
  • no root outside the predicted sets in 22,000 starts
  • three components and four, but two distinct directions each
New fact: the KnotInfo drawing of 819 never reaches 2/3. For torus knots the root list depends on the drawing.
03

One integer runs through three papers

Two Trees Thm 8.7  ↔  vacuum disk  ↔  Dirac Time Thm 13.1

On a torus knot the same odd k is the rotation of the meridian product, tr(ab) = 2cos(kπ/q); the direction on the disk, −k²/(pq); and the arc end of the meridian phase, k/(2pq).

  • 31: k = 1  ·  51: k = 1, 3  ·  71: k = 1, 3, 5 — roots and directions both reproduced
  • trefoil arc 1/12 < α < 5/12 (VOTKP 13.7) = k = 1 and 5; both ends give 5/6
  • the direction is the end-point product of the arc angle and the lift slope
Checked on T(2,q) through the Riley relation and on T(3,4), T(3,5) through the roots.
04

A torus knot is a pure turn, and the corpus says so three times

VOTKP A.10  ↔  Two Trees Thm 8.7  ↔  vacuum disk

VOTKP: on a branch L = εM−k the volume form vanishes identically. Two Trees: the product of the two meridian boosts is a rotation of finite order. Vacuum disk: zero decay, a rational fraction of a turn. Two boosts that compose to a pure turn make no space.

  • the knots that assemble themselves in chemistry are of this kind (5 of 6)
05

Two Trees' commutator number is the UPG defect

Two Trees Thm 8.6 and census 8.8  ↔  UPG §4.4 (UPGTorusAnchor, Lean)

Two Trees measures the meridian commutator by k = 2 − tr[a,b] and leaves open why its limits take the values they do. For a turning commutator k = |λ − 1|², which is UPG's relator defect δ, with δ + g² = 4. The rotation band 0 < k < 4 is UPG's admissible domain.

  • every limit value with m = 1 is a clock-and-shift defect 4 sin²(πj/d): 1, 2, 3 at d = 6, 4, 3; φ−2, φ² at d = 10; the three sevenths at d = 14
  • in the census d = 10 comes with q = 5 and d = 14 with q = 7
The values divided by m² (1/2, 1/4, 1/9, φ²/4) are not clock values and stay open; d = 2q is a lead, not yet tested on a new knot.
06

The line Γ² = ½ turns up five times

Two Trees §5 and §8  ↔  path notes (elements)  ↔  VOTKP §8  ↔  vacuum disk

It separates Schottky from non-discrete boosts; it is where crossing meridian axes always turn; metals lie above it and non-metals below; the alkali first steps sit above it down to caesium and francium falls below; and on the vacuum disk it parts knot pieces from metals.

  • 99.7% of 2,777 knot tetrahedra lie inside it; metals 35 of 35 outside
  • 91 of 94 tabulated compounds inside; 17 absorbing ones (TiN, VN, TiC, MoO2, selenides) enter the bowl in two dimensions
The bowl itself has no feature there; the line is about where things sit.
07

The twin primes turn their frame one way

One Tree, One Ball  ↔  BallJoins J7  ↔  the base-10 prime pattern (with Elizabeth Johnson)

Walked through the tree of peel steps, the twin chain shows no order beyond one step. But the one step has a hand: the chain prefers the arms in the order i, j, k, and its frame carries a net signed turn that reverses when the chain is read backwards. The prime-pattern work had already recorded the same thing from the other side: equal consecutive gaps are suppressed.

  • signed turn −0.183 forward, +0.205 reversed (ratio 5); outside every no-memory chain, inside the one-step band
  • the unsigned angle could not see it; the signed record does
The one-step preference is of the known kind (repeats avoided); it is recorded, not claimed as new.
08

Order lives only in the phase

VOTKP §15.2  ↔  vacuum disk §5

VOTKP proves that peel steps commute and a stack at zero phase forgets its order; only the phase between additions keeps it. The gluing of a knot's pieces is that same sum closing to zero, and its logarithms close to 2πi. The integer is what the sum cannot see. That is why the twist sits in the winding integers and not in the shapes.

  • with the integers: Chern–Simons exact on 454 of 454; without them: 1 of 79
