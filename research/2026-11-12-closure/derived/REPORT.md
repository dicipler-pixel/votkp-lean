## 1. Matter-wave factor: HOLDS WITH THIS CORRECTION (the printed theorem is right; the "share one coordinate" sentence needs a named variable)
Derivation. x<0: psi=e^{ik1x}+r e^{-ik1x}; x>0: psi=t e^{ik2x}. Continuity of psi: 1+r=t. Continuity of psi': ik1(1-r)=ik2 t. Substitute: k1(1-r)=k2(1+r), so r=(k1-k2)/(k1+k2), t=2k1/(k1+k2) (sympy solve, exact). With tau=ln(k2/k1): r=-tanh(tau/2). Printed v0.3 Thm 15.1 is exactly this, and its own definition is tau=ln(k2/k1), a WAVENUMBER ratio.
Variable in which each case is (a-b)/(a+b):
- Matter wave: wavenumbers, k=sqrt(2m(E-V))/hbar. In the energy ratio zE=(E-V2)/(E-V1), k2/k1=sqrt(zE), so r=-tanh((1/4) ln zE). Exact (sympy). Probability current is proportional to k, so k plays the impedance role.
- Light at normal incidence: refractive indices, r=(n1-n2)/(n1+n2)=(Z2-Z1)/(Z2+Z1) with Z=Z0/n (exact). Impedance ratio, or index ratio.
- Ionization ladder: Gamma=(IE_{k+1}-IE_k)/(IE_{k+1}+IE_k)=tanh((1/2) ln z), z an ENERGY ratio. Radar reading: z=(1+b)/(1-b) is a frequency ratio and b=(z-1)/(z+1) (exact). The frequency/energy ratio equals the wavenumber ratio only for linear dispersion (photon, omega=ck).
So the ionization coordinate matches the matter-wave step only if the atom's energy ratio is replaced by a wavenumber ratio, i.e. z_atom=(k2/k1). With z=14.03 (Li): tanh((1/2)ln z)=0.866933; tanh((1/4)ln z)=0.578563 (the dossier's 0.5786 confirmed).
