# T11_many_balls: what the crystal looks like when many balls are joined

Work: Jeromie Beasley's balls, arms, peel step and loop. Exploration through his picture, not a final test. All scripts (s1..s6), raw outputs and the lab notebook sit beside this file. No web, no fitting. Other people's data used: T9 lattice table (ASE / Ashcroft-Mermin structures, NIST ionization energies) and the mendeleev ladder for IE3.

## 1. Results first

**Assembly (step 1).** Balls of rim radius 1, each carrying an arm set, ball 2 placed along an arm facing ball 1, grown breadth-first (up to 1,773 balls, merge tolerance 1e-9). Closure test (written before the rerun): minimum separation of distinct centres unchanged at the last two generations and growth exponent of the ball count at most 3.3 (a lattice gives 3). A first density criterion was flawed in this setup (it came out "no lattice" even for simple cubic) and was replaced before any result was read.

The three centre-distance rules (a) rims touch d = 2, (b) cut surfaces touch d = G_n + G_m, (c) tanh of the summed lengths (artanh G_n + artanh G_m + gap lengths) change only the spacing, never the lattice type. Rule (b) with the two forced gaps added back is identical to (a) (G_n + 1 - G_n = 1 per ball), so I used (b) as "cut surfaces touch". Dimensionless spacing under (a), exact: nearest-neighbour distance over cut diameter is 3/2, 13/12, 62/... for simple cubic z = 5 at depths 1, 2, 3 (1.5, 1.0833, 1.0161); body-centred z = 7: 1.3333, 1.0417, 1.0059; face-centred z = 11: 1.2, 1.0167, 1.0015. Cut-ball packing fractions: 0.155, 0.412, 0.499 (sc); 0.287, 0.602, 0.668 (bcc); 0.429, 0.705, 0.737 (fcc), against rim packings 0.524, 0.680, 0.740.

Why the 13 set does not close: the diagonal offset d(1,1,1)/sqrt3 is commensurate with the axis offset only if d_diag / d_axis = sqrt3 times a rational. Controls with that ratio set by hand: exactly sqrt3/2 gives bcc, sqrt3 gives simple cubic with diagonals, 1 and 0.9 give no lattice. His rational peel distances never give it. His own pair (z = 5 axes, z = 7 diagonals, depth 1) has ratio 1.125, the wrong side (a body-centred lattice needs the diagonal arm shorter, 0.866). Searching classwise steps, the closest approach to 0.866 is 0.866013 at (z_axis = 52, z_diag = 11), approximate only.

**Mixed sizes (step 2).** Two species, same-species arms plus cross-species arms. With "cross cut surfaces touch, larger species touch" the typed clusters close at exactly the radius ratios (small cut radius over large): rock salt (cross six axes, same twelve face diagonals) at sqrt2 - 1 = 0.41421, shells 6 other, 12 same, 8 other, 6 same; caesium chloride (cross eight diagonals, same six axes) at sqrt3 - 1 = 0.73205; zinc blende (cross four alternating tetrahedral arms, same twelve face diagonals) at sqrt6/2 - 1 = 0.22474. These are the classical thresholds, so this is a structural comparison and not a ruler. Under rule (a) (all rims radius 1) no ratio condition arises at all. In terms of his arm sets, rock salt is the 17 set (axes cross plus face diagonals), caesium chloride is the 13 set (5 set within a species, 7 set across: the interpenetrating-pair reading of T10), zinc blende is 4 cross plus 12 same.
With his cut radii G_n(z) (rational), 858 species pairs from z in {2,3,4,5,7,11,13}, depths 1 to 6: 682 land in the caesium-chloride window (ratio above 0.732), 142 in the rock-salt window, 34 in the zinc-blende window. No exact hit exists (thresholds irrational, ratios rational). Closest: 5/12 (z = 2 depth 1 against z = 3 depth 2) to 0.41421; 13/18 (z = 5 depth 1 against z = 5 depth 2) to 0.73205. One step cut at two depths: z = 3 (1,2) 5/8 and z = 5 (1,2) 13/18 sit in the rock-salt window, z = 7 (1,2) 25/32 in the caesium-chloride window. The fork on his absorber and radiator wording (absorber small G or absorber deep) only swaps which species sits where; the ratio and lattice are the same. Element pairs by first-step G1 (Na-Cl, K-Br, Cs-Cl, Zn-S ...) did not sort compounds into windows in this setup.

**Special turn-angle steps (step 3).** With q = R/T = G^2/(1 - G^2) = sinh^2(r/2) = (z-1)^2/(4z), the loop law cos(theta/2) = 1 - 2q^2 inverts exactly to q = sin(theta/4), sqrt z = sqrt q + sqrt(1+q), G^2 = q/(1+q). Checked against the actual loop matrix (trace/2 = cos(theta/2)): worst difference 7.6e-6 degrees, at the wall only.

Stacking (layer offset rotated by theta each layer, declared model, hand argument checked first): theta = 0, 120, 240 gives ABC (fcc); theta = 60, 180, 300 gives ABAB (hcp); theta = 72, 90, 144, 270, 288 puts the next layers off hollow sites (periodic with period 5 or 4 only because the offsets close a polygon; not close-packed). So fcc at z = 3.7320508 and 5.2745106, hcp at z = 2.6592271, 4.6115818 and 5.6878912. In the plain twist model (layer lattice itself rotated) the rotated layer coincides with the first only at 60, 120, 180, 240, 300 and not at 72, 90, 144. At theta = 360/m the plaquette closes after m passes (W^m = plus or minus I, residual under 2.1e-10 at m = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6).
Beside the elements: the first-step turn theta(z1) of the 60 elements inside the wall is 24 to 35 degrees for the bulk (median hcp 27.3, fcc 28.8, bcc 34.0, diamond 29.5), only Cu (58.3 degrees) within 2 degrees of a special angle. Registered line C1 (written first): hcp elements closer to the ABAB angles than fcc elements, mean difference at least 10 degrees and label-shuffle p < 0.05. Result: difference -4.9 degrees, p = 0.81, not met in this setup. Opposite (second step z2 = IE3/IE2): +8.3 degrees, p = 0.068, also not met. No claim.

**The wall and z* in the assembly (step 4).** The single join is exactly reversible at every z (inverse boost). What does not close is the square of four joins in his geometry: its holonomy is the loop W, trace/2 = 1 - 2q^2 everywhere. Inside the wall W is a rotation by theta about a point at radius rho from the ball centre (rho^2 = 1/2 at z = 5, rho tends to 1 at the wall, the centre of turn walks to the rim); at the wall it is parabolic; past the wall it is hyperbolic with translation length lambda (no finite order, repeated passes run off toward the rim). The defect between the two routes, d(B_x B_y o, B_y B_x o), grows smoothly through the wall, with no jump: 0.0017 r at z = 1.05, 1.0798 r at z = 3, exactly 2 r at theta = 180, 2.3766 r at the wall, 2.4722 r at z*, 2.9676 r at z = 11 (r = ln z). So the wall changes the kind of defect, not its size. At z* lambda = ln z* to 2e-16: the drift per loop pass equals one ball step; measured D(W^n o)/n = r + 3.0/n (1.8962, 1.8659, 1.8508 at n = 50, 100, 200; r = 1.8356). At z = 6 and 7 the rate goes to lambda, not r. The gap law g_n = 2/(z^n + 1) keeps its form at the wall and at z*; what changes is the loop amplitude (unit modulus inside, decaying e^{-n lambda} past), as T8 found.
Facing arms: the frame rotation omega between the two routes round a square rises from 0, touches exactly 180 degrees at z = 4.6115817893 (same z as loop turn 180), then falls (151.9 at the wall, 145.4 at z*, 93.96 at z = 60). All 6, 8 and 12 arms of the axis, diagonal and face-diagonal sets are carried into themselves only at omega = 90 (z = 2.890054, the golden-ratio z, which is also loop turn 72) and omega = 180; at omega = 60, 72, 120, 144 only the 2 arms on the rotation axis survive. A square of two perpendicular steps cannot carry a 3-fold or 6-fold turn.

**Pressure balance (step 5).** P = 2 G^2, K = 1/(1 - P). Pushes equal (G1 = G2): only the trivial same-ball case. P1 + P2 = 1 (G1^2 + G2^2 = 1/2) for z in {3,4,5,7,11,13}, depths 1 to 6, exact: one solution, z = 3 depth 1 against z = 3 depth 1 (G = 1/2, P = 1/2 each, K = 2). Over every integer step ratio Z up to 4000 (any Z = z^n) still only (3,3). Nearest misses: (3,1) + (4,1) = 61/100; (3,1) + (5,1) = 25/36. For z = 4 and 5 at depth 1 a balancing partner exists but is not an integer step (z2 = 2.1957 and 1.6168); z of 7 or more at depth 1 and every z at depth 2 or more have G^2 above 1/2 already, so none exists. In this setup pressure balance fixes the depths at one point only.

## 2. What the play suggested (leads)

1. The 13 set closes into body-centred cubic only if the diagonal arm is exactly sqrt3/2 of the axis arm; the peel gives 1.125 for (5,7). Look for a step pair or a different length rule (his Klein chord, Smith coordinate) that makes the ratio exactly sqrt3/2. Run in part (rational G ratios only); chord and other readings not run.
2. The first-step turn of nearly every element is 24 to 35 degrees, below all crystal angles. A different loop (steps at 60 and 70.53 degrees between arms, the real hexagonal and bcc plaquettes) would give new turn laws; solve the turn for non-perpendicular steps and ask where it hits 60 and 120. Not run.
3. Pressure balance of two lossless layers has exactly one integer point, z = 3, K = 2. Whether z = 3 plays a role in the crystal (it is a depth-1 pair, both balls with Gamma^2 = 1/4) is open. Pressure times area for unequal facing surfaces not run.
4. At omega = 90 the square's frame mismatch is a cubic symmetry exactly at the golden-ratio step z = 2.890054 (loop turn 72). Check whether this is an identity (cosh r = golden ratio) or numerology by deriving omega(z) in closed form. Not run.
5. The 180-degree step z = 4.6115817893 has G^2 = sqrt2 - 1, the same number as the rock-salt closing ratio, and is where omega also reaches 180 and every arm set is satisfied. Whether this is more than coincidence of form: not run.
6. Alkalis are past the wall (no finite turn) and bcc (T9); the plaquette there is a drift with no closing order. Describe bcc metals as the drift case and ask whether the half-set between 3 and 5.8 (finite order) maps onto fcc/hcp polytypes: element-level test beyond C1 not run.
7. Heat, temperature, time and conductivity (his controlling factors) and light's pressure with rotation inside and outside the layers were not used here beyond P = 2G^2. Not run.

## 3. Untested or limits
