Start with one step of light.
A step is a boost of length r, and its size in the picture is gamma, the hyperbolic tangent of r over two.
Halfway along the step stands a face. A share gamma squared turns back, and the rest passes on.
The two shares make exactly one. Nothing is lost at the face.
Two faces on arms at right angles are compared by one number, g.
When g is below one, the faces cross. When g is above one, they stand apart, and the light between them can only tunnel.
At the wall, where the step ratio is three plus two root two, the faces just touch.
Now put three balls on the field, and kick the centre ball.
Light does not jump. It crosses the gap at speed one, so the next ball stays dark until the light arrives.
At each face a share returns and the rest passes. The light in the balls plus the light in the gaps is always exactly one.
Every ball keeps its own clock. This is a hypothesis, and it is marked as one: a ball one step out ticks at one over z.
With these clocks the faces carry a load even at rest. That number is computed, and the clock ratio is the hypothesis.
Seven balls make a flock. Kick the centre, and light spreads through the gaps.
Do the balls swing together, like clocks on one beam? In every run so far, they do not lock.
