## The extra domain condition for a directed loop

**DERIVED.** Full supporting geodesics and directed face segments give different interpretations at small tilts. For forward carbon and nitrogen ratios take g and h as their positive reflections, with g>h. Let θ be the angle **between the outward radial axes at the disk centre**, 0<θ<180°. A face of reflection g has two ideal endpoints at polar angles ±arccos(g) measured from its axis. The second face’s endpoints occur at θ±arccos(h). The two complete geodesics cross in the interior precisely when their ideal endpoints alternate on the boundary. At endpoint coincidence they have a common ideal point. This gives the carbon and nitrogen ideal angles 1.51888748250° and 89.82270223492°. A direct Euclidean circle-distance calculation independently confirms both.

For a convex loop confined to the angular sector from 0 to θ, the crossing must additionally lie downstream of both feet, on their sector-facing rays. In Klein coordinates the tangential component on the first face is (h−g cos θ)/sin θ and the inward component on the second face is (g−h cos θ)/sin θ. Both must be positive for a nondegenerate interior sector loop. The carbon and nitrogen entry angle is consequently **arccos(h/g) = 13.11528523271°**. The crossing at 2° lies inside the unit disk but at polar angle −36.1281°, outside the sector. At the entry angle the intersection is a face foot and the putative quadrilateral degenerates. A nondegenerate finite sector loop thus exists here for **13.11528523271° < θ < 89.82270223492°**. The published complete-geodesic classification holds throughout its stated open angle domain if “closes” means intersection of the full supports. For a loop restricted to its sector the directional qualifier is essential. At 89.8° both interpretations give closure; at the upper ideal angle they share the relevant ideal endpoint; at 90° both give open. The lower ideal endpoint belongs to the extensions of the faces outside the sector. At 0° or 180° the two normal equations become parallel and require separate treatment.
