# Xenon under pressure, with an iodine comparison

**HYPOTHESIS — test condition.** In Jeromie Beasley's ball picture a changing intersite junction may alter bulk charge transport on compression. A model that uses only an isolated atom's static peel ratios will give one prediction for every pressure of the same element. A model that assigns the metal transition directly to a new close-packed coordination must predict that a resolved structural conversion coincides with the change in low-temperature resistance. Distinct thresholds would defeat that identification. Record the actual specimen, pressure, temperature, structure and transport separately.

## Xenon

| Observable | Evidence | What the instrument distinguishes |
|---|---|---|
| **MEASURED** stacking | Cynn et al. (2001) followed diffraction to 127 GPa and identified a broad face-centred cubic to hexagonal close-packed transformation across 3–70 GPa. A later experiment by Rosa et al. (2024) resolved residual cubic xenon at 55–60 GPa along its tested isotherms, including 568 and 728 K. | The two ideal close-packed arrangements both have twelve first neighbours. They differ in stacking. The phase fractions can depend on path and protocol. [Cynn et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.86.4552) · [Rosa et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gca.2024.06.001) |
| **MEASURED** optical | Goettel et al. (1989) inferred a threshold of 132±5 GPa from optical absorption and a carrier-response model. Reichlin et al. (1989) saw an optical change near 150 GPa and indexed hexagonal close-packed xenon to 172 GPa. | The optical reports use different criteria. A change in low-frequency response can coexist with visible transparency. [Goettel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.62.665) · [Reichlin et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.62.669) |
| **MEASURED** electrical sign and low-temperature qualification | In Eremets et al. (2000), 121–138 GPa bracketed the emergence of predominantly metallic resistance versus temperature. At 138 GPa the resistance rose again on cooling below 25 K; at 141 GPa the upturn appeared below 1 K. The 155 GPa run retained metallic behaviour to the lowest measured 0.027 K. | Compression and cooling were independent tests. A room-temperature resistance value alone would have missed the low-temperature reversals. [Eremets et al., Figs. 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.85.2797) |
| **MEASURED / authors' estimate** conductivity | At 155 GPa the authors estimated 500–2000 Ω⁻¹ cm⁻¹, or 5×10⁴–2×10⁵ S m⁻¹. The apparatus used paired platinum contacts in a quasi-four-electrode geometry. The experiment reported no superconducting transition within its pressure and temperature coverage. | Conductivity carries geometric and contact uncertainty. Their estimate and our extraction of sample resistance are separate quantities. [Eremets et al., conclusion and note 22](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=28731) |

**DERIVED from measured geometry.** At 429 K, Rosa et al. Table 2 gives the fcc per-atom volume 54.48(7) Å³ at 1.39(14) GPa and 45.387(5) Å³ at 3.0(3) GPa. Using a=(4V)¹ᐟ³ and d=a/√2, the nearest xenon–xenon spacing contracts from 4.2552(18) to 4.00389(15) Å. The propagated errors here use the tabulated volume refinement errors alone, δd=dδV/(3V). This is one comparison along the same isotherm, far below metallization pressures. The earlier electronic calculations in this project concern solid krypton and rubidium, so no Xe projector value has been transferred here. [Rosa et al. (2024), Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gca.2024.06.001).

**DERIVED from the published graph.** We extracted all sixteen square-marker centres from the vector PDF of Eremets et al. Figure 2 at 300 K. The plotted sample resistance falls from approximately 2.49×10⁸ Ω at 57.7 GPa to 5.67 Ω at 147.0 GPa, spanning 7.642 decades. As a descriptive check on the authors' slope change near 75 GPa, least-squares slopes of log₁₀(R/Ω) are −0.264 per GPa below 75 GPa and −0.053 per GPa above it (eight markers per side). The authors identified this feature with completion of the structural transformation in their pressure protocol. The apparent growth of metallic transport occurred substantially later. Marker centres were calibrated against six linear pressure ticks and nine logarithmic resistance ticks. The digitized coordinates describe plotted markers; instrument-level precision and original raw time traces are unavailable in this extraction. [Derived table](Xe_300K_figure2.csv) · [Published source, Fig. 2](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=28731).

**MEASURED qualification.** The reported transport pressure can shift during cooling. In the authors' optical cryostat a pressure rise of roughly 8 GPa was typical above 100 GPa. In the separate low-temperature refrigerator runs they measured pressure at room temperature. Their note 17 estimates a 1–2 GPa pressure variation near the contacts in one test. Additional low-temperature resistance could arise from contact or sample inhomogeneity; an excitonic state was discussed as a hypothesis. No unique cause was established. [Eremets et al., experiment and note 17](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=28731).

**RECOGNISED / mainstream-blend watch.** Pressure moves the xenon–xenon separation, and the first-neighbour count in the ideal cubic and hexagonal packings stays at twelve. The onset of mixed stacking, the change of slope of R(P), the high-pressure optical response and the low-temperature transport criterion carry different information. A future junction calculation can use measured crystal cells and pressure-dependent occupied projectors to predict both a site-link weight and an independent transport observable for the measured phases. An equal-clock junction would still give Γ=0 on both sides of the electronic change. There is no measured projector in these experiments.

## Iodine

**MEASURED.** Sakai, Takemura and Tsuji (1982) measured iodine resistance under compression up to 25 GPa, cooling to 1.7 K, and measured its thermopower at room temperature. They recorded a room-temperature resistance anomaly at 22.5 GPa and thermopower +4±3 µV/K at 24.0 GPa. The temperature 225 K quoted for their 23.5 GPa metallic specimen is a Grüneisen–Bloch fit parameter, **not** a transition temperature. [Sakai et al.](https://doi.org/10.1143/JPSJ.51.1811).

**MEASURED structural input / RECOGNISED assignments.** Ishikawa et al. (2013) used resistance and diffraction and placed metallization at 14–16 GPa in molecular iodine. Bykova et al. (2023) used single-crystal diffraction and Raman spectroscopy; their data resolve the molecular Cmce structure at 15 GPa, a molecular Cmc2₁ structure at 17 GPa, and a modulated Fmmm(00γ)s00 structure near 20 GPa containing short chains and molecular units. They interpreted the structural boundary near 20 GPa as consistent with metallization. Their 20 GPa assignment was not a simultaneous transport measurement. Their high-pressure protocol used neon and ruby gauges; the separate 1800 K anneal at 27 GPa must not be attached to the 16 or 20 GPa assignments. [Ishikawa et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/08957959.2012.755524) · [Bykova et al., methods and Figs. 1–2](https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevB.108.024104).

**HYPOTHESIS / discriminating test.** A single iodine pressure would conflate three experimental criteria. To decide whether the molecular Cmc2₁ phase can already conduct in bulk, collect low-temperature four-contact transport plus contemporaneous single-crystal diffraction on the same 14–22 GPa pressure path. Require the metallic response while the lattice still indexes solely as the commensurate molecular structure; the first appearance of transport only after resolved incommensurate satellites would contradict that earlier onset for the tested specimen.

## Reproduction

Install NumPy and PyMuPDF and run `python xenon_resistance.py`; the program fetches and SHA-256 checks the NIST-hosted paper. A local-copy replay is `python xenon_resistance.py --pdf /path/to/paper.pdf --output replay.csv`. It reads published graphic paths; no image thresholding or manual transcription of resistance values is involved.
