# Barium through neodymium in the element paper

**HYPOTHESIS (Jeromie Beasley).** Each nucleus sits at the centre of its ball. The electron cloud supplies its wall. Two neighbours have facing ports and a layer between them. The clock inside an isolated ball is read from an ordered pair of its removal energies. A junction between equivalent, equally clocked neighbours has reflection zero. The dimensionless apex of an internal face is tanh(ln z/4). Neither a crystal contact in ångströms nor a nuclear radius in femtometres supplies a conversion from that apex to length. The structural comparison below tests what happens to neighbouring sites when pressure, temperature, occupancy or phase changes. ## Crystal contacts, heat and conduction

**MEASURED / DERIVED.** [crystals.csv](crystals.csv) contains 30 indexed COD records with year, space group, pressure or temperature when available and a link to each refinement. For a fully populated commensurate basis, the first two distinct centre-to-centre distances come from symmetry expansion and enumeration through two unit-cell translations along each axis. ### Some barium gaps lengthen under compression

**HYPOTHESIS to test.** If compression shortened *every* axial channel separation in a given solid phase, the largest guest–guest distance along the Ba-IVb channel could never grow while the framework's repeat along the same axis contracted. This finite comparison tests that extra geometric claim. The model's internal face variable and its heating experiment measuring projector leakage are separate readings.

**MEASURED / DERIVED.** At 300 K the ambient bcc cell has a = 5.025 Å, giving d = √3a/2 = 4.35178 Å. At room temperature pressure takes Ba-I (bcc) to Ba-II (hcp), into the complex Ba-IV family, then back to hcp Ba-V, at roughly 5, 12 and 45 GPa. The IV family includes ordered and incommensurate variants and depends on preparation and pressure medium. The room-temperature status of a previously labelled Ba-III was disputed in diffraction; a separate narrow temperature interval has also carried that name. Cold compression can instead select orthorhombic Ba-VI. Ac susceptibility and resistivity showed a maximum superconducting transition near 8 K on that path. [300 K CIF](https://www.crystallography.net/cod/9008529.html) · [1994 diffraction](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.50.16238) · [2017 pressure path and superconductivity](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.96.184514).

**MEASURED / DERIVED.** At 293 K the 17.4 GPa Ba-IVb refinement gives c = 4.6341 Å and a largest adjacent guest-chain distance of 6.85(9) Å over its modulation; at 18.5 GPa these are 4.599 Å and 7.48(5) Å. The repeat decreases by 0.0351 Å as the largest chain gap grows by 0.63 Å. Even extending each printed distance by three standard uncertainties leaves 6.85 + 3(0.09) = 7.12 < 7.48 − 3(0.05) = 7.33 Å. This is a sensitivity check using reported uncertainties and central fitted structures; common model error remains possible. At the two pressures the shortest listed host–guest distances are 2.80(5) and 2.74(6) Å. A single material can maintain separate narrow and wide spacings. All six CIFs give the full guest–guest, host–guest and host–host envelopes, with physically distinct extrema that can occur at different modulation phases. [Original single-crystal experiment, 2017](https://journals.iucr.org/m/issues/2017/02/00/yu5011/index.html) · [175 individual pair records](barium_modulated_bonds.csv) · [18 family/pressure envelopes](barium_modulation_summary.csv) · [Reproduce the extraction](barium_modulation.py).

### The neighbour set in lanthanum

**MEASURED / DERIVED.** The complete dhcp model gives a = 3.770, c = 12.159 Å; neighbouring layers have d = √(a²/3+c²/16) = 3.73868 Å, while same-layer neighbours have d = a. The pressure sequence includes hP4, cF4, hR24, a re-entrant cF4 and then tI2. High-pressure diffraction published in 2024 revised the assignment above about 80 GPa. The investigators distinguished hydride contamination peaks from elemental La. The 224 GPa tI2 refinement has a = 2.6378(4), c = 3.8867(11) Å. Pressure changes the elemental superconducting transition; older measurements in dhcp and fcc reached 9 K. Heat at low pressure also changes the stacking, as the NIST phase entries above show. [Complete dhcp basis](https://www.crystallography.net/cod/9008525.html) · [2024 diffraction, Table I](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.110.024107) · [1966 superconductivity measurements](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.146.291).

**DERIVED / falsifier.** If space-group identity alone fixed the number of nearly equidistant ports for a distance-based cutoff, La and Pr in the same tI2 structure would give the same number for an identical fractional tolerance. In this tetragonal cell four basal neighbours have distance a and eight body diagonals have distance d = √(2a² + c²)/2. Counting neighbours within 1.05a gives **12 for La** (d/a = 1.02116 at 224 GPa) and **4 for Pr** (1.13134 at 205 GPa). The strict shortest shell has four in each. The count at 5% persists over the individual lattice-parameter uncertainty boxes. [All five tolerances and bounds](tetragonal_neighbours.csv) · [Exact enumeration](tetragonal_neighbours.py). ### Cerium preserves lattice symmetry through a collapse

**HYPOTHESIS / comparison.** A rule that associates each crystal topology with a single contact length fails if diffraction finds a substantial volume change with the same space group. The 1956 fcc reference has a = 5.1612 Å, with nearest-neighbour d = a/√2 = 3.64952 Å. At room temperature the γ→α transition near 0.7 GPa involves two fcc phases and reaches an approximately 17% volume loss in the low-pressure regime. In an ideal fcc cell, V₂/V₁ = 0.83 means d₂/d₁ = (0.83)^(1/3) ≈ 0.940, a roughly 6% change with the same 12 nearest-neighbour directions. A 2024 microscopy/diffraction study resolved the microstructure, and 2025 time-resolved measurements followed the transition on rapid compression. At several GPa orthorhombic oC4 and monoclinic mC4 compete depending on sample history. The α-cF4/oC4/tI2 triple point was measured at 6.1 GPa and 640 K. [1956 fcc refinement](https://www.crystallography.net/cod/9010987.html) · [2024 microstructure](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actamat.2024.119863) · [2025 compression-rate test](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0244242) · [Phase diagram, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-648X/ab7f02).

**MEASURED.** A 2026 pressure-contact experiment on elemental Ce reported a superconducting response above 20 K in the region loaded by a sharp metallic tip. The authors compare it with 1.8 K in bulk Ce under 54 GPa hydrostatic pressure. The geometry and stressed volume differ. These findings give separate targets for a junction model and a bulk phase model; a universal critical temperature for the neutral element fails this comparison. [Point-contact experiment, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/tqzv-6df2).

### The superconductivity bound for collapsed praseodymium

**HYPOTHESIS / comparison.** The assertion that crossing into an α-uranium-type structure by itself generates superconductivity above 50 mK can be checked on elemental Pr independently of Ce. Pr's ambient dhcp model gives a = 3.6725, c = 11.8354 Å and a near contact at 3.64013 Å. At room temperature the observed pressure route goes hP4→cF4→hR24→oI16→oC4→tI2. The oI16→oC4 step around 20 GPa collapses the volume. In oC4 at 165 GPa, a = 2.4199(4), b = 4.7817(5), c = 4.3799(7) Å and the 4c site is (0, 0.115(17), ¼). In tI2 at 205(3) GPa, a = 2.383(4), c = 4.209(11) Å. The independent transport experiment found no evidence for superconductivity down to approximately 50 mK at 41, 73 and 104 GPa or 1.1 K at 120 GPa. The null bound refers to those pressures and sensitivities. [Ambient basis](https://www.crystallography.net/cod/9008527.html) · [Pressure refinements, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.105.174104) · [Resistivity limit, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.84.033101).

### Neodymium's ion, magnetic texture and nearest contact

**HYPOTHESIS / comparison.** If the electron count alone imposed a xenon core, the intermediate ion in the sixth-to-seventh removal pair would show the xenon ground configuration. Its ASD configuration fails that test. There is a further structural contrast with Pr. Nd has an ambient dhcp basis with a = 3.6579, c = 11.7992 Å and shortest distance 3.62786 Å. The measured room-temperature sequence is hP4→cF4→hR24→oI16→hP3→oF8→oC4. The final two transitions start at 71(2) and 98(1) GPa; the transformation to oC4 is sluggish. At 302 GPa the published oC4 cell has a = 2.280(1), b = 4.515(1), c = 4.129(2) Å, 4c at (0, 0.114(6), ¼). The published Nd diffraction sequence extends to 302 GPa, beyond the pressure at which Pr already has tI2. [Ambient basis](https://www.crystallography.net/cod/9008526.html) · [2021 structural measurements](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.103.134117).

**MEASURED / open test.** Four-probe work on Nd found its ordering temperature rising from roughly 20 K to 180 K near 70 GPa, then decreasing. The same work reported no superconductivity down to 1.5 K at 51, 112 and 155 GPa. In 2026 spin-polarized microscopy resolved heterogeneous aging in the spin-glass state of elemental Nd. For a proposed magnetic chirality invariant one would have to select an actual oriented spin field at a specified preparation and time, define a closed path, and test reversal and a spatial mirror separately. The dynamic Q pockets alone do not furnish a knot polynomial. [2017 resistivity](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.95.205138) · [2026 magnetic imaging](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42323300/).

**Mainstream-blend watch.** Diffraction, thermal measurements, matrix spectroscopy and bulk transport supply independent phase-tagged observations. The calculations here use the crystal metric only to evaluate *reported physical distances*. They impose no ambient spacetime metric on Jeromie's intrinsic wall, supply no conversion of its apex to length and assign no atomic clock to a dissimilar optical transition without a separate definition.

**Set aside, with why.** No closed elemental knot assignment, filled-space projector for these five metals, calibrated conversion of apex to crystal distance, or experimentally complete pressure–temperature atlas follows here. Some phases lack a verified basis in the accessible structural tables, so their nearest distances remain open. NIST strong-line intensities are within-source relative values, not absolute absorption cross sections. No magnetic moment was inferred for an unassigned spin. Full error covariances on the nuclear radii and phase refinements were unavailable. The separate pure-phase and point-contact superconducting results keep their sample conditions.
