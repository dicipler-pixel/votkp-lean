# Following the same barium lines into a neon host

**HYPOTHESIS to test.** Give neutral barium one free-atom optical reference and compare each identified transition with the same transition in solid neon. A one-parameter model that multiplies *every* transition energy by one common positive scale s predicts that all ratios ν̃(host)/ν̃(free) are equal. Opposite signs of well-assigned shifts rule out that uniform rescaling at the reported centres. This is an auxiliary model. Jeromie's original site model explicitly carries several levels and a link-dependent energy current. The experimental line comparison supplies one way to discriminate an oversimplified clock from a state-dependent response. [His atomic sites, ports and energy-current definition](https://github.com/dicipler-pixel/votkp-lean/blob/a60a000/research/hydrogen-in-the-space-between/hsb_model_v1.js).

**MEASURED.** Lippi and colleagues embedded neutral barium in solid neon at 6.8 K and measured fluorescence following 355 nm pulsed excitation. The article appeared in April 2026. The input rows here transcribe the two whole-wavenumber columns, linewidth and shift from Table I of the authors' December 2025 preprint. C, E, F, G and H are their five reliably identified transitions. A and B are overlapping components fitted to a broad peak; D has several possible contributions. In a separate experiment the group measured a 0.39 ± 0.02 s lifetime for 5d6s ³D₁ in neon. An inhomogeneous linewidth is a distribution width; it cannot substitute for an uncertainty on the fitted centre. [Published experiment](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0316792) · [Numerical source, Table I](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11140v1#S4.T1) · [Transcribed values](barium_neon_lines.csv).

**DERIVED.** For this fixed-transition comparison, define z(opt) = ν̃(host)/ν̃(free) and Γ(opt) = [ν̃(host) − ν̃(free)]/[ν̃(host) + ν̃(free)]. These quantities concern emission from one element in two environments. A VOTKP electronic peel instead uses two adjacent removal thresholds in one atom. The archive's barium IE and EA have no role in the optical computation.

**DERIVED.** For C, Γ = −93/(17968 + 18061). For H it is +27/(11422 + 11395). The upper states sit above the same ground state, and their shifts have opposite signs. No single s matches their printed centres. Even allowing each printed wavenumber to vary by ±0.5 cm⁻¹ for display rounding, z(C) ≤ 0.994906010 and z(H) ≥ 1.002281602. The source quotes FWHM 156 and 102 cm⁻¹ respectively and flags H as electric-dipole forbidden in free gas. Experimental confidence for a microscopic model would need calibrated centre errors and trapping-site information. The rounding calculation does not supply either. [All eight directed comparisons](barium_neon_clock_check.csv) · [Exact rational check](barium_optical_clocks.py).

**DERIVED.** The measured G and C lines determine the spacing of their upper levels because they share one lower level. That difference changes from 18061 − 12635 = 5426 cm⁻¹ in the free atom to 17968 − 12599 = 5369 cm⁻¹ in neon, a change of −57 cm⁻¹. E and F supply the complementary check, sharing the 5d6p ³F₂ upper level. The separation of their lower ³D components changes from 13030 − 12848 = 182 cm⁻¹ to 13011 − 12838 = 173 cm⁻¹. Subtracting the printed integer centres for E, F and G gives −19, −10 and −36 cm⁻¹; the authors separately print −18, −9 and −35 cm⁻¹. Both columns are preserved. The one-unit differences are consistent with rounding. The experiment does not resolve a transport projector, or prove a microscopic reason for the shifts.

**MEASURED / DERIVED.** Visible vacuum-surface optical constants for metallic films are a third experiment. In Table II of the 1936 vacuum study, the historical extinction parameter is k and the modern imaginary refractive index κ is printed in the column nk. At 546.1 nm the three (n, κ) pairs are Ba (0.89, 1.51), La (1.79, 3.43) and Ce (1.74, 2.39). For a semi-infinite medium at normal incidence, R = [(n − 1)² + κ²]/[(n + 1)² + κ²]. The source's rounded percent reflectances are 40, 63 and 47. The intensity attenuation lengths λ/(4πκ) are approximately 28.8, 12.7 and 18.2 nm. The measured object here is a vacuum-prepared surface; these values cannot be read from vapour lines or from the fixed-element anion rung. [1936 vacuum film measurements, Table II](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSA.26.000122).

**Set aside, with why.** Knyazev and Noskov measured optical absorption of the elemental La, Pr and Nd metals. The absolute visible Pr and Nd optical constants from their 1973 experiment have not been transcribed here. Independent Nd-film ellipsometry covers 0.33–3.0 eV; the Pr film dataset tabulating 4–1600 eV begins in the ultraviolet. Neither a Pr-doped glass spectrum nor a Nd magnet alloy supplies the missing pure-metal visible pair. [1973 study listed in the Weaver bibliography](https://jhweaver.matse.illinois.edu/optical-prop/Bibliography-2.pdf) · [Nd film measurement](https://etheses.durham.ac.uk/id/eprint/8799/1/8799_5730.PDF) · [Pr film data and experimental reference](https://refractiveindex.info/?book=Pr&page=Fernandez-Perea&shelf=main).
