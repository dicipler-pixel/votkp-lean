# Where the 54-electron label and the xenon configuration come apart

**DERIVED — test fixed by electron count.** For nuclear charge Z choose k=Z−54. The two removals run from 55 electrons through 54 to 53. This identifies a unique row regardless of the spectroscopic orbital assignment. The input is the pinned October archive, including its experimental, semi-empirical and theoretical flags. The script tests independent rectangular endpoints with rational arithmetic and emits all the values directly. The quoted boxes describe input ranges, without a probability interpretation. [Generated channel table](xe_channel.csv) · [reproduction script](check_channel.py)

**DERIVED — two thresholds with different outcomes.** From cesium to neodymium, *each entire ratio interval* lies below the preceding interval. Cesium is outside the loop wall and all five successors are inside. Against the reference 36/25 = 1.44 for a pure hydrogenic 6s-to-5p change, cerium is definitively above, praseodymium straddles it, and neodymium is definitively below. Taking neodymium's independent rectangular extremes gives 94/88 ≤ z ≤ 104/80 = 1.30. Both its energies are theoretical archive entries. The bare number is a comparison value. Interpreting it as a universal lower bound for the 54-electron channel would fail this test.

**RECOGNISED — the same electron count carries different orbital assignments.** In ASD, the 55-electron parent has a 6s outer electron for cesium and barium, a 5d for lanthanum, and a 4f for cerium and praseodymium. Consequently the phrase “last 6s to first 5p” describes only the first two of those five. For neodymium the interpretation diverges further. The table below omits the common closed subshells through 5s². The three configurations are the 55-, 54- and 53-electron states respectively. **RECOGNISED — what the sources establish.** Table A.2 of Carvajal Gallego et al. puts the ASD assignments alongside three calculations. For Pr VII, Nd VI and Nd VII all three calculations in that table agree with each other and differ from ASD. The authors discuss the close 5s, 5p and 4f orbitals, interactions among configurations and dependence on the chosen orbitals. ASD leaves the Nd VII ground term empty in the ionization table. The newer calculated assignments are credible alternatives; counting the calculations cannot establish a spectroscopic identification. [2024 paper, Table A.2 and §3](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202347723) · [accessible paper](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/333300/1/Paper313_aa47723-23.pdf) · [Kilbane & O’Sullivan 2010](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.82.062504)

**HYPOTHESIS / falsifier.** A ground-state-resolved measurement that fixes a nonzero J for Nd VII would exclude a pure closed 5p⁶ ¹S₀ ground state. A measured J=0 alone would leave room for configurations with open subshells coupled to J=0. Ground-linked level assignments with lifetimes, transition strengths or field response would constrain that alternative. A changed assignment changes the orbitals in the ledger. Revised thresholds would be needed to change its numerical wall call. These are separate tests of what a peel keeps.
