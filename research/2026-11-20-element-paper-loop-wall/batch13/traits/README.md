# What an ionization step keeps at subshell resolution

**DERIVED.** This is a ledger of the occupations assigned to each atomic ground state. It compares all 605 successive removals from barium through terbium, including ten final H-like → bare transitions. It uses the two independently fetched complete ASD configuration snapshots and the original repository parser to expand each noble-core abbreviation. [Occupation ledger, 605 rows](occupancy_ledger.csv) · [per-element summary](summary.csv) · [barium–neodymium snapshot](../../batch12/ladder/asd_states_20261121.csv) · [promethium–terbium snapshot](../ladder/asd_states_20261121.csv)

For a transition, let bₗ and aₗ be its initial and final occupations of subshell l. Keep

- K = Σ min(bₗ,aₗ), the number of occupied slots that can be matched within the same subshell;
- R = Σ max(aₗ−bₗ,0), the minimum number of surviving slots a matching must reassign to other subshells.

At most min(bₗ,aₗ) slots can match within each subshell, and matching that many in every subshell attains the bound. There are R unmatched target slots, R+1 unmatched source slots and one net electron removed. Thus N_after=K+R and ‖b−a‖₁=2R+1. The table lists every net loss and gain, the complete retained occupation vector and every *occupied* subshell with unchanged occupation. A subshell whose occupation decreases can still contribute to K. These counts describe changes of leading-configuration labels. The counts alone contain no observation of an individual electron moving during removal.

**DERIVED / formally checked.** The identities hold for arbitrary finite lists of aligned natural-valued occupations under the one-particle-loss hypothesis. Core Lean 4.34.1 builds five theorems; the first three use only propext and Quot.sound; the last two also use Classical.choice. The Python control separately found the unrestricted minimum by brute force for small occupancy vectors. [Lean proof](SubshellTraits.lean) · [axiom output](lean_run.txt) · [independent controls](controls.txt)

**RECOGNISED in ASD.** Of 605 transitions, 595 have R=0, nine have R=1, and one has R=2. Barium, neodymium, promethium and europium have R=0 throughout under these assignments. Lanthanum’s first removal changes 5d6s² to 5d² outside [Xe], giving two fewer 6s and one more 5d occupation. Cerium’s first two removals each give R=1. The unusually large single step belongs to gadolinium: in the 14th removal the 4f count falls from five to two and 5s rises from zero to two. K=48, R=2 and the L¹ change is five. The three gadolinium removals 13–15 give R=1,2,1 in succession.

**Falsifier for an assignment-independent reading.** If an alternative complete calculation changes R for the same charge change, the data do not justify a unique subshell-rearrangement call. Each alternative must provide both states; mixing models at the boundary of a table can create a spurious reallocation. An E, S or T flag on an ionization *energy* cannot resolve competing configurations.

**DERIVED / 2024 comparison.** Table A.2 of Carvajal Gallego et al. compares three calculations with NIST, all spanning La–Tb V–VII. Its NIST counts coincide with the fresh ASD snapshots in all 27 states. Across 18 transitions with both states in the table, each alternative changes the R call for the same three sixth removals (Pr, Sm, Tb): ASD has R=1 and all three alternatives give R=0. The identity of the net occupation lost or gained changes in 12, 9 and 12 transitions for Kilbane (2010), Banerjee (2023) and Carvajal Gallego (2024), respectively. Thus an unchanged scalar R can conceal a different subshell loss. For example, in neodymium’s sixth removal the 2024 calculation loses one 4f where ASD loses one 5p, both with R=0. The supplementary CSV also labels 18 mixed-endpoint transitions per model as *one tabulated state plus ASD flank*. They are sensitivity checks. [Table A.2](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202347723) · [numerical comparison](alternative_configurations.csv)

**DERIVED / wider independent check.** The original Kilbane–O’Sullivan Table II spans spectra V–XVII. Reading its 117 assignments and expanding implicit cores reproduces every one of its 27 reprinted states in the 2024 comparison. Of 108 transitions with both states available, 21 change R and 37 change the net subshell losses/gains relative to ASD. Its gadolinium sequence for charges 12,13,14,15 has outer occupations 5s²4f⁴ → 5s²4f³ → 5s4f³ → 4f³. Every step has R=0. This removes the entire apparent 1,2,1 episode. Across all 108 transitions the 2010 model has 14 total reallocations versus eight under ASD, so removing this episode does not imply less rearrangement across the sequence. The 2010 authors warn explicitly that ranking configuration-averaged energies may fail to identify the lowest fine-structure level. Both readings are kept. [Primary paper, Table II](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.82.062504) · [accessible source PDF](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/server/api/core/bitstreams/d2a7192f-8cd8-45c8-ba91-169785f55aec/content) · [108-transition control](extended_comparison.csv)

**Open test.** Resolve the ground multiplets of Gd XIII–XVI experimentally and use the inferred 5s/4f occupations to test which assignment sequence they support. Strong configuration mixing may require a spectroscopic or many-body occupation distribution in place of any single integer label. Regardless of that input choice, the current ionization energy ledger uses the same pinned energies. R, the signed energy reflection Γ and a crystal pseudoscalar answer different questions.
