# Dysprosium through ytterbium

**HYPOTHESIS (Jeromie Beasley).** The nuclear centre and the electronic wall belong to one ball, each atom has internal face clocks, and an adjoining wall has its own clock comparison. The intrinsic face apex is tanh(ln z/4). Here the same individual element has different contact geometries, magnetic textures or decay channels depending on whether the experiment follows electronic removal, a crystallographic direction or a physical change of state. These provide independent tests of which traits survive each operation. **MEASURED.** The five fresh metals are silvery. Most of the selected neutral lines fall in the violet/blue, while Yb also has a prominent green line. The NIST strong-line ledger contains 163 Dy, 173 Ho, 152 Er, 136 Tm and 144 Yb entries, **768 total**, preserving neutral/ion labels, air/vacuum convention and intensity flags. Relative source intensities are not absolute bulk absorption coefficients. Tm offers a particularly clean clock distinction. The 2024 study found ground- and clock-level hyperfine splittings of 1,496,550,658.23(3) and 2,113,946,873.08(9) Hz. Here the optical clock links two fine-structure components of the *same* 4f¹³6s² configuration. Its energy is separate from an ionization interval. [NIST Handbook, 2007, per-row links](spectral_lines.csv) · [2024 thulium experiment](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.110.032817) · [RSC element descriptions](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/).

**MEASURED.** A 2025 Yb⁺ study tracks the common lower ²S₁/₂ state to ²D₅/₂ and ²F₇/₂ on the same five even isotopes. For A=168,170,172,174, define the comparison νᴬ−νᴬ⁺². All four isotope shifts of the first transition are positive, all four shifts of the second are negative. For 168→170 the pair is +2,179,098,868.0(5.3) and −4,438,159,671.1(15.7) Hz. Thus even the *sign* of an optical isotope shift is transition-specific. A single common isotopic multiplier applied to all transition frequencies fails these opposite signs. Absolute frequencies and mass-corrected King-plot nonlinearities are different observables. [Measured shifts, Table I, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.134.063002).

### Phase-specific tests

- **MEASURED / Dy.** A temperature-independent turning angle in a magnetic helix fails neutron diffraction. In hcp Dy the magnetic turn rises from about 27° per layer near 87 K to about 43° near 176 K at ambient pressure. A 10.8 GPa Sm-type sample instead showed a ferromagnetic transition at 59 K. The 2025 structural sequence reaches hP2→hR9→hP4→hR24→oF16→oF8, with oF8 appearing above 195.5 GPa in a sample studied to 337 GPa without a pressure medium. At atmospheric pressure NIST evaluates hcp→bcc at 1653 ± 4 K before melting. [Dy diffraction and magnetic order](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1573903) · [2025 high-pressure diffraction](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.111.064112) · [NIST](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.TN.2312).
- **MEASURED / Ho.** A rule assigning a fixed transition pressure from the close-packed ambient lattice fails against Dy and Ho. June 2026 synchrotron diffraction found Ho hcp→Sm-type→dhcp→distorted fcc→oF16→oF8. Its final transition begins near 257 GPa and both phases persist to 327 GPa, without a resolved volume discontinuity. Thus onset lies about 61.5 GPa above Dy's in these experiments. Ambient magnetic reference anomalies occur around 20 and 131.7 K in the NIST evaluation. [Ho, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/lzlp-zwnn) · [Dy, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.111.064112) · [NIST](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.TN.2312).
- **MEASURED / Er.** A universal initial suppression of ferromagnetic order by compression fails for Er. The 2024 magnetization comparison found Er's initial ferromagnetic ordering temperature rose, unlike the five other lanthanides in the comparison. High-pressure diffraction gives hcp→Sm-type→dhcp→distorted fcc and the revised oF16 structure. A 2025 pure-Er transport experiment found a magnetic anomaly at 171 K at 166 GPa and no superconducting indication in the measured 3.5–295 K runs. The Y(Er) dilute-alloy pair-breaking experiment in the same paper is a separate material. [2024 comparison](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.109.064414) · [2019 structure reassignment](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.100.024107) · [2025 transport](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.111.125117).
- **MEASURED / Tm.** The assumption that the ferromagnetic and helimagnetic signatures disappear together fails magnetometry. The ferromagnetic anomaly falls below detectability before the hcp→Sm-type structural change, while the helimagnetic signal persists in the Sm-type phase. Compression continues through dhcp and hR24. The 2011 report put the post-hR24 change at 124 ± 4 GPa, assigned a monoclinic structure and reached 195 GPa. The 2019 reanalysis assigns oF16 to the collapsed heavy lanthanides including Tm. A SQUID used to measure magnetic order does not imply sample superconductivity. [2024 magnetometry](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.109.064414) · [2011 diffraction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21460429/) · [2019 reanalysis](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.100.024107).
- **MEASURED / Yb.** The maximum-temperature test for a universal neutral-atom superconducting classification fails within a single element. Yb at about 86 GPa has a superconducting onset near 1.4 K, growing to 4.6 K at 179 GPa. X-ray absorption in the same study found mixed valence to at least 125 GPa. July 2026 diffraction tests its structure to 293 GPa. At ambient pressure NIST lists a hysteretic hcp/fcc transformation near 260/270 K and fcc→bcc near 1033 ± 20 K. The hP3 high-pressure phase is distinct from that low-temperature hcp allotrope. [2018 transport, susceptibility and x-ray absorption](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.121.037004) · [2026 phase study](https://doi.org/10.1103/dv9c-y18p) · [Cross-phase contact and mirror tests](ytterbium_structure.md) · [NIST](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.TN.2312).

## Isotopes and the ball's centre

**MEASURED / DERIVED.** The FRIB discovery catalogue and the IAEA ground-state compilation serve different purposes. The former yields **205 discovered nuclides** comprising Dy 39, Ho 40, Er 40, Tm 43 and Yb 43. All 190 IAEA records appear among them. The additional 15 are Dy-138, Ho-179, Er-181,182, Tm-182 to 186, and Yb-147,148,186 to 189. Identification of an isotope does not establish its spin or radius. The CSVs give Z, A and N=A−Z, and the Standard Model ball counts u=2Z+N, d=Z+2N at its centre for every discovered nuclide. The IAEA companion supplies spins, nuclear rms radii and uncertainties where evaluated. Its raw dipole values can concern a different state; for a confirmed J=0 ground state the static ground dipole is exactly zero. **RECOGNISED.** The first three representatives are N=98 isotones. Their printed central radii fall and then rise. Dy and Ho overlap on their printed uncertainties, unlike the lower-N example in batch 12 where each adjacent radius difference remained positive across the quoted boxes. The isotope and uncertainty qualifications matter. A direct comparison against an actual decay is possible within this batch. Neutral ¹⁶³Ho electron-captures to ¹⁶³Dy; bare ¹⁶³Dy β⁻-decays into ¹⁶³Ho⁶⁶⁺. The archive-plus-neutral-mass calculation predicts a single reversal of the same-charge ground-mass ordering at q=56→57. [Derivation, controls and isotope-specific limits](holmium_dysprosium_decay.md).

**Mainstream-blend watch.** Magnetic helix handedness, the orientation-odd Yb crystal statistic, an optical clock, the ordered ionization face Γ and the signed mass difference of two isobars each have an explicitly different reversal operation. We compare them only after choosing that operation. The coordinate metric in Å is used to evaluate physical diffraction structures and never imposed on the intrinsic face geometry.

**Set aside, with why.** A signed torus-knot label for the elemental Yb path still needs an embedded closure. The pressure/temperature space is not exhaustively sampled for all five and several phases still lack a verified refined lattice/basis in these files. No established positive transition of elemental Dy, Ho or Tm to a superconducting phase was located here; this is not an experimental lower-temperature exclusion. The source records do not give bulk optical absorption functions or each isotope's experimentally resolved dipole and radius. Transport cannot be classified from nominal electron occupancy alone.
