# Lutetium to rhenium · what changes between a free atom, its neighbours and its nucleus?

**HYPOTHESIS.** Beasley's two readings ask for an actual closed path on the knot side and an actual sequence of retained data on the peel side. For the crystal one must also specify its sites, occupied electronic space and neighbourhood. These five metals give direct tests of how much is lost by assigning the whole material one number. Fully ionized rhenium supplies a measured case in which electronic occupancy changes the nuclear decay channel. ## Atomic traits and the two VOTKP readings

**MEASURED / evaluated.** Each neutral has the [Xe]4f¹⁴ core and 6s². Its number of neutral 5d electrons rises from one through five. The associated NIST ground terms, two illustrative strong *visible* neutral lines (in air), and the current recommended static scalar polarizability are below. The full [740-line NIST strong-line file](spectral_lines.csv) includes often stronger UV features and ions, with intensities and source references. These line positions are neither a bulk object's colour nor absolute absorption coefficients. Ta, W and Re also have measured bulk/film complex indices across much of the ultraviolet and visible spectrum. A native Ta oxide can affect its visible ellipsometry. **DERIVED / v1 and failure criterion.** For each of the five, a universal atom-specific knot model: **none found**. We can construct a closed path on an experimentally refined crystal. One four-contact loop is given explicitly in each of hcp Lu, hcp Hf, α-bcc Ta, α-bcc W and hcp Re, using each structure's actual first distance. Removing any edge when the distance is not in the claimed shell would invalidate that path. The checked edge lengths are respectively 3.434195, 3.130756, 2.860101, 2.740702 and 2.740357 Å. Each path returns to its original point even before periodic identification. The bcc path is a planar parallelogram. In the hcp path, the two basal A sites are joined through one B above and a vertically corresponding B below; the diagonals span a quadrilateral. The two triangles with a common basal diagonal have disjoint interiors above and below the basal plane, giving an embedded spanning disk. All five selected paths are **unknots**, with the single-loop normalized Alexander, Jones and HOMFLY-PT polynomials equal to 1. This gives a reproducible path-based control, not a polynomial for the entire crystal. ## What heat, pressure and preparation change

**MEASURED · Lu.** Ambient hcp a=3.5031 and c=5.5509 Å in a 99.9% Lu refinement. NIST lists a hexagonal solid through melting. Room-temperature diffraction under compression tracks the Sm-type and dhcp sequence (Sm-type→dhcp at 45±5 GPa), then a distorted fcc phase at 88 GPa with a reported 4.9% volume change; structural work reached 270 GPa. Elemental high-pressure susceptibility work gives Tc=12.4 K at 174 GPa. At low pressure historical sources disagree: a 1978 NBS table lists 0.10±0.03 K. Debessai et al. explain in note 48 that the experimental null above 22 mK was at **0.8 GPa**; their cited ambient ∼1 mK is an extrapolation. No unqualified room-pressure Tc is assigned here. The widely discussed high-temperature Lu hydrides are separate chemical compositions. [Lu diffraction, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.57.10221) · [study to 270 GPa](https://doi.org/10.4131/jshpreview.7.227) · [2008 elemental Tc and footnote 48](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.78.064519) · [1978 NBS table](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/TN/nbstechnicalnote983.pdf)

**MEASURED · Hf.** The representative ambient hcp axes are a=3.1967, c=5.0578 Å. At ordinary pressure NIST lists hcp→bcc 2016 [20] K, followed by melting from bcc. Another bcc Hf refinement reports a=3.61 Å (first distance 3.12635 Å); exact specimen conditions are not in that CIF. Under compression ultrapure Hf has α-hcp→ω-hexagonal→β-bcc. Without pressure medium, α→ω starts at 49.1 GPa and ends at 61.4 GPa; in He the corresponding interval is 60.3–63.7 GPa. β starts at 61.0 and completes at 64.0 GPa without medium; He gives 63.8–65.9 GPa. On release in He, ω→α spans 40.0 down to 8.5 GPa. Thus near 30 GPa, at the same element, medium and nominal room temperature, the loading history determines whether ω is present. Ordinary pressure Hf has Tc≈0.128 K; a pressure study to 64 GPa reported a 5.8→8.0 K step at the ω→β transition. [Hf compression/release, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-648X/ac33dd) · [Hf superconductivity under pressure, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1134/1.1857274) · [ambient Tc](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/TN/nbstechnicalnote983.pdf)

**DERIVED and HYPOTHESIS · the Hf gap.** For ideal-site hcp d_basal=a, d_interlayer/a=√(1/3+(c/a)²/4). Equality occurs at (c/a)²=8/3, a crystallographic equality distinct from the peel loop wall. Four 50 GPa α-Hf estimates in the ultrapure study are 1.631, 1.616 (no medium), 1.602 and 1.606 (He). All four printed values give shorter interlayer contacts, even allowing a last-place rounding enclosure. The closest one differs in *distance* from the basal shell by only 0.08135%; the four values do not carry individual measurement widths in the cited prose. A 2025 DFT/Migdal–Eliashberg study of **ω-Hf** predicts interstitial electrons in kagome and hexagonal patterns on two planes; spin–orbit coupling changes its calculated 60 GPa Tc from ∼3.0 to ∼5.3 K. This gives the sea hypothesis a concrete orbital/plane-specific target. The calculations measure neither an actual site-to-site occupied-projector norm in Jeromie's partition nor a Bruggeman fraction. The preceding hcp geometry and ω electronic calculation concern different phases. [Hf ratios and calculation](hafnium_shells.csv) · [2025 electronic structure](https://doi.org/10.1103/gbrd-kp9f)

**MEASURED and DERIVED · Ta.** α-Ta has bcc a=3.30256 Å. The 2026 dynamical compression XRD study observed bcc to a maximum stress of 531.7 GPa (−18.7/+21.3 GPa); it did not observe the proposed Pnma under that loading path. Ambient α-Ta superconducts near 4.47 K. β-Ta films have reported much higher normal resistivity (e.g. 200±20 µΩ·cm) and a reported 0.47–0.57 K transition; preparation matters. The tetragonal β phase is a metal made of Ta at both channel and framework sites. A 2002 single-crystal refinement has a=10.211(3), c=5.3064(10) Å, with principal guest contacts 2.622(10) and 2.687(10) Å. Their normalized length contrast is 0.01224 [0.00848,0.01601], an independent *geometric* reading. On a 293→120→293 K cycle the authors found the host symmetry returned and the guest symmetry did not. Low-temperature and rewarmed structures have a guest repeat c_host/2. A path that follows an infinite guest chain by a pair of links moves along the channel and does not close as an isolated knot. A putative fixed-temperature spacing prediction must specify the thermal history and sublattice. [Ta stress, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/klbh-bmbl) · [2002 β-Ta refinement](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0108768101017918) · [2003 cooling and rewarming](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0108768103009005) · [film transport](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-6090%2872%2990433-6) · [2026 comparison of thin-film phases](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43246-026-01352-1)

**MEASURED and DERIVED · W.** At ordinary pressure W is bcc in the heating diffraction series. All 35 W CIFs from the same 1997 paper were compared, with d=a√3/2. From 298 to 3548 K a rises 3.1648→3.2358 Å, an endpoint increase of 2.2434% in contact length and 6.8824% in volume. Strict central-value monotonicity fails: of 595 possible different-temperature pairs, 589 rise, four fall and two tie. The largest decrease was 0.0013 Å in a from the 1527 to 1537 K refinements. Independent per-row uncertainties and covariance were unavailable, so a material interval of negative thermal expansion is not established. A separate A15 β-W CIF gives a=5.083 Å. α-W superconducts near 0.011 K. In 2024 bulk transport, shielding, microscopy and spectroscopy on nominal β-W films implicated an amorphous underlayer in the >3 K signal, with no crystalline β-W transition found down to 2.3 K in that series. Low-nitrogen-stabilized films reported ∼1.1 K in another study, both below the first study's base temperature and under different material conditions. [1997 thermal diffraction](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002690050070) · [35 derived contacts](tungsten_thermal.csv) · [2024 W phase separation](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.109.104519) · [2024 nitrogen-stabilized films](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matlet.2024.136007)

**MEASURED and DERIVED · Re.** Ambient Re is hcp (a=2.7608, c=4.4582 Å in the reference CIF). A high-pressure diffraction refinement reports hcp axes 2.3884(10) and 3.7810(35) Å at the authors' pressure of 640(17) GPa. At room pressure, bulk Re has a superconducting transition near 1.7 K. Electroplated films on noble-metal layers give a different type-II response. In 2020, a type-II elemental sample yielded evidence of time-reversal symmetry breaking; a 2022 zero-field muon study on arc-melted type-I Re found preservation. Changing material preparation and superconductivity type changes what these tests imply. Quantum time reversal in a superconducting state and reversing the order of a peel are separate operations. **Source-label correction.** NIST TN 2312 Table 1 prints Z=75 as “Rh” and “fcc→liq”, with 3463 [15] K. Its own cited reference 72 is Sims et al. (1955), *Physical and Mechanical Properties of Rhenium*. Rhodium is Z=45. Hcp Re and the RSC melting value of 3458 K are used here with both source readings visible. No change to the pinned ionization archive is involved. [2012 compressed Re refinement](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms2160) · [electroplated film](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5027104) · [2020 type-II study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41535-020-00279-1) · [2022 type-I control](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.105.L020503) · [NIST original](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.TN.2312) · [1955 cited primary](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03377474)

**MEASURED · Ta.** The rare naturally occurring A=180 tantalum isomer illustrates why spin needs a state as well as Z and A. The ground state is 1⁺ with half-life 8.152 h. The long-lived natural isomer has spin 9⁻. A 2022 direct mass measurement revised its excitation from 75.3(1.4) to 76.79(55) keV; a 2023 underground search found no decay and set channel-dependent lower limits reaching 1.5×10¹⁹ yr. The natural-abundance complement in the table belongs to the long-lived isomer. ## Gap, clock and sea

**HYPOTHESIS / explicit mappings.** Lu supplies a close-packed, compressible wall with pressure-dependent stacking. Hf supplies a wall with two competing neighbour shells and a history-dependent phase. Ta supplies inequivalent host and guest walls in its metastable form. W supplies a repeat heating measurement on a single bcc lattice. Re supplies an hcp crystal and an independent isotope-resolved charge-state test. The distances above measure nucleus-to-nucleus contacts, so a physical thickness for the gap needs a separate definition and an independently measured electronic or charge-density partition. For a matched pair of equivalent sites the external clock ratio is 1, giving Γ=0. This assumption need not hold between inequivalent Ta sites. Each atom still has the distinct *internal* clocks tabulated in the ladder. [Beasley's two-atom model](https://github.com/dicipler-pixel/votkp-lean/tree/a60a000/research/two-atoms-in-a-ball) · [internal clocks](../ladder/rungs_detailed.csv) · [contact checks](metal_contacts_audit.txt)

**HYPOTHESIS / cutoffs.** For each phase and filling the link test requires a declared occupied projector P, Xᵢⱼ=‖Pᵢⱼ‖², a declared neighbour count and Bᵢ=Tr(Cᵢ−Cᵢ²). The measured fact that these samples conduct cannot determine whether Xᵢⱼ exceeds Bᵢ/(3n). No phase-resolved five-metal projector was run here. Neither an ionic radius nor an atomic peel Γ supplies that missing observable. The same limitation applies to assigning a gap-volume fraction and comparing it with Bruggeman's one third. The tungsten thermal expansion, hafnium hysteresis and separate isotope-specific Re decay data are independently checkable observables. A dynamical claim about light leading energy transfer needs frequency- and polarization-resolved transport and temporal ordering; a static wavelength or crystal contact does not measure such an ordering. [Frozen material and isotope tests](TESTS.md)

**RECOGNISED / mainstream-blend watch.** The simple cycles reconstructed on all five lattices bound an explicit disk and are unknotted. Alternative closures or more elaborate crystallographic paths need their own embedding. For v1, an element-specific polynomial for any of these five: **none found**. In particular an infinite guest channel, a finite contractible loop and a periodic knot in a three-torus pose different questions. For v2, ionic removal thresholds and radii are atom/ion data while metallic transport is a many-site response. An operational cross-reading would predict a new phase- or state-resolved observable and specify its sign or interval in advance. [Embedded path coordinates](explicit_closed_paths.csv) · [test output](metal_contacts_audit.txt)

**Set aside, with why.** No exhaustive, uncertainty-bounded lattice constants exist in this extraction for every pressure, temperature, film morphology and metastable phase. β-Ta partial occupancy and Hf axis/refinement sensitivity limit how literally a static contact network represents every occupied site. Several raw magnetic moments lack state identification, individual charge radii remain unavailable, and bulk reflectance and atomic excitation spectra require distinct specimens and observables. No κ measurement in the supplied data controls independently for sample purity and temperature across the complete sequence. No projector crossing or nuclear decay rate is inferred from a ratio alone.
