# Fixed tests for the material and isotope controls

**HYPOTHESIS / rhenium.** Compare the dimensionless span IE₇₅/IE₁ of the pinned Re threshold ladder with the measured neutral-to-bare acceleration of ¹⁸⁷Re beta decay. Failure of their stated input bounds to overlap rejects equality of these two particular spans. Then test a physically different quantity: for a beta electron born in the daughter 1s orbital, compute ground-state nuclear energy release from the *sum* of the two complete electronic binding budgets and the neutral-isotope beta Q. Do not double count the daughter's last IE after cancellation. Count every quoted width by an interval when available. A nonpositive upper bound closes a channel, and a positive lower bound opens it within these inputs. The second isotope, ¹⁸⁵Re, has the same element-wide archive but a different measured nuclear mass. Evaluate it by the identical expression. Separate kinematics from half-life; transitions to excited nuclear levels need the excitation energy subtracted, plus nuclear selection rules for decay rates.
 
**HYPOTHESIS / hafnium.** Compare all four reported hcp c/a ratios at 50 GPa against the exact ideal-shell equality (c/a)²=8/3. For each report which shell is shorter on its printed central value and its last-place rounding enclosure. If the experiment publishes no per-row uncertainty, do not claim physical exclusion of equality. Evaluate the pressure-medium and loading-history control using reported phase intervals in the same ultrapure elemental Hf study.

**HYPOTHESIS / tungsten.** Take every CIF from the same 1997 ambient-pressure W heating series, COD 9006486 through 9006520 inclusive. Sort by reported temperature and retain replicate temperatures. An axis or nearest distance falling on any strictly increasing-temperature pair refutes strict measured central-value monotonicity in the series. Separate an observed endpoint expansion from an all-pairs monotonicity claim. No assumption that distinct refinements supply independent error bars.
