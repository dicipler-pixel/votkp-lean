# Rubidium and krypton at measured crystal geometries

**Question and failure conditions.** Can a material-specific calculation provide the third observable in §5 of Jeromie Beasley’s element paper? The electronic test for ambient bcc Rb fails if its PBE bands give a gap at its electron count or if the converged intraband response vanishes. The contrasting test for fcc solid Kr fails if a band crosses the chemical potential at its closed-shell filling. Crystal temperature labels the source of the lattice parameter. The electronic calculation uses a static periodic crystal without phonons.

## Electronic structures

**Measured inputs.** The bcc Rb cell uses a conventional lattice parameter of 5.700 Å (297 K, COD 9012876, estimated lattice parameter in this refinement). The fcc Kr cell uses 5.721 Å (58 K, COD 9008471). Each primitive cell contains one atom. The Kr comparison concerns a cold crystal; krypton at room temperature and ambient pressure is gaseous. [Rb refinement](https://www.crystallography.net/cod/9012876.html) · [Kr refinement](https://www.crystallography.net/cod/9008471.html).

**Derived.** Ran Quantum ESPRESSO PWSCF 6.7MaX with scalar-relativistic PSLibrary 1.0.0 PBE PAW potentials, one unpolarised channel with spin degeneracy two. Rb uses nine explicit electrons (4s²4p⁶5s¹), Kr eighteen (3d¹⁰4s²4p⁶). The recorded SHA-256 values in [qe_compare.py](qe_compare.py) are checked against each downloaded UPF before running. Rb has no arbitrary onsite staggering in this calculation. The plane-wave basis and atomic potentials, lattice and electron number determine the bands self-consistently. The runs used a 10⁻⁹ Ry electronic threshold, uniform mesh with equal division counts and zero offsets along the primitive reciprocal vectors, and 6–7 electronic iterations. The plane-wave and charge-density cutoffs, smearing and irreducible k counts are in the two CSVs. [Giannozzi et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-648X/aa8f79) · [PSLibrary (Dal Corso 2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.commatsci.2014.07.043).

**Measured comparison.** An exciton-series analysis of low-temperature solid Kr reports a band gap of about 11.61 eV, corroborated by a two-photon measurement. The PBE Kohn–Sham gap here is about 4.38 eV lower. The occupied-band structure gives a stable insulating classification even while the gap magnitude shows this discrepancy. In the independent-particle limit an entirely filled isolated band gives zero intraband Drude response at zero temperature. Disorder, excitons and interband absorption require separate calculations. [Baldini (1962)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.128.1562) · [Reininger et al. (1982), discussion on p. 6295](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.26.6294).

## Experimental optical comparison

**Measured input and derived descriptive fit.** Smith (1970) inferred the room-temperature complex dielectric function of rubidium by multiple reflections at metal–silica interfaces. The n and k values used here come from a transcription of that experiment. Our reported normal-incidence powers and half-power bracket refer to a calculated vacuum–rubidium interface. The experimental apparatus used silica. [rb_optics.py](rb_optics.py) reads a pinned public-domain transcription, checks its file hash, converts n+ik to ε, and fits ε(E)=ε_b−Wp²/[E(E+iΓ)] to real and imaginary parts together. Five upper endpoints between 0.85 and 2 eV give Wp=3.570–3.591 eV and Γ=0.0292–0.0300 eV; the input starts at 0.554 eV. The numeric range measures this fit-window dependence. No pointwise errors were available for a statistical uncertainty. Finite-frequency optical response includes background and interband terms; the fitted damping is sample- and model-dependent. [Smith (1970)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.2.2840) · [archived transcription](https://github.com/polyanskiy/refractiveindex.info-database/blob/e6d15e364f70de1cad8a96a20f75689f2fba8b21/database/data/main/Rb/nk/Smith.yml) · [fits](rb_optics.csv).

## A bulk response with units

**Derived.** Use the highest-cutoff SCF charge density for Rb, then diagonalize that fixed Hamiltonian on denser k grids. Spin degeneracy supplies four completely filled semicore bands and one half-filled band. The sixth band stays clear of the Fermi surface. In the formula below the cell average is over the full primitive Brillouin zone, the band energy ε is in eV, and the Cartesian wave vector is in Å⁻¹:

    D(EF) = 2 ⟨δ(μ−ε)⟩
    Kαβ = 2 ⟨δ(μ−ε) ∂αε ∂βε⟩ = 2 ⟨Θ(μ−ε) ∂α∂βε⟩
    Wp² = [e²/(ε₀ Vcell)] Tr(K)/3.

With the stated units e²/ε₀=180.9513 eV Å and Vcell=5.700³/2 Å³. K has units eV Å², D has units states/(eV·cell), and Wp=ℏωp has units eV. A partially filled smooth band permits both forms for K by integration by parts on the reciprocal torus. The calculation keeps the Fermi charge at one electron per Rb after removing the filled semicore bands. This is an independent-particle response in the clean, collisionless limit. Converting to DC conductivity requires a relaxation rate.

**Derived, two ways.** [rb_tetrahedra.py](rb_tetrahedra.py) slices each linear tetrahedron directly at half filling. Four choices of primitive-cube diagonal probe triangulation error. Its Fermi-plane intersection volume and derivative handle degenerate vertex energies. This is ordinary linear tetrahedron integration. [rb_fermi_surface.py](rb_fermi_surface.py) instead interpolates with Fourier and cubic splines, solves for half filling and integrates a smooth Fermi–Dirac derivative, with broadening 0.025–0.12 eV on 48³, 72³ and 96³ grids. It checks the velocity-squared and occupied-curvature forms of K separately. The electronic SCF occupation smearing and these later quadrature broadenings have different roles. All 48 cubic symmetry images are reconstructed from the irreducible k list, and each orbit multiplicity is checked against its QE integration weight. [Linear tetrahedron integration and its refinements](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.49.16223).

The zero-width tetrahedron results for Wp narrow from 3.710–3.719 eV at 24³ to 3.7131–3.7156 eV at 40³ and 3.71377–3.71588 eV at 48³, varying the primitive-cube diagonal. D(EF) at 48³ spans 0.91279–0.91387 states/(eV·cell). On the same QE data the 96³ broadened quadrature gives Wp = 3.71352–3.71428 eV at 0.025 eV width and 3.70643–3.70709 eV at 0.05 eV width. The two forms of K in the smooth calculation differ by at most 0.032% across these four entries. The estimated Rb curvature is finite, and the estimate of Wp exceeds the descriptive optical fit by a few percent. Using a free electron with the same cell count gives 3.859 eV. The intrinsic atomic peel angles did not enter these band energies or velocities. [Full mesh and width sensitivity](Rb_fermi_surface.csv) · [zero-width triangulations](Rb_tetrahedra.csv).

## The role of the Fermi-surface shape in the shell test

**Derived, restricted orbital control.** Assign one effective scalar orbital to each primitive Rb site. Give its Bloch states the actual PBE fifth-band dispersion. Occupy the lower half of all k states on each offset grid. Then C=f=½ per spin, the site budget is B=2f(1−f)=½ with spins summed, and X(R)=2|⟨f(k) exp(i k·R)⟩|². Summing over every nonzero direct-lattice displacement on the finite torus must recover B. The code checks that identity and finds the eight shortest R directly from the measured lattice.

On the fine meshes Rb has nearest-shell fraction about 0.455 in this scalar projection, versus 0.533 in the nearest-hopping bcc toy. Both exceed 1/3. This control transfers the Fermi-surface shape from the material calculation, while suppressing the k-dependence of the cell-periodic atomic-orbital content. An energy-ranked fifth band can also touch higher bands away from the Fermi surface. Its scalar translation basis is a defined model choice. An all-orbital spatially resolved PAW projector requires the Bloch wavefunctions, a stated site partition and an overlap convention. A nine-band filled scalar identity for Kr would have B=0 and an undefined shell fraction. [Restricted shell weights and sum-rule residuals](Rb_scalar_shell.csv).

## A measured light comparison at one Rb surface

**Derived from measured optics.** For a planar vacuum/Rb interface at normal incidence, the nonmagnetic Fresnel amplitude is r=(1−m)/(1+m) with complex optical index m=n+ik. The measured input is Smith’s optical index inferred from metal–silica reflections. The Fresnel calculation from these observations supplies a complex-valued realization of the reflection algebra. For m≠−1 its exact half-power wall is

    |r|² > 1/2 ⇔ |m|²−6 Re(m)+1 > 0 ⇔ |m−3|² > 8.

The positive real axis cuts that circle at the two familiar ratios 3±2√2. Reciprocating m sends r to −r. Each equality holds for every admissible m as algebra. An additional physical identification between m and a free-atom peel ratio would require an independently specified frequency-dependent mapping. The atomic peel uses real removal energies, while r is a surface reflection amplitude.

**Measured input / derived comparison.** Between Smith's samples at 3.2040 and 3.3970 eV, the calculated normal-incidence power falls from 0.587595 to 0.373590 across one half. The two sampled wavelengths are 386.967 and 364.981 nm. This bracket is independent of fitting a damping rate or assuming a shape between tabulated points. Every one of the 24 tabulated rows passed both the circle test and the reciprocal-amplitude check. [Pointwise Rb optical table](Rb_reflectance.csv) · [Smith optical constants (1970)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.2.2840).

**Measured control at nearly the same wavelength.** At 546.1 nm, crystalline Kr at 67 K gives n=1.3674 in Sinnock and Smith's minimum-deviation data. In the transparent-interface approximation (optical k≈0), R=[(1.3674−1)/(1.3674+1)]²=0.0241. Keeping this n fixed would require k≈2.31 to reach R=½. The refractometry table reports the real part of the index; the quoted 0.0241 is calculated. Smith's Rb observation at 545.945 nm gives R=0.850 from both measured components. That comparison concerns different bulk phases and temperatures and the same photon wavelength to within 0.16 nm. [Sinnock and Smith (1969)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.181.1297) · [numerical Kr table A.2 in Grace (2018)](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/29369028/2018GraceEPHD.pdf).

**False control.** At 2.237982 µm, the Rb input is m=0.172367+6.306323i. Keeping both optical components gives R=0.983243. Discarding the extinction coefficient gives R=0.498366 and reverses the half-power call. For these encoded decimal inputs the real-index shortcut changes the call. The real-index value lies 0.0007941 above the lower real-axis crossover n=3−2√2; an uncertainty of this size in n could affect the real-only classification. The table supplies no pointwise uncertainty. The complex-index power is far from the half-power threshold.

**DC extrapolation check.** The 0.554–1.25 eV optical fit gives Wp=3.58698 eV, Γ=0.029577 eV. Extrapolating that single-component fit gives σ(0)=ε₀ Wp²/(ℏΓ)=5.85 MS/m when the energies are converted consistently. The 295 K value from 12.9 µΩ cm is 7.75 MS/m. The optical extrapolation undershoots it by about 25%. We therefore retain the independent measured DC value for Rb alongside the fitted optical response. [Measured resistivity, Ekin table A6.1](https://www.researchmeasurements.com/figures/ExpTechLTMeas_Apdx_English.pdf).

**Exact rational check.** `OpticalWall.lean` proves the denominator-cleared circle identity, its half-power inequality for a positive denominator, the sign reversal of both Cartesian field components under exchange of incident and transmitted index, and equality of the corresponding powers. These theorems use rational Cartesian coordinates, which contain all the finite decimals in the input tables. Three numerical theorems check the rubidium half-power bracket, its real-only false control and the transparent krypton comparison. The extinction convention is m=n+ik and the electric-field amplitude incident from vacuum is (1−m)/(1+m). Changing convention conjugates the field and keeps power fixed. The mathematical swapping experiment interchanges the two homogeneous indices. The physical measurement at each wavelength was made on the reported specimen. The seven Lean theorems build on 4.34.1 with only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Rebuild with `lean OpticalWall.lean`.

**Optical-mass calibration.** Smith reports an infrared optical mass of 1.16 times the bare electron mass for rubidium. With one electron per primitive bcc cell at a=5.700 Å, the bare-electron plasma energy is ℏ√[e²/(mₑ ε₀ a³/2)]=3.85887 eV, using a³ in m³. Inserting the published optical mass gives 3.58287 eV, inside our five fit windows’ 3.570–3.591 eV range. The computed 40³ band response of about 3.714 eV corresponds to 1.080 mₑ. The optical mass and our fit share Smith’s measurements, so this calibrates the fitting method. The comparison between these measurements and the independently computed band mass tests the electronic calculation. [Smith (1970)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.2.2840).

### Shape of the rubidium Fermi surface against quantum oscillations

**Measured input.** Shoenberg and Stiles obtained Fermi-surface areas by de Haas–van Alphen oscillations. The three measured area distortions reproduced by Lee (1969), Table III, are shown below. Their observable is the area perpendicular to the stated magnetic-field direction. A free-electron reference with one electron per atom fixes k₀=(3π²/V_cell)^(1/3) and A₀=πk₀². These tests use orbit areas directly and avoid inverting measured areas to infer radial distortion. [Original experiment (1964)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1964.0169) · [Lee (1969), Table III](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.178.953) · [Royal Society supporting tables](https://makingscience.royalsociety.org/items/ap_83_1/supporting-data-regarding-the-de-haasvan-alphen-effect-in-alkali-metals-by-david-shoenberg-and-p-j-stiles).

**Derived.** For the 5 K structure, solve ε₅(k_F ŵ)=μ in each direction of a spherical Gauss–Legendre quadrature. Determine μ by ⟨k_F³⟩=k₀³. In a central plane A=π⟨k_F²⟩_azimuth. Inversion makes this section stationary; both slices at ±0.025 k₀ shift have smaller area in every orientation tested. The computed oscillation frequency is F=ℏ A/(2πe). At k-grid 32³, doubling the angular quadrature from 16×32 to 32×64 changes the largest distortion by under 0.00011 percentage points. Moving from the 24³ to the 32³ electronic mesh changes the three distortions by at most 0.014 percentage points. Moving from 32³ to 40³ changes them by at most 0.0024 percentage points. All measured distortions below carry the quoted ±0.022 percentage-point uncertainty.

### How much of the scalar shell result is already fixed by geometry?

**Derived, spherical control.** Assume one orthonormal scalar orbital per primitive bcc site, spin degeneracy two, equal occupation of both spins and one electron per site. For an occupied sphere of radius k₀, its normalized Fourier amplitude at a nearest bond of length R=√3a/2 is F=3(sin x−x cos x)/x³, with x=k₀R=√3(6π²)^(1/3)/2. This follows by integrating exp(i k·R) over the occupied sphere and dividing by its volume. The nearest-shell fraction is 8F²=0.45325439. The lattice constant cancels. The PBE scalar-orbital result is about 0.45544. Thus a spherical pocket already supplies most of the observed numerical value in that restricted basis.

**Derived, non-uniqueness check.** Write the squared reduced pocket radius as s²=(k_F/k₀)²=1+c₀+Σ c_l H_l(u), where u lies on the unit sphere and H_l(u)=Σᵢ P_l(uᵢ), l=4,6,8,10. These are cubically symmetric harmonics since each P_l(uᵢ) is a zonal spherical harmonic and l is even. The central area satisfies A(h)/A₀=1+c₀+Σ c_l P_l(0)H_l(h). This also follows by direct angular integration around the circle normal to h; the code checks all nine targets by a second numerical circle integration. Impose the three measured central areas and full volume ⟨s³⟩=1. Choosing c₁₀ at −0.002, 0 and +0.002 gives different perfectly compatible model shapes and nearest-shell fractions 0.454780, 0.454462 and 0.454143. These values are conditional reconstructions of the three rounded central inputs. They demonstrate non-uniqueness; their spread is not an uncertainty for rubidium or a bound over admissible shapes. Extending measured data to other angles would constrain the remaining modes. [Reconstruction coefficients](Rb_shape_controls.csv).

**Derived, sufficient bound with an explicit failure condition.** Consider any cubic-symmetric, fixed-volume scalar Fermi occupation enclosed between radii (1−η)k₀ and (1+η)k₀ in every direction, with every state inside the inner sphere occupied and every state outside the outer sphere empty. These conditions permit a shape-independent bound. Cubic symmetry lets the eight equivalent nearest-bond Fourier kernels be averaged pointwise, yielding K=cos(k_x a/2) cos(k_y a/2) cos(k_z a/2). Write q=k₀a/2 and L=cos[(1+η)q], U=cos³[(1−η)q/√3]. For η≤0.023, at most one cosine is negative because (1+η)q<π/√2. If there is one negative factor the product is at least L and at most zero. If the factors are positive, concavity of log(cos√t) in t implies their product is at most U by Jensen’s inequality. The concavity follows from t=θ² and θ−sinθ cosθ≥0 on 0≤θ<π/2. Here L<0<U. Thus L≤K≤U throughout the exchanged shell. An equal volume m is removed and added, in units of the reference-sphere volume, and m≤min[(1+η)³−1, 1−(1−η)³]. The change in normalized amplitude is bounded by m(U−L), giving shell fraction ≥8 max(0,F−m(U−L))². At η=1% this lower bound is 0.401327; at η=2.2% it is 0.338289. Both exceed one third; this sufficient bound itself descends through one third at η≈2.29451%. Broader shapes can still pass. Three observed sections cannot establish the required all-direction inner/outer envelope for a real sample. Multiple orbitals and cell-periodic texture change the kernel. This bound concerns the scalar construction only.

**Exact arithmetic scope.** `ScalarShellBound.lean` proves the added/removed moment estimate, the inner-volume enclosure at η=2.2%, and the final one-third inequality from conservative rational bounds F≥0.238, m≤0.065 and U−L≤0.505. At that η, the numerically evaluated width is approximately 0.50173. The angular concavity and trigonometric estimates are derived above, outside the Lean file. All three Lean theorems build on 4.34.1 with only the three standard axioms. Reproduce the analytic controls with `python rb_shape_controls.py Rb_shape_controls.csv`.
