**DERIVED · what the data resolve.** The C-minus-N central difference is 0.070. Quadrature of the two quoted errors gives 0.122 for scale **if** those errors are independent; neither the confidence model nor cross-facility covariance is established by that calculation. The quoted C and N intervals overlap from 0.383 to 0.483. The adjacent N-to-O decrease is 0.085, larger than the C-to-N decrease of 0.070. Facility and wall class coincide throughout this four-ion comparison. A fitted step would therefore be confounded with an inter-facility normalization shift. The measured line occurs on both sides. These data cannot locate a transition at 3 + 2√2. [Comparative measurements, Table 5](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1526291)

**MEASURED · a second check from a single experiment.** Crandall et al. (1979) measured electron-impact single-ionization cross sections for Li-like C³⁺, N⁴⁺ and O⁵⁺ from threshold to 1500 eV. The authors observed the increase associated with excitation of an inner-shell electron in each ion. This rules out a binary interpretation in which that channel disappears on the inside. Excitation–autoionization in the starting Li-like ion and removing a 1s electron from the He-like product correspond to different processes. [Crandall, Phaneuf, Hasselquist and Gregory 1979](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3700/12/7/008)

**HYPOTHESIS · scope of a future physical test.** The existing loop-wall identities provide a classification. They specify no size, direction or functional law for a change in oscillator strength or impact cross section. The proposal to test either observable needs that extra mapping from the framework, fixed before new data are examined. A single-facility isoelectronic scan with common normalization and published systematic-error correlations would test a quantitative prediction against a smooth trend. To discriminate the exact cutoff one would also need observations or a justified continuous control that separate competing thresholds, or a quantitative dependence that differs between the models. A model of how two physical systems couple and a joint observable would be needed to test the mixed-face law physically. The single-ion experiments constrain candidate mappings, and currently supply no measurement of a mixed C/N face.
