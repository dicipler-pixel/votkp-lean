# What spectroscopy brackets for the second and third ionizations of technetium and rhodium

**Test specified before comparison.** For the uncertainty-based forward-step test, assume the true first four ionization energies are ordered. Use the quoted outer envelope for IE₁ and IE₄. A separate interval enclosing either IE₂ or IE₃ entirely between IE₄,upper/T and T·IE₁,lower is sufficient to bound all three consecutive ratios by T. With T=5.80801027 the target intervals are approximately [7.35,41.35] eV for technetium and [7.53,43.32] eV for rhodium. This T is the requested tighter comparison, separate from the loop wall 3+2√2. Spectroscopic standard uncertainties used as envelopes remain conditional.

**MEASURED / source trace.** NIST ASD v5.12 gives semiempirical [15.26], [29.55] eV for technetium and [18.08], [31.06] eV for rhodium. All four uncertainty fields are blank. Moore’s 1958 NBS circular explains both *second* thresholds. Catalán and Rico interpolated the technetium limit from neighbours where technetium had no known series. For rhodium they interpolated the ns series trend across the singly ionized sequence from strontium to cadmium. Moore also identifies the rhodium *third* threshold as an interpolation of third spectra from yttrium through indium, even though 854 rhodium lines had been classified. Moore’s 1970 compilation tabulates each without a numerical error envelope and warns that the precision of two-place entries was poorly established. These sources share a data lineage. A later technetium radiative-lifetime study explicitly credits this same interpolation. [1958 circular, technetium II and rhodium II entries](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-84e5c3cafc125d0f2db8cf94cb3d6f91/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-84e5c3cafc125d0f2db8cf94cb3d6f91.pdf), [1970 NBS compilation, introduction and tables 1–2](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-39bf2d7a22b8bcd02f86c211903aae71/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-39bf2d7a22b8bcd02f86c211903aae71.pdf), [radiative study](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/374/1/63/960455), [current ASD](https://doi.org/10.18434/T4W30F).

**DERIVED.** Converted with hc = 0.0001239841984332 eV cm. Assuming the bound-level assignments, monotonicity and rhodium IE₄ ≤43.7 eV, its middle ratio obeys IE₃/IE₂ ≤43.7/8.09879 <5.397 and its upper ratio IE₄/IE₃ <43.7/14.5050 <3.013. Thus rhodium k=2 and k=3 pass under stated conditions; k=1 still needs an upper bound on IE₂. A finite radiative lifetime or classified excited line by itself would not exclude an autoionizing resonance. The statuses here incorporate that distinction. **Set aside, with why.** A field-evaporation measurement produces rhodium dications at a surface under high electric field. Its field-dependent appearance energy includes surface and electrostatic work and cannot be used directly for a free ground-state ionization bound. A ground-state ionic photoionization onset or a field-free, state-selected collision below the required upper endpoint would answer the missing question. No such source with both necessary qualifications was located in this pass. [Rhodium field-evaporation experiment (1981)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.24.6234).
