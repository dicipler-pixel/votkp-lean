# A matched junction can transmit a damped wave

**HYPOTHESIS to test.** If a zero same-material junction reflection meant the absence of propagation loss, a passive occupied metal with matched optical properties on both sides would have zero attenuation at nonzero photon frequency. A positive measured extinction coefficient in a homogeneous section rejects that implication. The complementary check places vacuum on one side only. This tests the extra optical inference, rather than assigning an electromagnetic meaning to every atomic peel.

**DERIVED.** For a specified optical medium with complex index N=n+iκ, a monochromatic plane wave propagating in +x has E(x)=E(0)exp(i2πNx/λ₀). Its intensity attenuation length is ℓ=λ₀/(4πκ), from |E(x)|²=|E(0)|²exp(−x/ℓ). At a flat semi-infinite vacuum-to-medium interface, taking relative magnetic permeability as unity at optical frequency, the normal-incidence reflection amplitude is (1−N)/(1+N). Reflectance R=[(1−n)²+κ²]/[(1+n)²+κ²]. Two identical optical media give (N−N)/(N+N)=0 even when κ>0. No photon trajectory within a specific atomic chain or dimensioned conversion of the abstract face coordinate follows from these continuum formulas. Optical reflection and intensity attenuation need independent entries in the experiment.

**MEASURED source differences.** REELS-derived Mo constants give R≈0.944 at 413.281 nm. Optical measurements of clean rolled bulk Mo in ultrahigh vacuum give visible near-normal reflectance around 0.55 and agree with the bulk optical values retained above. REELS is an electron-energy-loss measurement with an inversion to n and κ. Its low-energy-derived reflectances should not replace the optical results. Keep both records with their methods. The 1934 bulk-vs-sputtered optical comparison also found preparation dependence. The Ru values are optical constants extracted for films; the tabulated R formula is for a semi-infinite medium with those constants. To compare an actual 8–40 nm Ru film to a measured stack one must include native oxide, substrate and multiple reflections. **RECOGNISED.** The Nb series crosses the ordinary superconducting transition on its way to 4.2 K. Absorption remains finite at violet optical frequencies on both sides. These data at one wavelength do not isolate the microscopic cause of the difference between temperatures. A zero junction Γ in Beasley's equal-clock calculation can coexist with a positive optical extinction coefficient. This rejects the auxiliary assertion that matched faces by themselves give lossless propagation. It leaves the proposed relation of light to intrinsic volume uncalibrated. [Nb temperature series, 1969](https://refractiveindex.info/?shelf=main&book=Nb&page=Golovashkin-293K) · [Rh optical work](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.15.4115) · [open tabulated constants, CC0](https://github.com/polyanskiy/refractiveindex.info-database/tree/main/database/data/main).

**Set aside, with why.** Tc has an experimental energy-loss spectrum, and published theory for the imaginary interband dielectric response. An arbitrary-unit loss signal does not uniquely supply absolutely normalized, orientation-resolved visible n and κ. A measured intensity attenuation length would require calibrated reflectometry or ellipsometry, sample orientation, surface chemistry and thickness. This evidence grade is specific to elemental Tc, distinct from optical data on Tc compounds or atomic-vapour lines. [Chatterjee, 1983](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.27.4722) · [comparison including measured loss, Fig. 9](https://mason.gmu.edu/~imazin2/list/optJP.pdf).
