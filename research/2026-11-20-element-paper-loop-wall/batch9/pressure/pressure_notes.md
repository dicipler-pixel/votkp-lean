# Nb–Rh • what diffraction puts on the physical distance axis

**DERIVED.** Run `python3 cells.py` beside `rh_s1.dat`. It writes [phase_contacts.csv](phase_contacts.csv) from the published inputs below. The 55 rows include separate conditions for the same phase. For a direct-lattice metric G and basis sites s and t, each candidate is d²=(t−s+m)ᵀG(t−s+m), where m is an integer translation. The code reproduced the two shortest shells and closest-contact multiplicity with translations extending to ±2 and ±3. The fcc distances were also checked analytically. A short crystallographic separation identifies an electron-density or transport link only after the appropriate electronic observable is specified. A coordination shell can contain slightly split crystallographic lengths. The multiplicity of the exact central-model minimum should not be substituted for that shell's total coordination.

**HYPOTHESIS / rejection conditions.** A proposed conversion from an unchanged atomic clock to a unique physical distance would need to produce both coexisting Nb distances under a single P,T prescription, and the Rh contraction over an unchanged coordination graph. A gap-density or conductivity claim also needs an electronic state at each P,T. The 55 entries establish geometric controls for such tests.
