# Does the helium-core opening pass below four?

**HYPOTHESIS frozen this run.** Starting from the lithium-like 1s²2s ground ion, take the ratio of the *next* helium-like threshold to the lithium-like threshold (k=Z−2). The frozen prediction is that the central ratio will first fall below 4 between **gold (79) and mercury (80)**. This concerns the bare n=2→1 reference, inside the loop wall on either side. Require the published central values to straddle four; also report whether the complete input boxes lie on opposite sides. If the central crossing occurs elsewhere, the bracket prediction fails. Preserve pointwise residuals from both models and grade the two separately.

Two numerical forecasts give the same bracket: a secant using terbium and ytterbium, and a point-Coulomb Dirac ratio with an empirical correction proportional to 1/Z matched at ytterbium. For a point nucleus of infinite mass and a single electron set g=√(1−(Zα)²), h=√((1+g)/2). The binding energies in units of the rest energy are 1−g and 1−h. Their ratio simplifies to (1−g)/(1−h)=2(1+h)<4 for 0<Zα<1. That exact single-electron formula supplies the reference curve. The multielectron difference calibrated at Z=70 is an empirical residual; a successful crossing would not separate screening, finite-size, recoil and radiative terms. The 1/Z ansatz has one parameter, and the secant uses two data points. [CODATA 2022, hydrogenic Dirac energy](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=958143)

The forecast script indexes no energy with Z>70. The underlying archive already exists and parts of the full core map had previously been surveyed in this project. This is a recorded test of extrapolation from a truncated input range, with no claim of blindness to previously published atomic physics.
