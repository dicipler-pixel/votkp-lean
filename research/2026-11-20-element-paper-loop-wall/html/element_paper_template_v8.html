The Loop Wall: Peeling the Elements from Hydrogen to Astatine

Jeromie Beasley
21 November 2026
AI-assisted. The research direction, the framework and the questions are Jeromie Beasley's. The element data passes were researched with ChatGPT; every number was rechecked and the results formalized in Lean with Claude (Anthropic).

Peel an atom one electron at a time and record each step as a ratio z of successive removal energies. Its reflection Γ = (z − 1)/(z + 1) is the half-step tanh(½ ln z) of the intrinsic ledger. A step is outside the loop wall when Γ² > ½, the condition where two equal faces satisfy sinh ha · sinh hb = 1.

Running the wall through every element from hydrogen to astatine shows a clean structure:

  • Few steps cross it. Only two kinds of step ever reach outside: the anion rung (the extra electron coming off) and the first opening of a closed noble-gas core. Every other step from hydrogen to astatine, all of them checked against the archive, is inside.
  • Bare shells never cross. A change of shell alone always lands inside: the hydrogenic floors 4, 9/4, 16/9, … are inside. The departure from the bare shell carries an opening across, and that departure shrinks shell by shell.
  • Each core opening crosses inward at the same place. The core openings cross inward between carbon and nitrogen, sodium and magnesium, potassium and calcium, rubidium and strontium, and caesium and barium. A frozen prediction for rubidium and strontium passed.
  • The wall is where a loop stops closing. Put a step on two faces of the ball, draw the perpendicular at each face, and ask only whether those two perpendiculars meet. They meet exactly when the step is inside the wall. Outside, the loop is left open.
  • In chemistry language, the wall at the anion rung is where electrophilicity equals hardness. The atoms inside are the ones whose hunger for an electron beats their resistance to change.
  • Direction is kept separately from the wall. Reversing a peel flips the sign of Γ and keeps Γ². A crystal mirror does the opposite to a screw sign, in β-manganese and in trigonal selenium alike. So the ledger carries Russell's two halves separately.

All of it is certified in %%NT%% Lean theorems.

1. The peel and its reflection

For an ordered pair of removal energies, first a then b:

z = b / a, r = ln z, Γ = tanh(r/2) = (z − 1)/(z + 1)

DERIVED The ratio comes back from the reflection, z = (1 + Γ)/(1 − Γ). The face apex of a ball is tanh(r/4) = (√z − 1)/(√z + 1). It returns the full ratio on squaring.

DERIVED Direction. Reversing the order sends z → 1/z, r → −r and Γ → −Γ. The returned power Γ² is the same both ways. The sign remembers which way the peel went; the power does not. A ledger that keeps only Γ² loses the direction.

The loop wall, both branches

Γ² > ½ ⇔ z² − 6z + 1 > 0 ⇔ 0 < z < 3 − 2√2 or z > 3 + 2√2 = 5.8284…

The upper branch is the forward peel and the lower branch is its reverse. A step and its reverse always sit on the same side: z = 6 gives Γ = 5/7 and z = 1/6 gives Γ = −5/7, both with Γ² = 25/49 > ½. With h = ½ ln z, sinh² h = (z − 1)²/4z = Γ²/(1 − Γ²), so the equal-face condition sinh ha sinh hb = 1 is exactly Γ² = ½. The wall of the tree and the wall of the peel are one condition.

DERIVED Fixed points. Γ = 2/3 exactly at z = 5. A bare change of principal shell has ratio ((n+1)/n)²: 4, 9/4, 16/9, 25/16, 36/25, 49/36. Every one is inside the wall. An opening can only stand outside through a departure from the bare shell, which screening provides at low Z.

2. Three boundaries in every element

Each element is read at its anion rung (z₀ = IE₁/EA, where a bound anion exists) and at each closed-core opening: the last removal above the core, then the first removal from it.

  • K opening is the opening of the helium-like 1s² core (2s → 1s).
  • L opening is the opening of the neon-like core, written "Ne core" (3s → 2p).
  • Ar-core opening (the M opening) is the opening of the argon-like core: 4s → 3p for potassium and calcium, and the last 3d removal → 3p from scandium to bromine. That last kind keeps the principal shell (both are n = 3), so its bare hydrogenic ratio is 1, not 16/9.
  • Kr-core opening is the opening of the krypton-like 4s²4p⁶ core, from rubidium on.
  • Xe-core opening is the opening of the xenon-like 5s²5p⁶ core, from caesium on.

Each cell gives z, with Γ below it. Gold means outside the wall, blue inside. Energies come from the NIST-derived archive in the repository. Electron affinities are the 2022 evaluated values.

Two elements make the boundaries visible:

  • Oxygen stands on both sides at once: its anion rung is outside and its core opening inside.
  • Silicon's anion rung is the closest call (Γ = 0.70873 against the wall at 0.70711). It stays outside across its quoted uncertainties.

MEASURED Conduction does not need an outside step. Magnesium and calcium are metals with every ordinary step inside, and so are scandium to zinc, gallium, strontium, yttrium to silver, indium and tin. The wall reads the atom's own ladder. Conduction is a property of the connections between atoms, and section 5 takes it up.

4. The two screening readouts and the 5/8

The last two electrons of every element give two readings of the same pair:

  • the gap per unit charge, S = (h − b)/Z;
  • the effective screening charge, σ = Z − √(2b).

Here h and b are the H-like and He-like energies in hartree. At leading order both tend to 5/8.

%%SR%%
ElementSσS − σ

DERIVED An exact identity: Z(S − σ) = (h − Z²/2) − σ²/2. If the H-like energy were the bare Z²/2, S could never exceed σ, for any element. So the two readings can only pass each other through the H-like ion's extra binding beyond Z²/2: the Dirac term, less the nuclear-mass and Lamb terms. They do pass, between aluminium and silicon, near 0.617.

The three events are distinct, and the round numbers are not where the readings meet:

  • σ crosses 5/8 between neon and sodium (on measured energies, which include nuclear recoil; without recoil this event moves to between fluorine and neon);
  • S crosses σ between aluminium and silicon;
  • S crosses 5/8 between argon and potassium;
  • σ falls through ½ between nickel and copper;
  • σ turns negative between palladium and silver, where the helium-like energy exceeds Z²/2.

DERIVED Every one of these events needs the electron's spinor (Dirac) structure. With a scalar wave alone and an infinitely heavy nucleus, the 1/Z expansion gives S = 5/8 − 0.157666/Z and σ = 5/8 + 0.037646/Z, so S stays below 5/8 and σ above it for every element. The leading spinor term α²Z⁴/8 that the Dirac equation adds to the hydrogen-like energy predicts where S meets σ: Z* = (4σ²/α²)^¼ = 13.0 for σ = 0.617, exactly between aluminium and silicon.

5. The jump as a loop: closing and opening

DERIVED The wall can be read without the sinh formula at all. Put the corner of a ball at the centre of the disk, with one face along each of two perpendicular directions. Each face sits at its half-step distance h = ½ ln z from the corner. At each face draw the line perpendicular to its direction; in the disk that line is a circle meeting the rim at right angles. Each such circle has radius 1/sinh h, so the two circles cross exactly when

sinh ha · sinh hb < 1

When they cross, the loop around the step closes. When they miss, the loop is open. The border case is the classical quadrilateral with three right angles and its fourth corner on the rim. For equal faces the border is z = 3 + 2√2: the loop wall.

On the data from hydrogen to astatine, any cutoff between silver's anion rung at 5.8079 and silicon's at 5.8665 sorts every step the same way, and no cutoff outside that window does. The window is 0.059 wide, and the derived 3 + 2√2 = 5.8284 sits inside it. The ordinary steps alone pin it only between nitrogen's 5.640 and caesium's 5.947; caesium's xenon-core opening is the first ordinary step that a cutoff of 6 gets wrong. The anion rungs sharpen the window further. So the silver and silicon electron affinities are the two measurements that test the wall hardest.

6. The wall in chemistry language

DERIVED At the anion rung the reflection is

Γ₀ = (IE − EA)/(IE + EA) = η / χ

where η = (IE − EA)/2 is Pearson's absolute hardness, how much an atom resists having its electron count changed, and χ = (IE + EA)/2 is Mulliken's electronegativity, how strongly it pulls. Parr's electrophilicity index ω = χ²/2η measures how much energy an atom gains by soaking up electrons until pull and stiffness balance. Then

ω / η = 1 / (2Γ₀²)

so the loop wall Γ₀² = ½ is exactly electrophilicity = hardness. Inside the wall the hunger wins (ω > η); outside, the stiffness wins. At the wall the most electrons the atom would take, Parr's ΔNmax = χ/η, is √2. In the language of waves on a line, Γ² = ½ is the half-power point: half the power is reflected.

RECOGNISED The inside set is not the metals against the nonmetals, not the metalloid line, and not hard against soft. It is the strong electron attachers: fluorine, sulfur, chlorine, selenium, bromine, silver, tellurium and iodine. Silver (ω/η = 1.0025) and silicon (0.9954) bracket the wall. The quantities are standard (Parr and Pearson 1983; Parr, von Szentpály and Liu 1999), but no published use of ω = η, η/χ = 1/√2 or IE/EA = 3 + 2√2 as a boundary turned up; the nearest published special ratio is IE/EA = 3.

Result: gold 3.996, platinum 4.216, iridium 5.733 and astatine 3.857 are inside; osmium 7.830, bismuth 7.731 and lead 20.79 are outside. 7. The ball: faces, junctions and the sea

DERIVED Two kinds of step. Two jumps carry the same formula but use different pairs of inputs:

  • the jump between two peels of one atom, which belongs inside the ball;
  • the jump at the junction between two neighbouring atoms, which belongs on the face.

Two identical neighbours have equal clocks at the junction, so Γ there is 0 and the junction is transparent. In the hydrogen-in-the-space-between projector, putting the atom's peel ratio on the faces instead of inside the ball made sodium look less connected than argon. Moving it inside the ball closes about 98% of that gap.

DERIVED The sum rule. For the occupied projector P with on-site block Ci, the total weight a site can share with all other sites is

Σj≠i ‖Pij‖² = Tr(Ci − Ci²)

The connection cutoff in the model, 1/12, is built as (1/4)/3. In a half-filled chain each site has ½ of weight for two neighbours, so ¼ is the most one link can carry. The cutoff is therefore a Bruggeman third of the per-link share. Carried over to any lattice, it asks whether the nearest shell holds more than a third of all the weight the site shares.

DERIVED What that third measures. It is a reading of how close in a site's sharing is, not of conduction by itself:

  • A half-filled bcc band (sodium's coordination and valence) passes: link weight 0.0333 against the cutoff 1/48.
  • A dilute band must fail it for every filling up to 1/25, though the band is partly filled.
  • A fully filled, gapped band passes it more strongly as the gap grows, while its absolute link weight falls.

So the paper keeps three separate readings for every junction:

  • the absolute shared weight;
  • the fraction carried by each neighbour shell;
  • a bulk transport response.

RECOGNISED The sum rule is the chemists' delocalization sum rule. Tr(Ci − Ci²) is the variance of the number of electrons on site i, and ‖Pij‖² is half the delocalization index between sites i and j (per spin), the quantity behind Bader's atoms-in-molecules analysis and the Wiberg–Mayer bond orders. Dividing the nearest shell's share by the whole budget is the new step. A half-filled chain gives exactly 8/π² = 0.811.

DERIVED The third reading comes from the same weights. For any position operator X that is constant on each site,

Var(X) = ½ Σij ‖Pij‖² (xi − xj)²

so the localization tensor (Resta and Sorella) is the second moment of the same off-site weights whose zeroth moment gives the shell fraction. It stays finite in insulators and diverges in metals, and it is the ground-state quantum metric: the IQGT object itself. Measured from the same weights, the site-centre spread per electron grows with the size of the sampled crystal in rubidium (5.68, 7.97, 10.38, 12.62 Å² for meshes of 4, 6, 8 and 10) and stays flat in solid krypton (0.0669 Å² at every mesh). Growth against saturation is the metal against the insulator, which the shell fraction alone could not show. Periodic calculations of real crystals give nearest-shell fractions of 0.478 for rubidium (a metal) and 0.991 for solid krypton (an insulator). Both pass the third, which confirms that the third measures how close in the sharing is, while the second moment and the band crossing tell the metal from the insulator.

8. Direction: the even half and the odd half

Walter Russell's two-column method carried every quantity that stays the same under a mirror and lost every quantity that flips. The peel ledger keeps both halves:

  • Γ² and the wall classification are the even half.
  • The sign of Γ is the odd half under reversing the peel.

DERIVED A real crystal supplies the spatial half. β-manganese comes in two mirror crystals, space groups P4₁32 and P4₃32. Its shortest-contact screw orbit (step 2.3743 Å) gives a four-site path with screw sign χ = (d₀ × d₁) · d₂ = +9.9306 Å³.

Trigonal selenium (P3₁21, crystal record 1534711, bond 2.30066 Å) is a second, different screw: a three-fold helix rather than β-manganese's four-fold one. It obeys the same law, and so does trigonal tellurium (P3₁21, crystal record 2020222, bond 2.8345 Å). In Barron's terms the screw sign is a true-chirality quantity: odd under a mirror, even under time reversal. The peel reflection is the opposite kind. Tellurium's handedness is measured directly: current-induced magnetisation and the circularly polarised Raman shift both reverse between the two hands. Closing the β-manganese screw path around one lattice period gives an unknot for both hands. An unframed knot polynomial of that closure cannot tell them apart, but the framed winding and the local sign can. The knot side of VOTKP needs a closed path that keeps that sign.

9. What stays open

  • Bulk conduction. The projector still uses two-level hydrogen cells. Each element needs its own outer shell and empty states, with a transport response (a boundary twist with size scaling) as the third reading.
  • The knot assignment. No element yet has a reproducible closed path to which the volume polynomial applies. β-Mn's framed screw is the first candidate.
  • Clocks and lengths. Each peel ratio is a dimensionless clock. Turning one into a distance in ångströms needs a calibration fixed before the comparison.
  • Francium and element 119. Francium's opening straddles the wall on present data. Element 119's call is written down and waits for the element to be made.
  • The rest of the table. Radon onward, and polonium's affinity, continue batch by batch, with every energy checked against the archive before it is used.
