**Abstract.** I peel each atom one electron at a time and record every step as the ratio $z$ of successive removal energies, with reflection $\Gamma = (z-1)/(z+1) = \tanh(\tfrac12\ln z)$ on my Smith disc and two books $E^- = (\Delta IE)^2$ and $E^+ = (\Sigma IE)^2$ whose ratio is $\Gamma^2$. The loop wall $\Gamma^2 = \tfrac12$, at $z = 3+2\sqrt2 = 5.828427$, is derived, not fitted: it is where two equal faces at a right angle stop closing a loop, the point where their commutator becomes a single Jordan block, and the only equal-face cutoff for which the sum and product forms of the two-face rule agree. Run through every element from hydrogen to astatine, the wall sorts 3,570 ordinary steps so that exactly eight cross — lithium, beryllium, boron and carbon at the helium core, and sodium, potassium, rubidium and caesium at the neon, argon, krypton and xenon cores — and every core sequence crosses inward once and stays inside. At the anion rung the reflection is hardness over electronegativity, and the wall is exactly electrophilicity equal to hardness; 53 of 65 adopted anion rungs lie outside, and a heavy-anion prediction written before the numbers held seven of seven. Every classification survives only for cutoffs between silver's anion rung and silicon's, 5.80790 to 5.86654, and the wall lies inside. Across the visible, all 35 metals with optical data reflect above the same half-power circle and none of the 6 non-metals do. The anion rung and the first core opening give each element an address in seven classes. My face apex $\tanh(\ln z/4)$ is exactly the scale coordinate of orbital survival under a sudden change of Coulomb scale, and a signed same-charge reflection, anchored to a mass, predicts that holmium-163 and dysprosium-163 swap ground-mass order between 56+ and 57+.
