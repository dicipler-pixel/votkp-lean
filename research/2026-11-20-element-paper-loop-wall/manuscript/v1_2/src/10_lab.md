## What survives the passage from a peel to a laboratory reading?

**HYPOTHESIS.** "The vector determines the direction, that may be decay." In the element work a direction has to say what is ordered: the two thresholds of one atom, the same-charge thresholds of two isobars, the endpoints of an oriented crystal walk, or the polarisation and frequency of a light probe. Each ordering permits an explicit reversal experiment. The operations act on different spaces, so a sign that survives one operation can reverse under another. The test is to write the observation map and identify what survives it. I start from the intrinsic record; ångströms, electronvolts and laboratory time enter only where a crystallographic, mass or spectral datum is supplied.

### An internal face, a junction, a current

**DERIVED.** The internal peel compares two successive removal thresholds. A junction compares the clocks on opposing sites. For identical clocks the junction ratio is one and its scalar reflection is zero, whatever the internal helium-core opening. In the original finite hydrogen-cell construction, sodium's internal L opening on an exterior port gave a weakest squared link of about 0.000292; keeping that clock inside and matching the exterior clocks gave about 0.000865. Argon's corresponding L-opening calculation changed from 0.000648 to 0.000872 (its first ordinary step, from 0.000804 to 0.000874), and the all-matched control gave 0.000876 in both conventions, against the all-ones reference 0.000875546. Putting the atom's peel ratio on the faces had made sodium look less connected than argon; moving it inside the ball closes about 98% of that gap, and every row stays below $1/12$. These are numerical outputs of the specified two-level cell. They test whether a clock was attached to the correct link; they do not supply the bands or the filling of sodium metal.

**DERIVED.** For an orthogonal occupied projector $P = P^2 = P^\dagger$, choose mutually orthogonal, complete site projectors $\Pi_i$. Put $P_{ij} = \Pi_iP\Pi_j$, $C_i = P_{ii}$ on its site, and $X_{ij} = \operatorname{Tr}(P_{ij}P_{ji})$. The diagonal block of $P^2 = P$ gives an exact budget,
$$B_i = \operatorname{Tr}\big(C_i - C_i^2\big) = \sum_{j\ne i}X_{ij}.$$
If a shell contains $n$ equivalent neighbours, the strict per-neighbour condition $X_{ij}>B_i/(3n)$ says exactly that more than one third of the off-site weight lies on that shell. For inequivalent neighbours the actual shell sum is required, and the normalised share needs $B_i>0$. The one-third criterion is an assigned cutoff. A claim about a macroscopic percolation transition would need an independently defined network, conducting constituents, a disorder ensemble and a conductivity observable.

**RECOGNISED.** This budget is the chemists' delocalisation sum rule. $\operatorname{Tr}(C_i-C_i^2)$ is the variance of the number of electrons on site $i$, and $\|P_{ij}\|^2$ is half the delocalisation index between sites $i$ and $j$ per spin — the quantity behind Bader's atoms-in-molecules analysis and the Wiberg–Mayer bond orders. Dividing the nearest shell's share by the whole budget is my new step. A half-filled chain gives exactly $8/\pi^2 = 0.811$ (checked: 0.81056934 against 0.81056947).

**DERIVED, false controls.** For one spin-degenerate orbital per site in the translation-invariant band model with per-spin filling $0<f<1$, the magnitude of a separated per-spin matrix element cannot exceed $f$ by the triangle inequality on its Fourier sum. Thus $X_{ij}\le2f^2$ and $B_i = 2f(1-f)$. For eight neighbours the nearest-shell fraction is at most $8f/(1-f)$, which cannot exceed one third if $f\le1/25$, even though the band is partly filled (Lean: `dilute_bcc_fails`). Conversely, a filled, gapped bonding-band example has strong nearest-shell concentration, and passes more strongly as the gap grows while its absolute link weight falls. In the half-filled bcc example the link weight 0.0333 exceeds its $1/48$ cutoff. Together these rule out inferring conductivity from this criterion alone. What it measures is the spatial distribution of occupied-state sharing. So I keep three separate readings for every junction: the absolute shared weight, the fraction carried by each neighbour shell, and a bulk transport response.

### From a spatial distribution to a projector differential

**DERIVED, with crystallographic inputs.** In the rubidium and krypton calculation a site is a real-space Wigner–Seitz cell. For a supercell translation class $R$ the two-spin squared weight $X_R$ obeys $\sum_RX_R = \nu$, where $\nu = 1$ for the chosen outer rubidium band and $\nu = 8$ for the chosen krypton valence subspace. The spatial partition assigns each point the position of its site's centre. With all tied shortest Cartesian images averaged, the per-electron tensor is
$$\Lambda^c_{ab} = \frac{1}{2\nu}\sum_RX_R\,\big\langle R_aR_b\big\rangle_{\text{shortest}}.$$
Intracell structure contributes zero at $R = 0$ by this convention. The lattice parameters are 5.700 Å for bcc rubidium (297 K crystallographic input) and 5.721 Å for fcc krypton (58 K input); the calculation uses fixed structures and zero-temperature occupation, and the temperatures identify the structural sources, not a matched thermal experiment. For any position operator constant on each site,
$$\operatorname{Var}(X) = \tfrac12\sum_{ij}\|P_{ij}\|^2(x_i-x_j)^2,$$
the second moment of the same off-site weights whose zeroth moment is the shell fraction.

Rubidium's spread grows with the mesh and krypton's saturates: on the sampled sizes, rubidium delocalises and krypton stays local. The nearest-shell fractions of both exceed one third — which confirms that the third measures how close in the sharing is, while the second moment and the band crossing tell the metal from the insulator. The two normalisations (norm-conserving and projector-augmented-wave) are separate computations and are reported separately.

**DERIVED.** On a finite periodic crystal, let $U_q = \exp(iq\cdot r)$ and $L(q) = \|(1-P)U_qP\|_F^2/(N_e|q|^2)$, with the microscopic position; replacing it by a cell centre defines a different operator. Let $r_k$ be the one-spin occupied rank and $M_k$ the overlap between occupied subspaces at $k$ and $k+q$ with the prescribed reciprocal-zone identification. Since translation permutes the sectors, exactly
$$L(q) = \frac{\sum_k|r_k-r_{k+q}|/2+\sum_k\big[\min(r_k,r_{k+q})-\|M_k\|_F^2\big]}{|q|^2\sum_kr_k}.$$
Each summand in the second sum is non-negative, because the singular values of an overlap of orthonormal frames are at most one. At $12^3$, rank change accounts for about 97.37% of rubidium's microscopic reading. A source sector of larger rank than its target supplies a kernel for the complete overlap whenever the source inclusion is injective and the overlap factors through that target on the source sector; in finite dimension its determinant vanishes, which in the occupied Slater state forces the modulus of the twist determinant to zero. Constant rank alone guarantees no invertibility. Three algebraic declarations and an independent kernel replay address this factorisation. Ground-state composition and measured transport enter elsewhere. (Resta and Sorella 1999.)

**DERIVED, conditional bridge to my intrinsic construction.** Identify two equal-rank occupied projectors in one periodic reference space with the same Bloch sewing. Then
$$\operatorname{Tr}\big[P(k)\big(1-P(k+te)\big)\big] = \tfrac12\big\|P(k+te)-P(k)\big\|_F^2 .$$
For a differentiable family, dividing by $t^2$ and letting $t\to0$ recovers the local projector tangent form $\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_eP)^2]$ — the metric of my Intrinsic Quantum Geometric Tensor. The localisation tensor of a gapped crystal is therefore the ground-state integral of my intrinsic metric: finite in insulators, growing without bound in metals. This is the precise place where the material calculation meets the IQGT itself. Integration over an infinite system needs separate regularity and limit-interchange assumptions; the rank jump in rubidium is a direct obstruction to applying the argument uniformly, and finite growth is evidence of size dependence, not a thermodynamic-limit proof. The Euclidean length used to report crystal distances gives no spacetime meaning to the intrinsic tensor. An interacting three-dimensional Coulomb material can keep finite longitudinal fluctuations even where a transverse metallic response is present. (Souza, Wilkens and Martin 2000; Resta 2006; Onishi and Fu 2025.)

**RECOGNISED in the specified calculation.** Four numerical intervals for krypton at $10^3$ and $12^3$, set from the $4^3$–$8^3$ meshes, all contained the later microscopic or twist-log values; at $12^3$ the two were 0.2502921 and 0.2516569 Å². This tests extrapolation inside one gapped computational model. Changing the pseudopotential family at $6^3$ changes the krypton cell-centre estimate by about 0.00630 Å², much more than increasing the mesh from $8^3$ to $10^3$ in the fixed norm-conserving model. Rubidium's serial $10^3$ norm-conserving run was stopped and its $12^3$ run completed; a smooth progression through a missing mesh would be unsupported. A rubidium Fermi-surface control and its plasma energy (3.714–3.716 eV computed against 3.570–3.591 eV reference) and krypton's static dielectric constant (1.92155) are kept with the runs.

### Light as a probe with a specified channel

**MEASURED AND DERIVED.** The helium-core crossing between carbon and nitrogen gives a direct opportunity to test the atomic loop wall: carbon's and nitrogen's helium-core openings have opposite equal-face classifications. On a common transition of their lithium-like ions, published integrated resonance strengths are $0.483\pm0.10$ and $0.413\pm0.07$, and their quoted intervals overlap. Carbon and boron were measured at ALS, nitrogen and oxygen at SOLEIL, so a fitted wall step is confounded with facility. A separate single-facility electron-impact experiment detected an inner-shell excitation contribution on both sides, so loss of this excitation channel on crossing is ruled out. Single-ion measurements cannot implement the mixed carbon–nitrogen two-face construction without a physical coupling and a joint readout. An independent single-facility sequence with a predeclared effect size, normalisation and comparison to a smooth baseline is a concrete discriminating test. (Müller et al. 2009; Al Shorman et al. 2013; McLaughlin et al. 2017; Crandall et al. 1979.)

**DERIVED, channel dependence.** At normal incidence from vacuum on a homogeneous non-magnetic optical half-space, the measured complex index $n+i\kappa$ gives $R = [(n-1)^2+\kappa^2]/[(n+1)^2+\kappa^2]$ for the selected polarisation, so $R-\tfrac12$ has the sign of $(n-3)^2+\kappa^2-8$. At 650 nm the two principal responses of osmium give 0.48460 and 0.52845. The same sample can straddle optical half power by changing polarisation at fixed wavelength while its isolated-atom ladder is unchanged. That is the control on treating the common number one half as a physical identity between optics and ionisation. Signs at adjacent tabulated wavelengths survive display-rounding controls, which do not give the experimental systematics.

**DERIVED, full-spectrum constraint.** Let $T = \int_0^\infty\operatorname{Im}\epsilon(\omega)\,d\omega$ and $L = \int_0^\infty\operatorname{Im}[-1/\epsilon(\omega)]\,d\omega$ for a stable, passive scalar dielectric. Assume $\epsilon(\infty) = 1$, $1<\epsilon_s = \epsilon(0)<\infty$, non-zero finite $T$, and valid unsubtracted dispersion representations for the direct and inverse response. Set $a(\xi) = \epsilon(i\xi)-1$ and $f(x) = x/(1+x)$. The imaginary-frequency dispersion relations give $T = \int a(\xi)\,d\xi$ and $L = \int f(a(\xi))\,d\xi$, and positivity gives $T>L$. For the other side, set $s = \epsilon_s-1$ and normalise the positive transverse spectral measure with weight $2\operatorname{Im}\epsilon(\omega)/(\pi s\omega)$; in that measure $a(\xi)$ is the mean of $s\omega^2/(\omega^2+\xi^2)$. Concavity of $f$, followed by integration in $\xi$, gives
$$1<\frac TL\le\sqrt{\epsilon_s}.$$
One lossless resonance saturates the upper bound. For a full-spectrum forward ratio to exceed the loop wall, $\epsilon_s>17+12\sqrt2 = (3+2\sqrt2)^2$ is necessary. At the same $\epsilon_s = 36$ a single-line control gives 6 and a split-line control gives 2.30323. The static value supplies an upper bound; oscillator distribution still matters. A Drude metal fails the finite-static hypothesis.

**MEASURED INPUTS, CONDITIONAL RESULT.** For liquid krypton at 122 K, the density-matched static correlation gives $\epsilon_s\simeq1.66065$, so its full ratio is bounded above by about 1.28866. In the fitted 9–11 eV reflectivity window, $T_W = 3.27608$ and $L_W = 0.453872$ in photon-energy units, giving $T_W/L_W = 7.21806$. The inequalities $T\ge T_W$ and $L\ge T/\sqrt{\epsilon_s}$ then put at least 82.1% of the complete loss area outside the measured window, conditional on both fits and the scalar response assumptions. The chosen finite-window fit also stays within a necessary inverse-moment budget for every ellipsoid factor between zero and one. A broadband measurement of longitudinal loss at the same temperature and density could challenge the conclusion. (Laporte et al. 1987; Harvey and Lemmon 2005.)

**DERIVED, an inference that failed this way.** With a vacuum ellipsoid boundary let $\chi_N = \chi/(1+N\chi)$ and $M_N = \int\operatorname{Im}\chi_N\,d\omega$. The sphere has $N = 1/3$. For a conductor and positive $N_1<N_2$, the same argument applied to the dressed susceptibility gives $M_{N_1}/M_{N_2}\le\sqrt{N_2/N_1}$ when the causal, finite-area hypotheses hold for those dressed channels, so the full sphere-to-longitudinal ratio is bounded by $\sqrt3$. Applying this bound directly to four interpolations of a measured rubidium optical window initially suggested missing-area estimates. Each interpolation spent more than the whole positive inverse-moment allowance $\pi/(2N)$ at the admissible witness $N = 0.132$, overspending by about 4.1% to 12.9%, and adding positive spectral weight elsewhere only worsens it. The numerical rubidium missing-area fraction is withdrawn for all four interpolants; the optical measurements remain inputs for better causal fits. The ellipsoid's one third is a boundary shape, with a different derivation and meaning from the one-third nearest-shell criterion.

### The gap needs a position, a direction and a phase

**DERIVED from measured structures.** In dhcp neodymium the two crystallographic cages have identical lists of twelve distances, zero first vector sums and equal second-moment tensors. One contains six antipodal bond pairs and the other three. A basal third angular moment separates them, with squared magnitudes 0 and $1/108$ in the documented dimensionless convention. Polarised-neutron measurements found their susceptibilities agree above 40 K but diverge below 19.9 K. The geometry separates the sites at both temperatures; extracting a magnetic transition temperature would need an electronic and thermal model. In coexisting high-pressure ytterbium phases at 30.28 and 31.28 GPa, measured refinements give a smaller volume per atom in hcp but a longer shortest contact than bcc, and the signs survive three quoted lattice-parameter widths in both cells. These controls rule out reconstructing a unique gap geometry from total volume or from an atom's scalar peel face alone. (Moon et al. 1983; Storm et al. 2026.)

**DERIVED, orientation control.** For an embedded three-bond path let $\chi = \det(e_0,e_1,e_2)$. Reversing the path negates all three bonds and reverses their order; the two orientation changes cancel. A spatial mirror reverses $\chi$. In the specified handed high-pressure ytterbium refinement the average of $\chi$ over all 64 shortest three-step walks vanishes, while the dimensionless third moment $\langle(\chi/V_{\text{atom}})^3\rangle = -3/128$ changes sign under reflection. The choice of graph, ensemble of walks and orientation of the coordinate frame are indispensable. Powder diffraction supplies an allowed enantiomorphic model and cannot by itself establish the absolute hand of a specimen. A finite contact walk or a screw closed only modulo a translation does not define a classical knot polynomial; a defined embedded closure and invariance under its allowed changes are required on the Volume of the Knot Potential side.
