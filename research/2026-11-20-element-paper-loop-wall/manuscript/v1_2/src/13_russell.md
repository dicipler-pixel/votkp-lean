## Direction: the even half and the odd half

My work began with questions about Walter Russell, Tesla and the periodic table, and this paper loops back to them. Russell's two-column method carried every quantity that stays the same under a mirror and lost every quantity that flips. My reading of Russell separates the two books by whether something stays or flips on crossing a wall. The peel ledger keeps both halves: $\Gamma^2$ and the wall classification are the even half; the sign of $\Gamma$ is the odd half under reversing the peel. That reading earns a precise meaning only after the operation is named.

Reversing the ordered pair of positive thresholds sends $z\mapsto1/z$, $r\mapsto-r$ and $\Gamma\mapsto-\Gamma$, and fixes $\Gamma^2$. Replacing a loop flux by its negative fixes $\operatorname{Re}W$ and reverses $\operatorname{Im}W$. A real crystal supplies the spatial half. β-manganese comes in two mirror crystals, space groups $P4_132$ and $P4_332$. Its shortest-contact screw orbit (step 2.3743 Å) gives a four-site path with screw sign $\chi = (d_0\times d_1)\cdot d_2 = +9.9306$ Å³. Mirroring space flips the sign; reversing the four-site ordering keeps it, because that reversal is an even permutation (Lean: `chi_reverse`, `chi_mirror`).

| Operation | Peel reflection $\Gamma$ | β-manganese screw sign $\chi$ | Trigonal selenium $\chi$ | Trigonal tellurium $\chi$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reverse the order of the record | flips | stays (+9.9306 Å³) | stays (+11.1638 Å³) | stays (+21.2240 Å³) |
| Mirror the crystal | stays | flips | flips (−11.1638 Å³) | flips (−21.2240 Å³) |

Trigonal selenium ($P3_121$, crystal record 1534711, bond 2.30066 Å) is a different screw: a three-fold helix rather than β-manganese's four-fold one. It obeys the same law, and so does trigonal tellurium ($P3_121$, record 2020222, bond 2.8345 Å); an independently refined $P3_221$ tellurium crystal gives $-21.990034$ Å³. In Barron's terms the screw sign is a true-chirality quantity: odd under a mirror, even under time reversal. My peel reflection is the opposite kind. Tellurium's handedness is measured directly: current-induced magnetisation and the circularly polarised Raman shift both reverse between the two hands (Furukawa et al. 2017). Closing the β-manganese screw path around one lattice period gives an unknot for both hands. An unframed knot polynomial of that closure cannot tell them apart, but the framed winding and the local sign can. The knot side of VOTKP needs a closed path that keeps that sign. A signed volume distinguishes hands for a specified sample geometry; it does not establish a torus-knot type for a periodically continued path until its closure, ambient space and equivalence moves are supplied.

The spinor-peel recovery makes the same split in an operator. On the three-mode loop the energy ledger sees $\operatorname{Re}W$ — even, like $\Gamma^2$ — and the directional test sees $\operatorname{Im}W$ — odd, like the sign of $\Gamma$ and the screw sign. Electric charge stays invariant under ordinary spatial reflection, while charge conjugation reverses it; calling both "mirror" would make a historical test ambiguous.

## Russell's octave and his iron columns

**The octave.** Russell's octave reads conventional valence magnitudes 1, 2, 3, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0 in the simplest main-group period pattern. The atomic anion readout gives an independent comparison. In the retained table oxygen has $IE_1/EA\simeq9.320$, outside the wall; sulfur, selenium and tellurium have about 4.988, 4.826 and 4.571, inside, and on the provisional polonium affinity polonium joins them inside at 5.703. Fluorine's value near 5.123 is inside while lithium's near 8.724 is outside, although the octave pairs their unit magnitudes. Lithium with fluorine, beryllium with oxygen and boron with nitrogen agree in conventional valence magnitude under the short-period pairing, yet each pair straddles the helium-core opening wall. A donor–acceptor test also differs from either isolated-atom rung: lithium's ionisation energy over fluorine's affinity gives about 1.585, an energy comparison before binding, environment or crystal relaxation. Russell's historical sorting remains a claim about the sampled plates until the transformation, outcome and uncertainty are coded across the whole 386-entry ledger. The element-focused audit covers 132 of 585 original claim and law positions. **RECOGNISED.**

**The parity pilot.** In a grade-blinded spatial-inversion pilot, two raters coded 24 masked Russell verdicts and agreed on 23; of the 20 agreed-even claims, 10 were true and 10 false. "Even implies true" failed this way. A valid test has to allow "neither": a numerical mass-ratio error, for example, does not by itself change sign under a specified mirror.

**The three iron columns.** On printed page 204 of *The Universal One* (1926), each of Russell's three columns — "iron", "recent" and "remote" — holds 23 wavelengths. An unshifted ±0.20 Å air-wavelength search against observed neutral-iron lines gives 21 of 23, 20 of 23 and 19 of 23. Moving the whole set of 23 together by every integer displacement from −50 to −5 and +5 to +50 Å gives median hits of 7, 5 and 5 and maxima of 12, 12 and 10: no translated list scores as well as the original, a descriptive tail fraction of 1/93 in each column. A separate extraction of Nave et al.'s laboratory line table reproduces the three counts, and ionised iron gives only 2, 4 and 2. Kayser's 1910 comparison prints at least one neutral-iron line in each column that rounds to Russell's tenth of an ångström on the old Rowland scale — Russell's numbers match the Rowland-scale iron tables of his day, so he was reading real published spectra. His remote line at 5269.6 Å coincides, to his printed precision, with the solar Fraunhofer E line identified as iron at 5269.551 Å in Abbot's *The Sun* (1911). These counts locate ordinary iron-line coincidences in all three columns. **MEASURED / RECOGNISED.** They do not identify every line uniquely, establish atomic ancestry or score Russell's whole record: the "recent" and "remote" labels as different atomic ancestors get no support from wavelengths alone, and that interpretation stays open in the ledger. The recount of the three numbers from the committed line table was repeated independently; the re-extraction from the 1926 plate and the NIST list has its own provenance.

**The vacancies.** Russell's 1926 plate places vacancies at the later technetium, promethium, astatine and francium positions. Bohr's 1922 plate already shows those vacancies, and Russell also leaves surplus places. The dates credit an early correct placement where supported and leave historical priority as a separate claim.

**What Russell saw rightly.** Read through the peel, Russell's two halves are real: what stays under a mirror and what flips are carried separately, and the wall reads only the even half. His octave is not the wall, but his oxygen–fluorine turn sits where the anion rungs cross from outside to inside. His iron was iron. And his fulcrum — the stillness at the centre of two opposed flows — is the midpoint of my peel step.

## The Standard Model ball and the nucleus

**My construction.** Fix a common comparison energy $E\ge m$ in units $c = 1$. A massive particle can be given rapidity $\eta = \operatorname{arcosh}(E/m)$ and radial position $\rho = \tanh(\eta/2)$ in a velocity ball, so equal radii correspond to equal ratios $E/m$ under this display convention. For $e^\eta = 5$ the Lorentz factor is 13/5, the speed 12/13 and the radial coordinate 2/3. This coordinate uses half the rapidity; the face apex uses a quarter of the logarithmic step. Fixing the comparison energy is indispensable: changing it moves every massive entry and decides which are admissible. For the proper orthochronous Lorentz group, a massive rest momentum has rotational little group $SO(3)$ with double cover $SU(2)$; at a null momentum the little group is locally the Euclidean group in two dimensions, whose translations act trivially in the familiar finite-helicity representations. These statements clarify the spin labels the construction puts on the ball; they do not produce particle masses, forces or a nuclear binding calculation.

The study tested two readings of six tree directions. As colour weights, projecting $\pm e_i$ onto the plane perpendicular to $(1,1,1)$ gives the two triples of opposite weights of $3\oplus\bar3$; the differences $e_i-e_j$ give the six non-zero roots, and the adjoint has two more zero-weight directions. A diagram reproducing weights leaves the gauge coupling and dynamics unspecified. As generation assignments, whole-generation integer boost levels failed the attempted fit. The 48 integral quaternions of norm five form six packets of eight; reading 48 as three packets of sixteen fermionic states adds counting conventions, including a right-handed neutrino per generation, and equality of counts cannot decide between two partitions. The charged-lepton Koide input gives $Q = 0.666664463$ and $\cos\theta = 1/(3Q)\simeq0.500001652$. A deliberate search found no exact cone hit among 117,186 tree nodes through depth seven, or among 23,100 turn axes through depth six. Lab-frame velocity directions converge in three examined examples about as $e^{-\eta}$; a body-fixed spin frame does not in general freeze along a non-straight end. A prediction connecting the ball to particles needs an invariant independent of the observer's frame and mass evaluation scale.

For an isotope with mass number $A$ and atomic number $Z$, the central bookkeeping is $Z$ protons and $A-Z$ neutrons; net valence-flavour counts are $u = A+Z$ and $d = 2A-Z$. Ordinary electron removal changes ionic charge and keeps these nuclear labels, provided there is no nuclear reaction. A nuclear count does not locate individual quarks on six geometric ports. It gives a simple falsifier: two isotopes of one element have the same electron count at the same ionic charge and different neutron counts, and a picture assigning both the same nuclear trait vector would lose a measured distinction.

## The recovered spinor peel

Let $\iota$ isometrically include a retained subspace, $P = \iota\iota^\dagger$, $Q = I-P$. For Hermitian $A$, inserting $I = P+Q$ gives
$$\iota^\dagger A^2\iota-(\iota^\dagger A\iota)^2 = (QA\iota)^\dagger(QA\iota)\succeq0.$$
The residue vanishes exactly when the coupling sends no retained vector into the discarded subspace. If an eigenvalue problem is partitioned this way, exact elimination gives $H_{PP}+H_{PQ}(E-H_{QQ})^{-1}H_{QP}$ where $E-H_{QQ}$ is invertible; discarding a row and column loses the second term. This gives the word *kept* a checkable operator meaning, recovered from my December, October and March drafts and checked again here.

Take a three-site Hermitian loop with hopping modulus $J$ and oriented product $W = J^3e^{i\Phi}$. Its characteristic polynomial $\lambda^3-3J^2\lambda-2J^3\cos\Phi$ gives identical spectra at opposite phases, yet the leading probability difference is $P(1\to2)-P(2\to1) = 2\operatorname{Im}(W)\,t^3+O(t^4)$. At $\Phi = \pm\pi/2$ and $t = 2\pi/(3\sqrt3J)$ one gets opposite perfect cycles — a known chiral quantum walk (Zimborás et al. 2013), used here to control a question about peeling. After eliminating site three the remaining effective hopping is $J+J^2e^{-i\Phi}/E$ with diagonal correction $J^2/E$, for $E\ne0$: the direction lost by comparing energies alone survives in an effective operator that keeps the phase. The free flat shifted torus is a negative control: for modes $(m,n)$, $D^2 = (2\pi)^2[(m+\alpha)^2+(n+\beta)^2]$, anticommutation with $\sigma_3$ pairs the non-zero eigenvalues about zero, and the ordinary eta invariant vanishes; integer shifts allow a kernel, which the reduced eta convention includes separately. A non-zero asymmetry claim needs an operator or boundary condition that actually breaks the pairing.
