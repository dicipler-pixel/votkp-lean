## The November working ledger and its open decisions

My dated notes ask how a hypersurface peels itself, how a leading vector meets a covector at the cut, whether the space between atoms sets the lattice, how a ground can hold or move charge, how heating and cooling build structure, whether light guides the electronic response, and whether a directed change can select a decay. They put colour, superfluidity, conductivity and differential response to light on the agenda. My standing directions, in my words, are these: the way the hypersurface peels itself is where I want to look; conductivity or not is the key in all elements, set by the electron, with light as its twin guiding it; the space between atoms is the non-conductive part, like ground or the vacuum, and it varies with each element's lattice spacing; heat to cold is what forms matter into a lattice; time decides which branch gets cut at the hypersurface; the vector determines the direction, and that may be decay. Behind all of it is my quest: whether memory can make electricity faster. And the best question from those days stays open at the head of this ledger: when the needle swings, what does it keep? The checks below keep apart a proposed mechanism, a number inferred by an effective model and a laboratory observation.

### The volume-fraction proxy and the intrinsic face

**DERIVED.** The November working sheet explores the proxy $E = 1-(r_{\text{cov}}/r_{\text{room}})^3$, where $r_{\text{room}}$ comes from the observed per-atom volume and $r_{\text{cov}}$ from a chosen covalent-radius convention. The corresponding ratio $z_E = 1/(1-E)$ has $\Gamma_E = E/(2-E)$, so $E = 2/3$ gives $z_E = 3$ and $\Gamma_E = 1/2$. This is a valid re-expression of the proxy, and it is different from the internal clock $IE_{k+1}/IE_k$, the face apex $\tanh(r/4)$, the junction mismatch and the off-site projector weight. The $2/3$ empty-fraction line and the $3+2\sqrt2$ equal-face loop wall are separate thresholds. Ground-state tables and a chosen radius prescription cannot fix the microscopic contents of an interstitial region.

**RECOGNISED, archived results.** The 81-row current-data sheet has one exception at the fixed $2/3$ line (boron) and two at 0.63 (arsenic, polonium). On 35 metals the empty fraction and log DC conductivity have Spearman correlation only +0.072; the same fraction with optical phase has about +0.78. A phase cutoff at 0.661 rad separates the 41 members of one visible-optics sample; it was fitted on that sample. Measured pressure, density and phase changes give harder tests: the proxy catches the silicon and germanium melting changes in the supplied density brackets; fluid hydrogen and dense xenon put the observed metallisation close to the competing Herzfeld estimate; and expanded alkalis stay metallic beyond the proposed density line. A same-material phase comparison controls for the isolated-atom ladder. A second proxy built from static polarisability is a distinct model.

**RECOGNISED, archived results.** The signed optical-track area is clockwise in 39 of 41 first-set cases. The independent 60-material test records 56 clockwise, three counterclockwise and one flat. The fixed sign-wall optical metal classifier misses 25 of 41 and failed this way. Family-level outlier rates run from about 0.02 in the f-block lanthanum-to-lutetium group to 0.44 in zinc–cadmium–mercury. Scalar-relativistic contraction correlates with one heavy-element margin, while its pooled relation to outlier rate is weak and several computations failed to converge. The open ledger keeps every excluded run and every optical, proxy and radius convention. Colour is wavelength-, polarisation-, surface- and phase-dependent, while the free-atom threshold is shared across these conditions.

### What only this work has

Stated plainly, these are the results that are mine and that I have not found elsewhere:

1. **An exact wall from geometry, not a fit.** $3+2\sqrt2$ comes from asking when two faces at a right angle stop closing a loop, and it is the one equal-face cutoff for which the sum and product forms of the two-face rule agree (Lean-proved).
2. **The data window that contains it.** Every classification from hydrogen to astatine survives only for cutoffs in $[5.80790, 5.86654)$, silver to silicon, and the wall is inside.
3. **The eight outside steps.** Of 3,570 ordinary steps, only eight cross, and every one is a first noble-gas core opening; each core sequence crosses inward once and stays inside.
4. **The anion rung as $\omega = \eta$.** The wall at the zeroth rung is exactly electrophilicity equal to hardness, with 53 of 65 adopted rungs outside and a heavy-anion prediction that held 7 of 7.
5. **The colour line.** All 35 metals reflect above the half-power circle and none of the 6 non-metals — the same circle as the wall on my Smith disc.
6. **The element address.** Anion rung and first core opening give seven classes, with the out-and-out class lithium, boron, carbon, sodium, potassium, rubidium and caesium.
7. **The face apex as orbital survival.** The textbook sudden-change overlap is exactly a function of my quarter-log face apex.
8. **The mass-ordering reversal.** A signed same-charge reflection, multiplied by an anchored energy scale, predicts an increment of a measurable isobar mass difference, and holmium–dysprosium reverses order between 56+ and 57+.

### Research judgements and tests before the next claim

1. **Close a measured loop.** Scalar clocks on a connected network are identifiable up to one gain. Build an independently observed triangle across charge states or species, propagate shared-reference covariance, and declare a closure tolerance before acquisition. Ratios formed from the same three numbers telescope identically and only test arithmetic; an independent edge tests the premise.
2. **Test the first missing datum.** Select an admissible pair matching the proposed retained ledger and varying one controlled trait: the neodymium site pair tests angular structure, the β-manganese hands test parity, and the isotope pairs test the mass anchor.
3. **Predict a response in units.** A link cutoff predicts local concentration under a specified filling and partition. Keep the measured current response, localisation length, optical channel and finite-size trend separate; use a filled-band insulator and a matched metal, and record temperature, structure and probe.
4. **Complete the geometry-to-element map.** An element-specific knot needs a construction from measurable configurations, or a physically fixed closure on periodic paths. Declare equivalence under cell choice and origin, and test opposite hands. Compute polynomial and mirror controls before any atomic-volume assignment; the torus-knot phase identifications in Dirac Time give a family of mathematical specimens for testing this map.
5. **Freeze genuinely new heavy tests.** Hydrogen through astatine has full energy coverage in the archive. Beyond it, francium's uncertainty spans the wall and a lone measured first ionisation energy cannot fix a two-energy opening. Element 119 is a prospective test with its call written down.
6. **Keep the losses.** The heavy transfer failure, the provisional polonium miss, the failed twin equality and the scalar volume counterexample belong in the theory record. They say what information a better version has to carry.

**Synthesis.** The strongest physical bridge in this edition is that a signed same-charge reflection, multiplied by an externally anchored energy scale, predicts an increment of an independently measurable isobar mass difference. The strongest geometric bridge is the exact unequal-face closure, with a stated domain and falsifiable perturbations of the right-angle assumption. The weakest bridge is the step from an isolated atom's scalar ladder to its three-dimensional occupied environment, and the programme can now say exactly which observations it lacks there. What distinguishes the work is the ability to build two systems that agree on one book and ask which further record separates them. That question stays meaningful across my papers even when a proposed identification fails.
