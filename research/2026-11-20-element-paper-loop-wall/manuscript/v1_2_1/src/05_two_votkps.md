## What the two VOTKPs ask of one record

My starting question was whether a polynomial could tell me the volume around a knot. The later name, Vector of Traits Kept under Peeling, changed the question productively. What operation was performed, which traits survived it, and what would a second observer need in order to recover a trait the first one lost? The element work supplies a particularly clean instance. Electron removal leaves the nuclear charge fixed, changes the electronic state, and changes the energy of the next removal. The knot work supplies an instance in which different objects agree under several powerful tests. A common reading is possible because the operations, the retained quantities and the failures of recovery can all be named. There is still no map assigning a particular knot complement to a given element, or identifying its hyperbolic volume with an atomic volume. (Vector v2.9, §§1–3, 10–17; Volume, §§1, 6, 12–13.)

Both meanings of VOTKP stay in play, because I believe the two together are the key to mapping each element: the Volume of the Knot Potential asks what geometry an object carries, and the Vector of Traits Kept under Peeling asks what survives each removal.

### The intrinsic peel has direction and forgets an absolute scale

**DERIVED.** Put positive thresholds on the ordered edges of the electronic ladder. For a connected run, the coordinate $r_k = \log(I_{k+1}/I_k)$ satisfies $\sum r_k = \log(I_{\text{last}}/I_{\text{first}})$. The loss of absolute scale is exact: multiplying the whole record by a common positive constant leaves every edge unchanged. Conversely, fixing one threshold and the ordered ratios recovers every threshold connected to it. Reversal sends the edge $\Gamma_k = \tanh(r_k/2)$ to its negative. The endpoint keeps the signed sum while the interior carries the order and concentration of the steps. Logarithmic steps $a, b$ and $b, a$ give the same endpoint, and $a, -a$ and $0, 0$ give distinct paths with the same endpoint. For a vector of uniformly forward steps, variation equals net change; a cancellation reading needs a path with both signs. (Vector v2.9, §§8, 19–20.)

**DERIVED under the stated hypotheses.** For a complete ladder, the sum of the remaining thresholds is the energy needed to strip the remaining electrons. Dividing each energy by Planck's constant gives an absolute frequency scale and keeps neighbouring ratios. A response curve needs weights on its transitions: an ionisation threshold supplies no oscillator strength, pumped population, collision rate or linewidth. Two channels with equal total weight placed at different frequencies have different time kernels. That is the operational reason the book of gaps alone cannot predict delay, memory or colour. My clock reading of the ball is a hypothesis whose calibration has to say which of these separately accessible clocks a given port records. (Vector v2.9, §§19–21.)

**Archive.** The October tables contain 5,847 electronic thresholds through $Z = 110$, with 103 complete ladders. There are 5,714 neighbouring pairs, of which 156 carry two experimental flags. I report 56,191 complete windows of one to eleven steps passing endpoint composition, with maximum absolute discrepancy $5.55\times10^{-16}$. That is a consistency check of the archive and the arithmetic. An experimental determination, a theoretical estimate and an extrapolation need separate provenance flags even when each is an exact decimal in a Lean file. The pinned element branch is a separate census with its own anion entries and coverage. (Vector v2.9, §§19–21 and Appendix G.)

### Projectors give a second book

Let $P$ be an orthogonal projection on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, let $H = H^\dagger$, and put $P^\perp = I-P$, $B = PHP^\perp$ and $D = P^\perp H P^\perp$ on the complement. In this split the off-diagonal blocks of $[H,P]$ are $-B$ and $B^\dagger$. Thus
$$\tfrac12\big\|[H,P]\big\|_{HS}^2 = \|B\|_{HS}^2 = \operatorname{Tr} K(0),\qquad K(t) = B\,e^{-itD}B^\dagger$$
in units with $\hbar = 1$. **DERIVED.** Two eliminated channels with the same $\|B\|_{HS}$ can have entirely different $D$ and therefore different kernels. The off-diagonal information measures how the kept and the eliminated parts communicate — it is the connection energy across the cut. This calculation uses an explicitly Hermitian realisation; my general intrinsic metric and the physical clock remain separate objects. (Vector v2.9, §20; v3.3, §22.)

For a correlation matrix $0\le C\le I$ on $A\oplus B$, positivity of $C - C^2$ gives
$$0 \le C_{AB}C_{BA} \le C_{AA} - C_{AA}^2 \le I_A/4 .$$
If $C^2 = C$ and the split exhausts the space, the middle inequality is an equality. **DERIVED.** This is why the sum of weights on the other side of a cut is bounded, while the weakest link and the weight given to a particular neighbour depend on how that weight is distributed. The real and imaginary readings of the finite projector tensor keep separate information: in my convention the tensor is $Q = g - i\Omega/2$. A scalar gap spectrum also admits families of rotating eigenspaces with the same gaps, so inferring the full geometry from the spectrum alone fails a direct control. (v3.3, §§19, 22, 25.)

### Which atomic walls survive the controls?

**DERIVED.** Defining effective charges from two thresholds with principal shells $n_v$, $n_c$ gives
$$\frac{I_c}{I_v} = \Big(\frac{n_v}{n_c}\Big)^2\Big(\frac{Z_c}{Z_v}\Big)^2$$
in a common hydrogenic normalisation. The shell-only ratio for $n\to n-1$ is $[n/(n-1)]^2$, so the corresponding edge is $(2n-1)/[n^2+(n-1)^2]$. Every such bare edge at $n\ge2$ lies below $1/\sqrt2$: the bare shell floors $4, 9/4, 16/9, 25/16, 36/25, 49/36$ are all inside (Lean: `shell_floors_inside`). An inferred effective-charge ratio can carry the product across. The factorisation holds for charges defined this way; predictive content needs the charges specified independently. Across sodium to caesium the source finds only a 1.11% spread in the effective-charge ratio. Core configuration, occupancy and energy grades must accompany the crossings. (v3.3, §§8.7, 23.)

Among the 5,714 archived adjacent pairs, eight ordinary edges lie beyond $\Gamma^2 = 1/2$. Five are first edges of Li, Na, K, Rb and Cs. Three open the helium core of Be, B and C at higher charge. Only eight of 675 candidate closed-shell or subshell openings in my specified electron-count list cross. Francium's estimated second threshold straddles the decision once its uncertainty is propagated. **RECOGNISED.** The helium-like and lithium-like effective series in hartrees give a sharper local test than the bare-shell floor. The source uses
$$z_{\text{core}}(Z) \simeq \frac{Z^2/2 - 5Z/8 + 0.157666}{Z^2/8 - (290/729)\,Z + 0.250500}.$$
It gives $Z^* = 6.538$ for the upper wall, between carbon and nitrogen; replacing the helium-like numerator by the bare $Z^2/2$ moves it to about 9.47 (removing only the $5Z/8$ term gives 9.55). The comparison singles out the $5Z/8$ term — the Coulomb repulsion of the two $1s$ electrons — as what places the crossing between carbon's helium-core opening and nitrogen's. Distinct readouts of screening in the later element work keep their own definitions.

Rubidium and caesium already appear in the dated November paper. A later frozen call on rubidium and strontium tests the pipeline against concealed rows; it cannot date the first statement of the rubidium–strontium bracket, because Figure 35 of that version already places the non-helium core-line crossings after their alkali metals. (v3.3, §23.2, Figure 35.)

A loop of two specified perpendicular steps has a turning, a critical and a running regime. At equal faces the critical condition is $\Gamma^2 = 1/2$. The light signal depends on the prepared direction, the number of passes and the centre of turn; a scalar wall classification does not fix the signal. My proposed identification of $2\Gamma^2$ with an actual pressure from light inside an atom is an open physical question (§29). Section 26 studies a specific gain-and-loss chain with two band-end readings. Section 27 reports a first-ionisation threshold of 6.82 eV calibrated on $Z = 1$–13, with area 0.946 under the curve on $Z = 14$–118, for the presence of a visible ground-state line. That is an isolated-atom spectral property; a prepared metal's reflected colour needs its optical response, geometry and illumination. (v3.3, §§25–29.)

### The knot side gives the scalar a stringent test

Three polynomial readings are already distinct. Alexander and Jones are one-variable records; HOMFLY can separate some knots that agree in both. My census reports 19 such pairs at thirteen crossings and none through twelve. In the differently conditioned class of 2,525 thin, same-signature pairs blind to HOMFLY through thirteen crossings, the Khovanov and knot Floer tests produced no separation. A particularly instructive comparison pairs $13a_{3542}$ with the mirror of $13a_{4018}$: the sampled polynomial and homology records agree under my conventions, the signature function separates them on one window, and the longitude angle there is a numerical second reading. Each assertion has a specified population and invariant. Exchanging a knot for its mirror changes the question, so the identity of the input diagram belongs in the record. (Vector v2.9, §§11–13.)

The Volume paper begins with an ideal-tetrahedron shape $s$, the regular shape $w = e^{i\pi/3}$, and the coordinate
$$G = \frac{s-w}{s-\bar w}.$$
Its origin is the regular positive tetrahedron; real shapes sit on $|G| = 1$. Complex conjugation gives $G(\bar s) = 1/\overline{G(s)}$ where defined — an exact disc identity, and a different inversion from an ionisation-order reversal. For the Bloch–Wigner volume near $w$,
$$D(s) = D(w) - \frac{3\sqrt3}{4}|G|^2 + \frac{\sqrt3}{4}\,\Re\!\big(G^3\big) + \frac{3\sqrt3}{16}|G|^4 + O\big(|G|^5\big).$$
The quadratic term reads distance from the regular shape. Direction first enters at cubic order, consistent with order-three symmetry. Summing the truncated terms needs tetrahedral shapes and signed conventions from a specified triangulation; this construction has not extracted those shapes from a table of element thresholds. (Volume, §§2–4; Vector v3.3, §28.)

Two numerical records must survive. The 7 November paper reported a median relative error of 0.076% on 12,954 knots. The later §28 reconstruction reports 0.107% on 12,941, with worst discrepancy 11.376% at $11a_{247}$. I keep the earlier result as a dated, unreproduced run and use the later run's denominator with its own result. The source also reports an interval-computation remainder bound of $0.70|G|^5$ for $|G|\le0.9999$, over 14,806 cells and 606,204 squares. A remainder of one tetrahedron and a median error over knots are different quantities. Selection of the geometric root by proximity to the vacuum held on 31 of 31 cases searched in the early paper; a finite multistart search does not establish completion or global minimisation for all knots. (Volume, §§3–4; v3.3, §28.1–3.)

### A polynomial that actually keeps decay and direction

For each included boundary-parabolic geometry $\rho$ of an oriented drawing, my potential record is $V_0(\rho) = -\mathrm{CS}(\rho) + i\,\mathrm{Vol}(\rho)$. Its family can have continuously varying coordinates while this critical value stays constant. My polynomial has one root per included geometry:
$$P_K(x) = \prod_\rho \big(x - u_\rho\big),\qquad u_\rho = e^{\lambda V_0(\rho)} .$$
**DERIVED normalisation.** A real shift $V_0\mapsto V_0+\pi^2$ making exactly one positively oriented turn fixes $\lambda = 2i/\pi$; the other orientation reverses the sign. With the positive convention,
$$|u_\rho| = e^{-2\,\mathrm{Vol}(\rho)/\pi},\qquad \arg u_\rho = -2\,\mathrm{CS}(\rho)/\pi \pmod{2\pi}.$$
The modulus carries decay (volume) and the phase carries direction (Chern–Simons): that is why I call it the decay–direction polynomial. The Chern–Simons computation on 454 runs in §5 checks a congruence modulo $\pi^2/2$. A shift by that smaller period changes the sign of $u_\rho$, so a matched lift modulo $\pi^2$, or a sign-independent observable such as $u_\rho^2$, is needed before using such a computation to validate an individual phase. Squaring also discards phase information. (Volume, §§5–6.)

For the two non-abelian figure-eight geometries, with signed volumes $\pm2.02988\ldots$ and Chern–Simons zero,
$$P_{4_1}(x) = x^2 - 2\cosh\!\big(2\cdot2.02988\ldots/\pi\big)\,x + 1 \simeq x^2 - 3.91567\,x + 1 .$$
This uses the stated two-root family. For a family containing the complex-conjugate holonomy with the opposite signed volume and the same Chern–Simons class, the partner of $u$ is $1/\bar u$; that conditional statement needs a conjugation-closed set and compatible lifts. The database's named exterior and the diagram exterior have opposite handedness in 42 of the 79 signed comparisons. The geometric-top-root check succeeded in 79 of 79 knots at its tolerances, and a three-drawing spectrum check in 13 of 13. Neither count supplies the complete spectrum for every knot. (Volume, §6.)

The torus phase convention gives directions $-k^2/(pq) \pmod 1$. Every phase of that form is a $pq$-th root of unity. **DERIVED qualification.** The product over distinct such phases divides $x^{pq}-1$ over $\mathbb C$. The geometry-indexed polynomial can repeat a phase, and divisibility of that polynomial needs a no-collisions proof because $x^{pq}-1$ has simple roots in characteristic zero. In the source's search, $T(3,4)$ and $T(3,5)$ gave one direction each, with three and four expected components. The hyperbolic figure-eight shape polynomial $s^2 - s + 1$ defines $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$; its arithmetic field label belongs to that algebraic object. (Volume, §§6, 13.)

**Where the polynomial enters the element results, and where it does not yet.** The polynomial's own tests on knots — the geometric top root on 79 of 79 knots and the three-drawing spectrum on 13 of 13 — are in Volume v1 §6. The decay–direction polynomial is meant as a knot invariant; so far the evidence is that three drawings give the same spectrum on 13 of 13 knots, which is not yet a proof. It enters this paper through the knot-side tests of what a scalar keeps and loses, and through its modulus–phase split, which I read as an analogue of the split the peel makes between $\Gamma^2$ (what the wall reads) and the sign of $\Gamma$ (direction). It does not yet enter any elemental classification: no element is assigned a knot. The nearest element-side knot facts are these. Cyclooctaselenium's eight bonds form an unknot, so every finite collection of such rings admits disjoint discs; my representation product still needs a representation set before it can read the selenium control. The screw paths of β-manganese, trigonal selenium and tellurium carry a signed volume $\chi$ that does *not* change under path reversal and flips under a mirror — the opposite law to $\Gamma$ (see *Direction: the even half and the odd half*). **OPEN.**

### Additional crossings between the books

**RECOGNISED in the knot census.** A Jones polynomial evaluated along the unit circle supplies a climb, measured by its Mahler measure, and a turn, measured by normalised signed area. On 2,970 knots through twelve crossings the rank correlation is 0.8541 between climb and complement volume and $-0.9335$ between signed turn and signature. The corresponding correlations with Chern–Simons are $-0.031$ and $+0.053$. Among 9,985 thirteen-crossing knots the climb–volume correlation is 0.8542. This is exactly where the new potential polynomial earns its place: a simpler invariant already remembers much about size and sense of turn while doing poorly on the particular phase the new polynomial encodes. (v3.3, §28.4.)

My original HOMFLY test is even more diagnostic. Sampling both variables on a unit torus gave correlation 0.036. Along the $\mathfrak{sl}_N$ slices $v = t^{-N/2}$, $z = t^{1/2} - t^{-1/2}$, the climb–volume correlations are 0.60 for $N = 0$ (Alexander), 0.86 for $N = 2$ (Jones), and 0.78–0.86 for $N = 3$ through 8, on 12,955 knots. Using the slices and the log determinant gave $R^2 = 0.838$ against 0.795 for Jones and log determinant. These are empirical correlations that depend on where in the polynomial the reading is taken. The $N$ indexing a HOMFLY specialisation and the colour indexing representations in a coloured Jones sequence are different indices. (Volume, §7.)

The separate $\theta$ computation found 78 zero rows in a census of 12,965 and 12,887 non-zero. Of 865 mutant groups, 682 were fully separated at the chosen common evaluation point, 38 partly separated and 145 unresolved. Agreement on sampled points leaves functional equality open. The first mirror evaluator missed a term on the trefoil and stopped at its calibration test; the repaired one gave zero for 35 of 35 mirror sums and non-zero for 35 of 35 same-hand controls across its tested two-strand torus examples. The mirror-sensitive quantity here is a knot invariant evaluated under a definite operation. An optical phase, a signed peel, a knot signature and the sign of a spatial chirality observable each need their own transformation rule. (v3.3, §28.5.)

**Optical comparisons in the Volume paper.** The complex optical coordinate there is $(1-N)/(1+N)$ for the chosen incident convention — the same Smith form, with the complex index in place of the clock ratio. Its visible average of squared modulus separates all 35 metals from all six non-metals in that optical dataset; I take this up as the colour line in *Colour and the half-power line*. The source reports that phase gaps predict thin-film colour differences among 39 pairs similar in bulk, with rank correlation 0.64. Absorption, film preparation, spectral averaging and substrate enter this coordinate; substituting an isolated-atom peel into the optical classification would quietly change the input and the detection condition. In the finite-film closure test a frozen reflection threshold ($|\Gamma| = 0.856$, 171 film cases) scored 87%, with an explicit thickness interval and bump tolerance; a later threshold with higher in-sample accuracy belongs in the exploratory ledger. (Volume, §9; Vector v2.9, §§6–7.)

The surfaceology passage checks cross-ratio identities and hidden zeros in specific amplitudes, then adopts the strategy of locating zeros before deforming a record. The electron passage points to the shared group $SL(2,\mathbb C)$ of spin representations and lifted hyperbolic holonomy. Neither supplies a functor from an atomic state to a knot representation. The molecular section gives the most direct stress test of the material reading: five of six privileged knots are torus knots in the list it uses, and the exception, the figure-eight, has a hyperbolic complement. I keep that exception even though the abstract of the Volume paper uses broader language. A physical molecular selection mechanism would have to explain the exception and separate synthesis route, template and topology. (Volume, §§8, 10–12.)
