## Where the wall can be tested

The elemental ladders leave a window in which every cutoff classifies the archived steps alike. The geometry fixes one exact number inside it. Four settings now sit inside that window and can ask what the geometry predicts that its neighbours do not: a resolved oxygen channel, a theory-only hydrogen-like pair, a specified optical return, and francium.

For consecutive positive removal energies, reversing their order changes the sign of $\Gamma$ and keeps $\Gamma^2$. On a forward equal-face step, $\Gamma^2 = \tfrac12$ gives the exact geometric wall $w = 3+2\sqrt2$; comparing a tabulated ratio with $w$ is fully defined, including the equality case. A finite collection of mixed historical inputs also leaves freedom in the law that combines them: a strictly increasing piecewise map can fix every sampled reflection power, preserve every historical paired decision and still move the diagonal cutoff within the gap. A new setting and an independently observed outcome have different experimental roles from the historical table.

### The scandium–tin hydrogen-like pair

**DERIVED, theory only.** The 1s binding ratio of hydrogen-like tin (Sn⁴⁹⁺) to hydrogen-like scandium (Sc²⁰⁺) is the only one of 5,995 hydrogen-like pairs that lands between the wall and the arithmetic-midpoint rival (12 pairs fall somewhere in the silver–silicon window). The Schrödinger ratio $(50/21)^2 = 5.66893$ is outside the window entirely; the Dirac point-nucleus ratio 5.83665 is inside; with finite-nucleus and QED terms it is 5.832602195. This pair sits inside the window only because of the electron's spinor (Dirac) structure. It is a theory-only target: no measured pair of these thresholds exists to score.
