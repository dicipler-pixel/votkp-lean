## Where the vector reaches nuclear decay

**HYPOTHESIS and its falsifier.** "The vector determines the direction, that may be decay." Three different directions can be tested. Does the next ionisation move two isobars closer together or farther apart in ground mass? Which is heavier after many removals? Does a specific initial state actually decay, and at what rate? Each question admits its own counterexample. A wrong signed mass increment would challenge the first mapping. Correct increments can accumulate on opposite sides of a different starting mass. An energetically permitted transition can be too slow to detect on a particular experimental timescale. The distinctions become measurable in three isotope pairs.

**DERIVED.** Let $H$ and $L$ be isobars with higher and lower atomic numbers. Compare ions of equal positive charge $q$ and state the nuclear and electronic levels. Write $I_j^X$ for successive ground-state removal energies in species $X$. Then
$$M_X(q)c^2 = M_X(0)c^2 - q\,m_ec^2 + \sum_{j=1}^{q}I_j^X .$$
Set $Q(q) = [M_H(q)-M_L(q)]c^2$. At the next removal define $a = I^H_{q+1}$, $b = I^L_{q+1}$ and $\rho = (a-b)/(a+b)$. Cancellation of the removed electron's rest mass gives
$$Q(q+1)-Q(q) = a-b = (a+b)\rho,\qquad Q(n) = Q(0)+\sum_{q=0}^{n-1}(a_q+b_q)\rho_q .$$
Here the directed reflection compares two species at one removal index, while the ordinary peel compares two removal indices of one species. The sign of the interspecies reading gives the sign of the next increment exactly. The sign of the accumulated mass gap needs the mass anchor and the positive weights; given a starting energy for each species, its within-species peel vector rebuilds those weights.

**DERIVED, decisive scale control.** Multiply all threshold energies in both ladders by $c_0>0$ while holding units and the measured neutral nuclear mass difference fixed. Every dimensionless reflection, telescope ratio and shell-wall classification survives. In holmium–dysprosium at 57+ the central gap becomes $2863.2-7381.793561\,c_0$ eV: negative at $c_0 = 1$, positive at $c_0 = 0.01$, and in that case positive at every charge. A common rescaling of both ladders keeps every signed reflection and can still erase the crossing between 56+ and 57+. This tests what information the dimensionless record contains. The physical holmium energy scale is supplied by experiment.

**MEASURED AND DERIVED, holmium–dysprosium.** Neutral holmium-163 undergoes electron capture. Fully stripped dysprosium-163 has been seen to decay back to hydrogen-like holmium by bound-state beta emission. For $Q = M_{\mathrm{Ho}}c^2-M_{\mathrm{Dy}}c^2$ the neutral mass anchor is 2863.2(6) eV; its extraction includes electronic theory. On the archived thresholds the 56+ → 57+ increment is $5611-12081 = -6470$ eV, with $\rho = -3235/8846$. The central ordering switches from $+1951.406439$ to $-4518.593561$ eV. An alternative reconstruction starts separately at the measured Penning-trap ion frequency ratios at 38+, 39+ and 40+; from the last start, the outer input boxes are $[1782.877417, 2054.244830]$ eV at 56+ and $[-4697.122583, -4405.755170]$ eV at 57+. All three starts give the crossing; they share a mass reference and later electronic inputs. Neither isotope-specific threshold systematics nor decay rates at the two crossing charges were measured by this comparison. (Schweiger et al. 2024, Table 2; bare-ion decay, Jung et al. 1992.)

This uses neither ionisation sum to carry a mass to the other charge; the archive is compared only after the experimental interval is made.

**MEASURED AND DERIVED, lead–thallium.** Neutral lead-205 captures an electron. A storage ring measured a $291^{+33}_{-27}$ day half-life for fully stripped thallium-205 decaying the other way. At $q = 79$ the adopted next thresholds are 24938.2 and 96783.2 eV, so the equal-charge $Q = M_{\mathrm{Pb}}c^2-M_{\mathrm{Tl}}c^2$ changes by $-71845$ eV between 79+ and 80+; its central value falls from $+37.545$ to $-34.300$ keV, and at 81+ it is $-33.401$ keV. The daughter populated mainly in the ring is 2.329(7) keV above the lead nuclear ground state, so the actual channel gets about $31.072\pm4.557$ keV in the archive box. The published electronic-component route gives 31.1(5) keV and shares the same neutral gap and excitation energy; its electronic contribution and the archived sum differ by 2.53 eV. Daughter production is the independent observation. Electron occupation decides whether the daughter can accept the bound electron, and the nuclear matrix element helps set the rate. (Leckenby et al. 2024.)

**MEASURED AND DERIVED, rhenium–osmium.** The bare rhenium-187 half-life was measured at $32.9\pm2.0$ years against a neutral half-life of roughly 42 billion years, an acceleration of about $1.277\times10^9$. The archived rhenium peel span is $83162.41/7.83352 = 10616.225$, so a proposed equality between the two misses by a factor of about 120,250. For energetics, write $B_X = \sum_jI_j^X$ and $Q_K = Q_{\text{neutral}}+B_{\mathrm{Re}}-B_{\mathrm{Os}}+I^{\mathrm{Os}}_{76}$; for a bare parent the last term is recovered when the beta electron binds to the osmium K shell. With a measured equal-charge 29+ mass anchor the branch finds $+72.761\pm1.185$ keV available to the ground-state K channel, against $-12.853\pm1.185$ keV for a free final electron. The daughter's 9.756(19) keV nuclear level stays accessible to the bare parent through K capture of the newly emitted electron, which the corresponding neutral beta decay cannot populate. The same electronic correction applied to rhenium-185 leaves the bound channel below $-940$ keV throughout its input range. For a separate $2s$ excited-state comparison using an osmium isotope proxy, the interval spans zero and its direction stays unassigned. A physical rate model needs the transition matrix element, the electron wavefunction and phase space. The nuclear anchor and final state can change a prediction even when the element-level vectors coincide. (Bosch et al. 1996; PENTATRAP 2021.)

**Reading.** The mathematically strongest link with "that may be decay" is constructive. The vector with its first threshold rebuilds each electronic energy. Comparing two rebuilt ladders reproduces the charge-dependent part of a mass difference. The ground-mass difference then locates a possible change in energetic direction. A spatial hand, an order sense, a dielectric response, an ordering of nuclear masses and an observed event rate need different measurements, and keeping them apart lets an experiment contradict one proposed correspondence while leaving the independently tested ones intact.

## From a direction to a calibrated prediction

The charge-ladder experiment sharpens the connection to decay. Suppose each energy or other positive clock is a vertex of a finite graph, and a measured edge supplies $z_{uv}>0$ with an orientation from $u$ to $v$. I ask whether there are positive vertex clocks $x_v$ with $z_{uv} = x_v/x_u$ on every measured edge. This is the scalar reconstruction problem for the intrinsic clock readings; no spacing in laboratory metres or seconds has entered.

**DERIVED — reconstruction.** Set $b_{uv} = \ln z_{uv}$, with $b_{vu} = -b_{uv}$. Vertex clocks exist exactly when the signed $b$'s sum to zero on every cycle. Necessity follows by telescoping. For sufficiency, choose one root in each connected component, give it an arbitrary log-clock and propagate by summing $b$ along a spanning tree; any extra edge closes one fundamental cycle, and its zero sum makes the propagated values satisfy that edge. Subtracting any two solutions, the log-clock difference is constant on every component: exactly one free positive multiplier per component, including an isolated vertex. An independently calibrated edge joining two components fixes their relative multiplier, and a single absolute anchor then fixes both. A non-trivial measured cycle residual falsifies the scalar exact-difference premise for that set of edges. A concrete false control: three successive oriented ratios 2, 3, 1/5 have product 6/5, which forbids scalar clock potentials although every individual reflection is well defined. Statistical use needs correlations propagated, especially when two edges share a threshold. Non-commuting transport around a loop is an additional observation and does not satisfy this scalar hypothesis in general — which is exactly where my loop wall lives. A peel ladder is a coboundary; a loop with a non-trivial commutator is not.

**DERIVED — what the mass anchor buys.** For the holmium–dysprosium pair take $U_q = \sum_{k=1}^qI_{\mathrm{Ho},k}$ and $V_q = \sum_{k=1}^qI_{\mathrm{Dy},k}$, and let $Q_q = Q_0+U_q-V_q$ with $Q_0 = 2863.2\pm0.6$ eV. A common gain $c_H$ on the whole holmium ladder and $c_D$ on the whole dysprosium ladder preserves every intraspecies reflection; the two gains can change interspecies reflections, while varying one shared gain preserves those as well. I ask about independent gains: what calibration error can the predicted signs tolerate for every $q = 0,\dots,66$, all the charges available in both indexed ladders? These perturbations are diagnostic sensitivity parameters, with no distribution and no claim about instrument accuracy.

For interval half-width $\epsilon<1$ on each multiplicative gain, the largest adverse shift at fixed central inputs is $\epsilon(U_q+V_q)$. At $q>0$ the strict sign survives all such perturbations exactly when $\epsilon<|Q_q|/(U_q+V_q)$. To include the input rectangles, write $s = \operatorname{sgn}Q_q$, $d_U$ and $d_V$ for the summed published half-widths, and $d_0 = 0.6$ eV. With positive energies and independently admissible endpoints, the smallest possible signed margin in eV is
$$sQ_q-(d_0+d_U+d_V)-\epsilon\big[U_q+V_q+s(d_V-d_U)\big].$$
The adverse holmium corner is $(1-s\epsilon)(U_q-sd_U)$ and the adverse dysprosium corner $(1+s\epsilon)(V_q+sd_V)$; multiplying their difference by $s$ and using the worse neutral anchor proves it, and shows explicitly which perturbation reaches equality. The same gain per species applies at every charge. One failing admissible charge defeats the all-charge guarantee; a positive minimum at every charge guarantees it for the whole ladder.

**DERIVED — result.** Exact rational arithmetic on the October archive gives the binding charge $q = 56$ for both columns. At the full-rectangle threshold $\epsilon = 0.0066438313934\ldots$ a permitted corner reaches zero there, and a larger opposing gain can reverse the sign while keeping the shapes of both elemental ladders. For strictly smaller $\epsilon$ every common-charge sign survives all permitted corners: opposing species-wide scale changes of less than 0.664383% preserve every encoded central sign across the common charges. This is a sensitivity result about the encoded energy intervals and a specified anchor, with neither a confidence level nor a decay rate. An independent mass measurement outside the propagated range would challenge the calibrated construction or its inputs; a rate discrepancy alone would concern a different claim.

**Independent false control.** A second implementation evaluates the input and scale corners directly in rational arithmetic. At $\epsilon = 0.006645$, choosing $Q_0 = 2862.6$ eV, $U_{56} = 95557.2819$ eV, $V_{56} = 97139.636067$ eV and gains $1-\epsilon$, $1+\epsilon$ gives $Q_{56} = -0.225186890715$ eV. In this enumeration it is the only adverse corner and charge to flip at that gain; the 55+ rectangle keeps a positive sign, and charges 1+ to 12+ keep their signs throughout $0\le\epsilon<1$.

## What would make the vector a volume?

**DERIVED — a failure of identification.** Even the three lengths of three incident unit directions do not determine a volume. Take $v_1 = e_1$, $v_2 = e_2$ and $v_3 = \cos\alpha\,e_1+\sin\alpha\,e_3$ in a positive-definite comparison space. The three lengths stay one, while their signed volume is $\sin\alpha$, so its magnitude runs from zero to one and its sign can reverse. The Gram determinant is $\sin^2\alpha$ and misses the hand. A single chain of relative scalar steps carries still less incidence information. The example refutes a universal length-only reconstruction by exhibiting admissible inputs that cannot be told apart. It uses a finite comparison space; my intrinsic tensor can have a different domain, signature or degeneracy.

**HYPOTHESIS — a constructive way to ask my question.** Choose three independently addressable intrinsic perturbations at a junction. Specify the observed state or projector, establish that the three changes are linearly independent in the retained quotient, and state a volume functional and an orientation convention on that space. A positive Gram form on their span would define a magnitude $\sqrt{\det G}$; a signed three-form would also record an orientation. A degenerate form calls for its null directions to be identified, and an indefinite form needs its own convention. Then vary the geometry while keeping the scalar peel ladder fixed. Neodymium's inequivalent sites give a target with shared radial information; the screw hand in β-manganese gives a target with opposite spatial parity. The hypothesis gains content if an independently specified intrinsic reading predicts the measured difference on withheld sites or hands after the scalar inputs are matched. Failure places the missing information in the chosen projector, directions, volume functional or observation rule, and telling which needs all four recorded.

**HYPOTHESIS — light as the leader.** The full Dirac Time pump–remove–probe construction suggests a fair intervention. Prepare matched initial populations; drive two arms with the same deposited energy, spectrum, temperature and final scalar thresholds, varying only their order or relative phase. Remove the pump and compare a prespecified polarisation-resolved or time-resolved readout. Define the sign, timing window, repeatability and blinded null arm before acquisition. A coherent phase witness that reverses on the specified operation and survives matched heating would show a retained light-path record in that preparation. If a phase-scrambled control reproduces the signal within the preregistered sensitivity, that witness fails this way. A light-controlled volume claim also needs an independently measured change in the proposed volume observable; colour alone cannot establish it.
