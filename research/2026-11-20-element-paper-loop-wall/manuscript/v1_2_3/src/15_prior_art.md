## Prior art and where this work stands

I checked what others have done before claiming anything here as new.

- **Periodic tables for ions.** Lyu, Keitel and Harman (*Phys. Rev. Research* 7, L042071, 2025) build a periodic table for highly charged ions from the successive occupation of relativistic orbitals, with each cell an isoelectronic sequence. It is organised by orbital filling, not by a threshold on ratios of successive removal energies; it shares with my opening map the idea that an isoelectronic sequence is the natural unit. A 2026 follow-up in *Atoms* (14, 60) studies scaling within it.
- **Donor–acceptor maps.** Pineda-Urbina (*Atoms* 14, 48, 2026) builds a ΔSCF-DFT donor–acceptor descriptor map for 26 main-group atoms from hydrogen to krypton, using $\chi = (IP+EA)/2$, $\eta = (IP-EA)/2$, $\omega = \mu^2/2\eta$ and the electrodonating and electroaccepting powers. It is the closest prior art to my anion rung — the stone and the wanting — but it defines no ratio $(I-A)/(I+A)$ and no threshold; my $\Gamma_0 = \eta/\chi$ and the wall $\omega = \eta$ are not in it.
- **A coordinate for within-period trends.** Washburn, Simons and Allahyarov (arXiv:2605.00028, 2026) place first ionisation energy, electron affinity, Mulliken electronegativity and Pearson hardness on one noble-gas-centred axis with a golden-ratio cosh landscape function. It uses no reflection coefficient and no wall.
- **Conceptual density-functional theory.** Parr and Pearson (*J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 105, 7512, 1983) defined absolute hardness; Parr, von Szentpály and Liu (*J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 121, 1922, 1999) defined the electrophilicity index $\omega = \chi^2/2\eta$ and $\Delta N_{\max}$. I use their definitions as a borrowed tool with their hypotheses kept, in a new domain: the borrowed quantities read my peel step. I found no published use of $\omega = \eta$, $\eta/\chi = 1/\sqrt2$ or $IE/EA = 3+2\sqrt2$ as a boundary.
- **Large jumps in successive ionisation energies.** That a large jump in successive ionisation energies marks the opening of a closed core is standard teaching. What is new here is a single exact threshold, derived from geometry, that separates those openings from every other step across 85 elements, and the proof that each core sequence crosses it exactly once.
- **The delocalisation sum rule.** The site budget $\operatorname{Tr}(C_i-C_i^2) = \sum_{j\ne i}\|P_{ij}\|^2$ is the delocalisation sum rule of Bader's atoms-in-molecules analysis and the Wiberg–Mayer bond orders; the localisation tensor is from Resta and Sorella (1999), Souza, Wilkens and Martin (2000) and Resta (2006). Dividing the nearest shell's share by the whole budget, and reading the tensor as the integral of my intrinsic metric, are the steps I add; I found no earlier source that does them.
- **The sudden-change overlap.** The 1s overlap after a sudden change of Coulomb scale is textbook atomic physics, used for electron shake-off in beta decay. The new part is that its scale coordinate is my face apex.
- **The chiral quantum walk.** The three-site directional walk is Zimborás et al. (2013); here it controls a question about peeling.
- **The power–efficiency trade-off.** Shiraishi, Saito and Tasaki (2016) and Pietzonka and Seifert (2018) give the trade-off between power and efficiency for heat engines; a 2026 Pareto-front analysis bounds efficiency at maximum power by half of the maximum efficiency. Elizabeth Johnson put the trade-off as "fastest or maximum energy, can't be both"; the theorem behind it is Shiraishi–Saito–Tasaki 2016 and Pietzonka–Seifert 2018.
- **Earlier periodic tables.** Russell's octave chart (1926) and Bohr's plate (1922) are discussed in the Russell section.
- **Data.** Ionisation energies come from the NIST Atomic Spectra Database v5.12 (2024) through the pinned archive; electron affinities from Ning and Lu (2022) with the per-element updates listed in the sources.
