Each element is read as a chain of its own removal energies. Every step of the chain is a reflection on one disk, and the loop wall splits the disk in two. An element's address is where two of its steps fall: the anion rung, from the electron affinity to the first ionisation energy, and the first opening of its noble-gas core. Of 3,570 steps from hydrogen to astatine, eight lie outside the wall, and every one is the first opening of a core.

The address of an element is a pair: its anion rung and the first opening of its noble-gas core, each inside or outside the wall. Seven of the nine possible addresses occur.

Each strand follows one noble-gas core across the table: the ratio of the step that first opens that core, element by element. The gold line is the wall. Every strand starts outside the wall and crosses inward after one to four elements: helium after carbon, the other four cores after their first element.

All 3,570 removal steps (dots) and 65 anion rungs (rings) on the reflection disk: distance from the centre is Γ, angle is atomic number. The gold circle is the wall Γ² = ½. Only eight steps lie outside it.

The anion rung read as chemistry. Hardness η = (IE − EA)/2 is the stone; electronegativity χ = (IE + EA)/2 is the pull. The rung's reflection is Γ₀ = η/χ, and electrophilicity ω = χ²/2η. The wall is the line χ = √2·η, where ω = η and the ideal uptake ΔN = χ/η = 1/Γ₀ is exactly √2 electrons. Above the line the stone wins (53 of 65); below it the pull wins (12).

The same wall in the visible: the mean returned power |Γ|² of each element's surface across 400–700 nm (optical constants from the vacuum-disk paper, refractiveindex.info). All 35 metals sit above |Γ|² = ½ and none of the 6 non-metals do.

Walter Russell's octaves run from one inert gas to the next: tones 1+, 2+, 3+, apex 4, then 3−, 2−, 1−. Each element sits at the height of its anion-rung Γ₀²; the gold line is the wall. The turn inward falls between his 3− and 2− tones, one tone after his apex.
