# Do shorter ladders give an experimental comparison?

The nine-energy all-E panels in PT-REFERENCE-TRANSFER contained no rows. This second protocol was written after that result and before calculating shorter-prefix scores. It examines existing archive data and does not register a new atomic measurement.

The primary rung count is two. Two positive log steps are the shortest ladder with nontrivial projective shape. Use q=0,1,2; require all three original archive grades to be E for both query and candidate. Distances, energy convention, long-column labels, tie handling and exclusions follow PROTOCOL.md. Keep the original atomic-number domains F–At and Rn–Og, dropping the detached rows. In the historical panel, bar the query's entire period. In the heavy panel, only F–At can supply a reference. Include unavailable groups in the denominator and count them as misses, show their names and the achievable ceiling. Also list abstentions/ties. Give counts and the paired sign statistic even when the set is small. If a panel has no queries or no eligible candidates, return unscorable.

For K=2 through K=8, tabulate eligibility and both scores in every panel under the same E-only rule. This sweep is descriptive; selecting the best result after seeing it cannot turn it into a successful prediction. To separate rung information from cohort attrition, recalculate the two-rung results on each longer-rung cohort using exactly that cohort's candidate pool. Give the same comparison on the unchanged subset with an available true group in a permitted period. Do not substitute missing energies, uncertainty estimates or theoretical entries. In the primary historical panel only, compare simplex-only wins and log-only wins, with a two-sided sign threshold p<0.05 for claiming an advantage and more simplex hits required. Lack of an advantage is an admissible outcome.
