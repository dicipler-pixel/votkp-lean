# What to do with the modern tin measurement

Morgner et al. measured m(¹¹⁸Sn⁴⁹⁺)=117.874869069(56) u and g=1.910562058962(73)(42)(910). The quoted mass width alone is about 52.16 eV in rest energy. Even an exact independent bare ¹¹⁸Sn⁵⁰⁺ mass would leave this width larger than the 25.19-eV nearest margin of the current ⁴⁵Sc/¹²⁰Sn central result, before accounting for isotope differences and the scandium calibration. A new correlated same-isotope mass-difference determination could have a smaller differential uncertainty; method and covariance would matter. A true direct ground-threshold measurement satisfies B₁s=[M_bare + m_e − M_H-like]c². The ionic mass and the derived neutral mass in this paper cannot be combined as independent determinations of B₁s. [Morgner et al. (2023), eqs. 3 and 7 and Methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.06613)

**A companion g-factor check with explicit theory dependence.** In the point 1s Dirac problem g_D=2−4B_D/(3mc²). The physical g has finite-size, recoil and radiative terms, so g_exp cannot simply replace g_D. The experiment's Extended Data Table 3 gives g_D=1.9080792053 and total g_theory=1.9105618210(2988). If its non-Dirac magnetic response corrections are adopted, the measured-minus-theory residual is equivalent to a binding shift (3mc²/4)(g_theory−g_exp)≈−0.0912 eV, with a theory-width equivalent of about 0.115 eV. These figures convert *response residuals* to an energy scale under the Dirac derivative. They neither measure the last binding nor give bounds on its absolute Lamb shift. The experiment provides a stringent independently measured magnetic quantity for this atom; both fundamental corrections and the isotope assignment must be explicit in any joint interpretation. [Morgner et al., Extended Data Table 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.06613)
