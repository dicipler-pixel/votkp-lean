# Scandium–tin Lyman-α comparison

No experimental Sn/Sc ratio with a reportable uncertainty was located for either fine-structure component. Laboratory observers resolved the scandium doublet. The published metrology assigns its component wavelengths from theory. The available tin ion-trap measurement supplies a different observable.

## Measurement trail

- **Scandium, both components.** Beiersdorfer, Bitter, von Goeler and Hill (1989), Sec. II, describe the observed hydrogenic scandium Ly-α₁ and Ly-α₂ peaks used as wavelength references. In the same section the authors trace the assigned wavelengths to the calculations of Johnson and Soff. Their measured wavelengths and uncertainty of about one part in 20,000 apply to heliumlike transitions. Their two resolved hydrogenlike peaks provide identification but no independently calibrated pair of absolute Sc line energies with uncertainties. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.40.150) · [Full text](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/6321058)
- **Scandium fine-structure separation.** The investigators also describe a measurement of the spacing between the two hydrogenic Sc components, citing Beiersdorfer’s 1988 Princeton thesis as ref. 65. A spacing constrains the difference between the photon energies and leaves their common energy offset undetermined. The later 2012 scandium data compilation says the hydrogenlike spectrum had never been observed. The explicit 1989 primary account supersedes that statement for line detection. [Investigators’ account](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark%3A/67531/metadc1104751/m2/1/high_res_d/6054603.pdf) · [2012 compilation, Appendix A.1](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201117758)
- **Tin, both components.** Morgner et al. (2023) measured the bound-electron g factor of hydrogenlike tin-118 by microwave spin flips and measured the ion mass. Their conversion to neutral mass uses electronic binding-energy calculations. Neither measurement yields a reported Ly-α photon centroid or uncertainty. Queries on tin charge exchange, projectile x rays, storage rings, EBIT and laser plasmas yielded no qualifying Sn⁴⁹⁺ line pair. [Primary paper and methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06453-2)
- **Lamb-shift and energy tables.** Yerokhin and Shabaev’s Table III gives theoretical transition results. Table IV explicitly compares theory calculations including Z=50. Table V, the experimental comparison, has no row for Z=21 or 50. Nuclear radii measured elsewhere enter the calculations without turning their output into direct spectroscopy. The omission in Table V corroborates the historical search through 2015 and does not establish universal absence. [Full paper, pp. 8–9 and 52–53](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.01885)

## Reproduction and decision

Searches conducted 2026-11-21 Chicago time combined the names and identifiers Sc XXI, hydrogenic scandium, Sn49+, Sn L and hydrogenlike tin with Lyman alpha, 1s Lamb shift, wavelength, 2p, absolute measurement, projectile X rays, beam foil, laser plasma and storage ring. Both species and both specified components were fixed before the search. For a line to qualify, the record must identify that component and report its measured rest-frame energy or wavelength with an uncertainty. A relative measurement against an independently calibrated standard can qualify. Assigning the target its own predicted reference wavelength cannot.

Each ion lacks a located, independently measured line value meeting these criteria for either component. The literature therefore supplies no experimental ratio, propagated uncertainty, or wall discrimination in this test. This is a bounded negative search result.
