## Operational test of the geometry
Use a prepared, calibrated two-coordinate determinant-one linear transfer device. Define h=ln(z)/2. A=diag(exp(h_a),exp(−h_a)); B has diagonal cosh(h_b) and off-diagonal sinh(h_b). Both matrices and their inverses must be realizable in the same physical coordinates. Drive one loop W=ABA⁻¹B⁻¹. Direct multiplication yields tr(W)=2−4 sinh²(h_a)sinh²(h_b). Thus tr(W)<−2 iff S>1 for these forward faces. An ABCD or equivalent linear instrument can distinguish bounded elliptic response from a hyperbolic multiplier. This realization is already governed by transfer-matrix algebra. It tests the encoded loop, not a spontaneous Be/Rb atomic effect. A new empirical claim about atoms requires a mechanism and an independently specified joint observable.

Protocol fixed for a future run: calibrate each forward stage and inverse independently of the round-trip outputs. Blind the identities of the two settings and controls. Obtain both columns of the real round-trip map from independent prepared basis inputs, with reversed inputs to control offsets. Estimate the map, determinant, covariance and trace by repeated complete, interleaved blocks. Include (4,4) interior, (7,7) exterior and (9,4) marginal controls. The ideal traces are +0.734375, −4.612245 and −2. Report raw readouts; do not impose determinant 1 or reconstruct the loop from stage settings for the outcome. For a nonunit determinant D>0 the hyperbolicity discriminator is T²−4D and the sign of T must also be retained. Score only with jointly propagated intervals for T,D and independently measured stage tolerances that keep the target input on its designated side of BOTH rules and agree with the intended loop. For primary target the trace distance from −2 is 0.006465: require a pre-run uncertainty budget tighter than that including systematic effects. If either class boundary falls within the error set, score unresolved. If matched controls fail, record failed implementation. If resolved and the return is hyperbolic on the negative-trace branch, score the geometry against the shifted rival; if elliptic, the converse. In this engineered setting a discrepancy also triggers a check of stage implementation, alignment and linearity.

Parity control (from Russell, Corrected): invert both inputs (a,b)→(1/a,1/b); both signed reflections flip and S is unchanged. tr(W) is unchanged in this model. Retain individual signed stage readouts and the cycle classification separately. Report evidence for each independently. A circle intersection check and a direct four-matrix calculation were actually run locally. No laboratory outputs were obtained, and this private record is not a public time-stamped preregistration.

## Francium: an atomic target with a future scoring rule
Pin IE1=4.0727411(11) eV. In the interval 23.7376747 < IE2 < 23.7734875 eV the two equal-face cutoffs disagree (strict, central IE1). To make a resolved call require the full box for IE2/IE1 strictly between w and c. NIST lists [22.4] ± 1.9 eV, semiempirical, covering both boundaries. Even a perfect IE2 would determine the ratio; selecting a physical wall still needs a separately observed transition. https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/ASD/ie.pl?spectra=francium&units=1&unc_out=1

A different, simpler prospective hypothesis can already be scored by spectroscopy alone: the Fr ordinary entry lies below the loop wall, ending the Li–Cs outside streak. The last two tabulated alkalis give z_Rb=6.533086691787 and z_Cs=5.947100834227. A one-period linear continuation gives z_Fr=5.361114976667, IE2 ≈21.8344 eV. This extrapolation has no calibrated error model and the Fr semiempirical input was already known. The sign forecast alone is to be scored by a future independent, two-sided measurement. An interval entirely above w falsifies it; entirely below supports it; a straddling interval is unresolved. This tests continuation of the observed family trend. It does not by itself separate w from c unless the ratio is in their narrow disagreement interval, nor would the ratio alone establish material critical behaviour. Russell's repeating octave motivates the family comparison; the quantitative prediction belongs to this test.
