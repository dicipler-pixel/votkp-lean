Light reads the even rungs. The tensor sits on the odd rung between them.

Jeromie Beasley

S(k) = Σm fm Δmk ,   fm = 2 Δm |⟨0|D|m⟩|²
Q = Σm |⟨0|D|m⟩|² / Δm² = ½ S(−3)
α(ω) = S(−2) + ω² S(−4) + ω⁴ S(−6) + …

Every transition out of the ground state carries one weight: twice its gap times its squared dipole element. Multiply each weight by the gap to the power k and add them up. That is rung k. One distribution, nine rungs, nine different physical quantities.

The response of the electron to light of photon energy ω is built from rungs −2, −4 and −6 only. The intrinsic tensor Q is half of rung −3. Move the gold slider: the light's reading swings, changes sign and passes through zero at the tune-out. The blue rung does not move.

Conditions

  • A single ground state with every gap Δm open.
  • One field direction; the field enters as H(E) = H₀ − E·D.
  • The series for α(ω) holds below the first gap. Past it, use the exact sum Σ fm / (Δm² − ω²), which this card does.
  • Units with ħ = 1 and unit charge and mass, so that fm = 2Δm|D|².

How to cross

  1. List the gaps and dipole elements out of the ground state.
  2. Form the weights fm. Rung k is Σ fmΔmk.
  3. Read the tensor at k = −3 and halve it.
  4. Read the light's response from the even rungs below it.
  5. Divide any rung by the one beneath it to get a weighted mean gap; these fall toward the smallest gap.

What it says

  • A light probe can go silent while the tensor stays finite, because the tensor adds the same transitions with positive weights and cannot cancel.
  • Rung −2 is how far the cloud leans in a steady field. Rung −3 is the correction when the field changes in time: the inertia of the response.
  • For hydrogen the tensor is exactly 43/8.
