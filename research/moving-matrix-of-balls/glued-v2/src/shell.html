Light at the Hypersurface

Jeromie Beasley · v1 · 15 Nov 2026 · AI-assisted

One Poincaré ball in the peel (Smith) coordinate. The centre cell of the tree is bounded by six faces, each halfway along one peel step; the neighbouring cells carry their own faces further out. Light leaves the centre as geodesic rays: a broadcast in every direction, and a fan about each arm that spans the edge of its window. At each face a ray divides into a returned part and a crossing part by the step law, bends by the conserved a sin²θ, or is returned whole past the critical angle. Along each arm the layers are summed coherently. Every part runs on one clock, and every number below is recomputed on each frame. This page is an explorer of checked formulas, not a test of them.

Laws, live
His picture, shown as a hypothesis

Hypothesis (Jeromie Beasley): light drives through boundary layers; it is held between electron layers and is returned again and again at the hypersurface, or is absorbed and emitted further down; the pressure of light and the rotation inside and outside the layers matter; the electron sea is ground, follows the light and keeps a record of each return; balls join at levels and always keep a gap layer. He names this his Snell law at the hypersurface together with the exclusion principle. Drawn in violet and dashed: the gap between faces that stand apart, and the record of landings on the rim. Absorption with later emission is not modelled in this version.

What is not claimed
  • The ball is the peel coordinate of the tree. It is not a spacetime, and no spacetime metric is used. The Poincaré metric itself is not formalised in Lean; only (1 + Γ)/(1 − Γ) = z is.
  • Structural comparison only: the step and refraction formulas have the same algebraic form as the Fresnel and Snell laws of optics.
  • Reading a peel step as an admittance step (Y₁/Y₂ = z) and a = Y² are readings, not theorems. The button gives the opposite orientation.
  • Lean covers the algebra of the squared refraction law; the card B30 gives the path statement. The geodesic rays and the incidence relation are numerical checks made for this page.
  • Rays off the arms divide by flux, without phase. Coherent echoes are summed on the arms only, with one declared phase φ(τ) for every layer.
  • The faces move on a declared schedule. No law of motion, no retardation and no frequency shift from the motion of a face is modelled.
  • Below the wall the cells of the tree overlap, so the layer label belongs to the ray, not to the point.
  • The push 2R cos²θ is a reading. Momentum carried on by the crossing light is not modelled. Maxwell's result in Lean is the stress of a wave front; no energy-flux vector is formalised there.
  • Thomas turn: Lean at ratios 5 and 7 for equal steps; unequal steps are numerical checks. It is a turn of the frame; nothing is claimed about polarisation.
  • Lean was not run again for this page. A chip names the theorem in his repository.
